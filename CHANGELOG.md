- **Legacy long-term capital loans missing from the firm's loan mirrors (phase 4 follow-up).** Before: the legacy branch of `_maybe_offer_long_term_capital_loan` originated the loan with `bank.originate_loan` and never added it to `firm.bank_loan_principal`, `bank_loan_remaining` or `bank_loan_payment_per_tick`, while `_collect_bank_loan_repayments` subtracted each of its payments from `bank_loan_remaining` (clamped at 0), so the firm's debt-service estimates (the debt-adjusted wage ceiling in `plan_wage`, the housing rent target in `plan_pricing`, the distress mirrors) ignored a 520-tick loan and its payments ate into other loans' balances. The payment arm already mirrors it through `originate_v2`. Now: legacy origination adds the principal, the record's `remaining` and its `payment_per_tick`, as the other legacy bank loans do; when collection pays one off (`remaining` reaches 0), its `payment_per_tick` comes off `bank_loan_payment_per_tick`. Other legacy bank loans still never remove their installment from that mirror when they complete; that is left for follow-up. Evidence: new `test_legacy_long_term_capital_loan_is_in_firm_loan_mirrors` (before the fix the firm's `bank_loan_principal` was 0.00 after a 25,000 loan). Goldens: all three match without re-saving, and per-tick traces are identical: the default runs originate 60 (seed 42) and 211 (seed 7) long-term loans, all to generic Services firms, whose `plan_wage` returns before the debt-adjusted ceiling and whose service pricing reads only the service-infrastructure installment; no housing firm took one. Payment arm: unchanged.
- **Legacy treasury direct loans charged one year of interest (phase 4 follow-up to A4).** Before: when no bank channel lent, the legacy fallback in `_issue_firm_loan` booked `government_loan_remaining += amount * (1 + govt_rate)` and `loan_payment_per_tick += that / term`, so a 104-tick investment loan at 4% owed 4% in total and a 52-tick working-capital bridge at 2% owed 2%, whatever the term; the no-bank government-backed entrant seed in `_maybe_create_new_firms` did the same at 1% over 156 ticks. Now: both use `payment_loans.v2_payment`, the installment bank loans charge since A4, and book `installment * term` as the amount owed. Examples from the seed-42 run: 500 at 4% over 104 ticks, 5.0000 → 5.0044 a tick (520.00 → 520.46 owed); a 65.49 bridge at 2% over 52 ticks, 1.2847 → 1.2724 a tick (66.80 → 66.16 owed). So investment fallbacks owe slightly more and working-capital fallbacks slightly less. This path fires often in default legacy runs: 250 loans (124,509 principal) over the seed-42, 80-tick run and 1,794 (885,681) over the seed-7, 300-tick run, all investment or working-capital purposes; the no-bank seed never fires there, since those runs have a bank. Each run first differs from the pre-change run on the step of the first such loan (seed 42, tick 22; seed 7, tick 13). Evidence: new `test_legacy_direct_treasury_firm_loan_amortizes_over_its_term` and `test_legacy_no_bank_new_firm_treasury_seed_amortizes_over_its_term` (before the fix: 100.00 against 100.09 a tick, and 194.23 against 195.23). Payment arm: not changed. Its two treasury sites (the treasury branch of `_issue_firm_loan`, `amount * (1 + rate)`, and the entrant treasury seed at `requested * 1.01`) are direct treasury claims under the payment arm's own accepted contract (PSF10 in `docs/proposals/agent_round_02/PAYMENT_AUDIT_DISPOSITIONS.md`: a distinct actual-cash collector outside the registered bank book, whose installment also feeds `can_underwrite` through `_underwriting_due_index`), so they are left for a payment-arm follow-up; `income_first` goldens match. - **Affordability estimates used the one-year formula (phase 4 follow-up to A4).** Before: `FirmAgent._estimate_working_capital_payment` returned `amount * (1 + annual_rate) / term_ticks` (201.92 for 10,000 at 5% over 52 ticks), and the revenue gate in `_offer_service_infrastructure_loans` priced the upgrade at `principal * (1 + annual_rate) / term`, while the loans themselves have charged the amortized `payment_loans.v2_payment` installment since A4 (197.25 for the same terms; 525.67 against 525.00 for the gate test's 52,000 at 5% over 104 ticks). The working-capital estimate keeps its conservative rate (`working_capital_govt_rate + working_capital_spread`), because the funding channel is not known when it runs. Evidence: new `test_contract_services_infrastructure_gate_uses_the_loan_installment` (0.1 below the installment-based threshold was approved before the fix) and `test_contract_working_capital_estimate_is_the_amortized_installment` (201.92 against 197.25 before). - **B30, household constructor arguments were discarded.** Before: `HouseholdAgent.__post_init__` called `_initialize_personality_preferences`, which assigned about 40 fields from the seeded personality generator unconditionally (the preferences and tendencies, deposit behavior, healthcare thresholds, medical-career anchors, health decay, `consumption_budget_share`, `expected_wage`, `reservation_wage`, `price_expectation_alpha`, `job_search_cooldown`, `happiness`, `morale`, the decay rates and the minimum consumption levels, among others), so `HouseholdAgent(..., happiness=0.25)` produced a household with a sampled happiness (0.68 in the new test). Only `savings_rate_target` used a `None` sentinel. Now: those fields default to `None`, and the sampler assigns each one only when it is still `None`. Every draw still happens in the same order whether or not a field was supplied, so a default household is unchanged (all fields of households 1-399 compared equal to the pre-change code) and omitted fields of a customized household get the same values a default household gets. Derived fields (`deposit_buffer_weeks`, `deposit_fraction`, `savings_drawdown_rate`, `medical_school_weekly_interest_rate`, `health_decay_rate`) follow the final base values unless supplied themselves. The two conditional adjustments (critical below urgency threshold, reservation below expected wage) apply only to values the caller did not supply, and only then make their extra draws. The `consumption_budget_share` range check now runs after sampling, with the other validations. `category_weights` and `purchase_styles` keep their existing handling (supplied weights are still scaled by the sampled preferences). Callers checked: no constructor call in the repository passes a sampled field (the tools pass only `category_weights`; the test factories pass through whatever a test supplies, and no contract test's expectations changed). Evidence: new `test_contract_household_constructor_overrides_survive_personality_sampling` (failed before: happiness 0.68 instead of 0.25); all three golden compares match. Size: the diff is about 95 changed lines in `agents.py`, nearly all one-for-one (a default becomes `None`; `self.x = sample_range(...)` becomes `keep("x", sample_range(...))`), with about 12 net new lines. Payment arm: same household class on both paths.
- **B24, a bailout lever change refilled the cycle budget.** Before: `GovernmentAgent.set_lever` on `bailout_policy`, `bailout_target` or `bailout_budget` called `sync_bailout_cycle_budget`, which set `bailout_budget_remaining` back to the full `bailout_budget`, zeroed `bailout_cycle_disbursed`, `bailout_cycle_firms_assisted`, `bailout_cycle_sector_spend` and `bailout_cycle_assisted_firms`, and reset this tick's bailout telemetry. A manual change in the middle of a decision cycle (dashboard or tool) therefore granted a fresh budget on top of what the cycle had already paid out, and `begin_decision_cycle` then archived a cycle that looked unspent. Now: the method sets `bailout_cycle_authorized` to the new budget and `bailout_budget_remaining = max(0, new_budget - bailout_cycle_disbursed)`, and leaves the cycle counters and tick telemetry alone. `begin_decision_cycle` still starts each new cycle with the full budget. Callers: `set_lever` (dashboard, LLM apply before `begin_decision_cycle`), and `tools/llm/run_llm_government_test.py`, which zeroes the budget and calls it directly (remaining now 0 either way). Evidence: new `test_contract_mid_cycle_bailout_lever_change_keeps_cycle_spending` (failed before: remaining 10,000 after 4,000 was disbursed, expected 6,000); `test_contract_bailout_budget_resets_each_decision_cycle` still passes; all three golden compares match (no default run changes a bailout lever mid-run). Payment arm: same government method on both paths.
- **B17, infrastructure multiplier grew without bound.** Before: `GovernmentAgent.invest_in_infrastructure` did `infrastructure_productivity_multiplier += effective_gain` every funded tick (0.01 a tick at `high` and full efficiency), with no cap and no decay, so 200 ticks of `high` spending tripled every worker's productivity (2.9999 in the new test) and the gain never faded after the lever went back to `none`. Now: each call first decays the excess over 1.0 by 5% (`1 + (m - 1) * 0.95`, floored at 1.0), then adds the gain capped at 1.15, the cap `invest_in_technology` uses. Sustained spending settles at about 1 + 20 × the per-tick gain, capped: 1.05 at `low`, 1.10 at `medium`, 1.15 at `high`; after funding stops the excess halves in about 14 ticks. Design note: the 5% rate is the social-programs rate, applied to the excess over 1.0 as `invest_in_social_programs`' docstring describes ("half-life ~14 ticks"); that method's code instead multiplies the whole multiplier by 0.95 and floors it at 1.0, which keeps it at 1 + one tick's gain (1.05 in `test_contract_social_multiplier_is_policy_funded_and_decays`). Applying that form here would hold infrastructure at 1.0025 to 1.01. Social programs are not changed here. Evidence: new `test_contract_infrastructure_multiplier_is_capped_and_decays` (failed before with 3.00 against the 1.15 cap); all three golden compares match, because the default infrastructure budget is 0 and the decay leaves 1.0 unchanged. Payment arm: same method on both paths.
- **B22, firm `decision_diagnostics` never cleared.** Before: `FirmAgent.decision_diagnostics` was only ever written, so keys set on one path stayed after the firm took another: one-shot flags such as `services_headcount_last_resort`, `survival_turnaround_hiring` and `wage_raise_reason = "reservation_blocked_vacancies"` stayed set for the rest of the run, and `hiring_block_*` payload keys from an old block sat beside a newer reason. The only clearing was `_plan_services_capacity_labor` deleting `production_governor_*`. Now: `_phase_firm_planning` calls the new `FirmAgent.clear_planner_diagnostics()` for each firm before it plans, which deletes the keys that only the planners write (`plan_production_and_labor`, `plan_capital_investment`, `plan_pricing`, `plan_wage` and their helpers), listed as `_PLANNER_DIAGNOSTIC_KEYS` plus the prefix families `_PLANNER_DIAGNOSTIC_PREFIXES` in `agents.py`. Keys written by labor apply, settlement, service upgrades, the healthcare bonus and the economy (price and rent stabilization, working capital, long-term capital, public works) are kept, as are `healthcare_current_profit_margin` and `healthcare_target_margin`, which settlement also writes. After a step the diagnostics describe that tick's plans. Readers checked: `tools/llm/run_llm_government_test.py` reads `hire_limit_effective`, `adaptive_hiring_allowed` and `wage_raise_reason` after each step with defaults for missing keys, so its counts now cover the current tick only instead of any earlier tick; no engine code, server frame or warehouse reads the dict; contract tests read keys right after calling a planner. Evidence: new `test_contract_planner_diagnostics_do_not_outlive_their_tick` (failed before: six of seven stale planner keys survived a step); all three golden compares match. Payment arm: same planning loop on both paths.
- **B25, `deficit_ratio` reported a surplus as a deficit.** Before: `get_economic_metrics` set `metrics["deficit_ratio"] = abs(gov.cash_balance) / max(gdp, 1.0)`, so a treasury holding 5,000 in the new test reported a ratio of 17.70, the same as one owing 5,000. Separately, `_adjust_government_policy` computed an unemployment rate, a placeholder inflation rate, its own `abs()` deficit ratio, a tax-revenue estimate and a full firm pass counting negative-cash firms, and passed them all to `GovernmentAgent.adjust_policies`, which reads only `num_unemployed` (it sizes `transfer_budget`). Now: the metric is `max(0.0, -gov.cash_balance) / max(gdp, 1.0)`, zero in surplus. `_adjust_government_policy` counts the unemployed and calls `adjust_policies(num_unemployed=...)`, whose signature drops the six unused parameters (it had no other caller). Readers of the metric: `tools/llm/llm_government.py` (`budget_state["deficit_ratio"]` in the LLM government's observation) and `tools/checks/run_behavior_tests.py::test_deficit_ratio` (a manual spend-versus-tax check, which now compares zeros when both scenarios stay in surplus); no frontend, server frame, warehouse or forecasting reader. Evidence: new `test_contract_deficit_ratio_is_zero_in_surplus` (failed before with 17.70 for a surplus); all three golden compares match (the metric is not in the goldens and no engine code reads it). Payment arm: same metric code on both paths.

Goldens: both legacy goldens differ only in `households_sample[*]/ledger/bank` (2 leaves each); `income_first` matches; all three re-saved. Payment arm: unchanged.

- **Planning-order comment (phase 2 follow-up).** Before: the phase 2 loop fusion moved `HouseholdAgent.maybe_request_consumption_loan` from after consumption planning into the fused household loop in `_phase_household_planning`, and nothing in the code said that the later planning steps must leave the loan request's inputs alone. - **D50, loan ledger lookups scanned the whole ledger.** Before: `BankAgent.active_loans` is a flat list; `BankAgent._firm_existing_debt` (read by `_can_firm_borrow`, `_max_firm_borrowable` and the housing LTV gates) scanned every loan for each query, and the legacy branch of `_handle_firm_exits` scanned every loan for each exiting firm to write off its loans. Now: `BankAgent.loans_for(borrower_type, borrower_id)` returns that borrower's records from an index `{(borrower_type, borrower_id): [loan, ...]}` that keeps `active_loans` order, and both sites use it. `originate_loan` and `payment_loans.originate_v2` add their new record to the index; `payment_loans.rollback_medical_loan`, which pops a record in place, drops it; any other change of the list's identity or length (`cleanup_settled_loans`, the payment-path exit filter) is detected by a (list, length) stamp and rebuilds it on the next lookup. Settled records stay in the index until the list is cleaned, as they stayed in the scan, so sums add the same values in the same order. Evidence: all three golden compares match; new `test_contract_bank_loan_index_matches_ledger_order` checks lookups and `_firm_existing_debt` against a list scan after appends, a write-off, cleanup, an in-place pop and a reassignment. Payment arm: bookkeeping lines only in `payment_loans.py`; no change in behavior.
- **D48, household cash summed before the firm-cap check.** Before: `_maybe_create_new_firms` summed every household's cash on every post-warmup tick, then checked the legacy `< 1000` gate, then returned early when `firms + queued_firms >= target_total_firms`, which is most ticks once the economy is full; on the payment path the sum was never read. Now: the firm-cap return comes first, and the sum is computed only on the legacy path, where the gate and the tier-2 and tier-3 seed sizes read it. Both returns have no side effects, so their order does not change results. Evidence: all three golden compares match; contract suite passes. Payment arm: no longer computes the unused sum; otherwise unchanged.
- **Wage-bill cache contract test (phase 2 follow-up).** Before: `FirmAgent._current_wage_bill` caches its roster sum and depends on explicit `_invalidate_wage_bill_cache` calls after in-place roster or wage edits, and no test checked the cached value against a fresh sum over a running economy. ### 2026-09-28: Agents/economy remediation, phase 4 (money conservation)

Behavior changes on purpose, one leak per commit, each confined to the leaking path and proven by a conservation test. Audit: [docs/reviews/2026-09-28-agents-economy-code-audit.md](docs/reviews/2026-09-28-agents-economy-code-audit.md), section A. Plan: [docs/reviews/2026-09-28-agents-economy-remediation-plan.md](docs/reviews/2026-09-28-agents-economy-remediation-plan.md), phase 4. Drift figures come from `backend/tools/checks/money_supply_drift.py` at defaults (1,500 households, seed 42, 120 ticks, legacy).

- **A3, medical-loan fallback with a bank present.** Before: when a bank existed but both its medical loan and the government-backed loan were declined, `Economy._issue_medical_loan` still fell through to `HouseholdAgent.take_medical_loan`, which adds the amount to the household's cash with no lender debit, so the visit was paid for with new money (+15.00 in the pinned test). Now: that fallback runs only when `self.bank is None`; with a bank that declines, the function returns False and `_process_healthcare_services` refunds the subsidy and drops the visit, as it already did for other declines. Aggregates: no change; all three golden compares still match, because the fallback never fired in those runs. Drift: net -38,702.56 / gross 681,124.05 before and after. Test: `test_a3_medical_loan_fallback_with_bank_conserves_money` now passes (xfail removed). Payment arm: not affected; `payment_loans.fund_medical_loan` has no fallback and returns 0.0 on a decline.
- **A2, long-term capital loan principal wiped.** Before: in `_phase_firm_planning`, `_maybe_offer_long_term_capital_loan` debited the bank and added the principal's recycle units to `firm.capital_investment_this_tick`, and `FirmAgent.plan_capital_investment` later in the same per-firm loop reset that field to 0.0 on the legacy path, so `_recycle_capital_investment` never paid the principal to households; the money disappeared (-25,000.00 in the pinned test; 207 loans and 1,965,218.75 over the seed-7, 300-tick golden run). Now: on the legacy path the loop clears last tick's value before the offer and adds the offer's units back after `plan_capital_investment`, so the recycle pays households the full principal. The offer still runs before production planning, as the tick lifecycle documents. `total_investment_this_tick` in the metrics now includes those units. Test: `test_a2_long_term_capital_loan_conserves_money` now passes (xfail removed). Drift: net -38,702.56 / gross 681,124.05 before, net +389,775.97 (+4.30%) / gross 396,772.36 after; every remaining nonzero step but one is positive (the largest, +16,000 to +29,400, match legacy new-firm seeding, audit A5); the exception is -3,498.20 at tick 35, unattributed. Payment arm: not affected; it resets the field at the start of the tick, not in `plan_capital_investment`, and `income_first` goldens match. Unemployment at a single snapshot tick moves several points (seed 7, tick 80: 4.93% to 15.07%) because the series swings tick to tick in both runs; its mean over ticks 41-80 is 10.14% before and 10.26% after. Later windows diverge further; see the single-seed noise measurement in the A4 entry. Total money on the seed-7 run ends 1.95 million higher, which is the principal that used to be wiped.

- **A1, subsidized purchase scaled down after the seller was paid.** Before: `_clear_goods_market` booked `qty * price` for the seller and `_phase_firm_settlement` credited that revenue and removed the units from inventory; later `_batch_apply_household_updates` settled the household's share with `settle_capped_subsidized_goods_purchase`, which scales the purchase down when the household cannot afford its share, so the household and the treasury paid `scale * total_cost` and the unpaid `(1 - scale) * total_cost` was new money in the seller's cash, while the matching units left the seller and never reached the household (+1,144.45 in the pinned test). Now: when the scale is below 1, the same loop takes the unpaid amount back from the seller's cash, `last_revenue`, `last_profit` and `net_profit`, reduces `last_units_sold` and this tick's `per_firm_sales` entry (read later by R&D, statistics and next tick's planning) by the unsold units, and returns those units to the seller's inventory (not for generic services, whose inventory is always zero). Profit tax and the wage-to-revenue check had already run on the full revenue and are left as they were. The quantity cap was not moved into `_clear_goods_market` because the cap depends on cash after this tick's wages, transfers and taxes and on the treasury's remaining subsidy budget, which are applied later, so capping at clearing would also cut purchases that settle in full today. `_batch_apply_household_updates` takes a new optional `per_firm_sales` argument; the seller is looked up by good name, which the engine makes unique per firm. Aggregates: no change; all three golden compares still match, because no default run has a sector subsidy. A 1,500-household, 80-tick legacy run with a 50% food subsidy had no scale-downs either, so the path fires only when planned purchases exceed cash plus the tick's income. ### 2026-09-24: Frontend redesign, design decisions

Design phase only. No frontend code changed yet. Full design: [docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-24-frontend-learning-redesign-design.md](docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-24-frontend-learning-redesign-design.md). Approved mockups: [docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-24-mockups/](docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-24-mockups/).

Decisions:

- **Audience.** The dashboard is for newcomers learning economics by watching, not an analyst's console. Friendly names ("people out of work", "typical weekly pay", "town hall cash"), one-sentence definitions, a picture beside every number. The dense metric grid survives behind a "Show me all the numbers" link.
- **Unit of work is an experiment**, not a run: a mode, a seed, a shared world config, and one to four arms ("towns"). Arms run as live side-by-side backend sessions in one browser. The household budget is 10,000 per experiment, split evenly across arms. Fixed horizon, default five years, extendable a year at a time.
- **Three modes.** Compare policies (two to four towns, one policy difference each). Test an AI mayor (one LLM-governed town plus an optional frozen-policy control; comparing two LLMs happens later through saved runs). Just play (one town, all levers).
- **Run screen** is two matching columns, Town A and Town B: a live drawing of the town (houses coloured by work status, Main-street buildings sized by staff, struggling and closed flagged), businesses ranked richest-first with status tags, four random households as cards with a follow action, then a shared annotated full-horizon chart, a plain-language verdict ending in a question, four stat cards, an event feed, and a "how to read this" panel. The AI mayor variant adds a decision strip, an expanded decision card (what it saw versus the truth, what it asked for versus what the rules allowed, its reasoning, an evidence check, what happened next) and a report card.
- **Setup screen** leads with "What do you want to find out?": mode cards, question cards backed by simulation evidence, the towns, and shared world settings. Seeds are rolled automatically and hidden from newcomers.
- **Question cards must be evidence-backed.** Audited against mechanics and the checked-in policy sweep: minimum wage, benefits, wage-versus-profit tax, and food subsidy are backed; public works is wired but unmeasured and needs a smoke run before it ships. Two copy errors caught: high benefits raise distress rather than lower it, and a food subsidy is a voucher that lowers what households pay without moving the posted price.
- **Fiscal number.** The UI shows the town hall's cash balance, signed, so "owes" is visible without a separate debt metric. The frame's `govDebt` is derived from that balance and stays consistent; the internal `public_debt` metric is inert and is not used.
- **Backend widening**, not new machinery: experiment id and arm label on SETUP with a server-side household cap, a curated set of about 30 metrics out of the 150 already computed, a discrete event stream in the frame, all firms instead of top twelve, a larger reshufflable household sample with populated recent events, full LLM decision history with the true values stored beside what the model saw, and a run-recording tool for backend-free frontend development.
- **Frontend architecture.** New shell around an experiment store (one socket and full frame history per arm, a derived layer that aligns arms by tick), a single narration module of event templates, replay mode from recorded runs, and the existing token mechanism with new values: light theme, Bricolage Grotesque and Instrument Sans, colour-blind-validated arm and status colours. Keep the current time-series component, its by-tick alignment, and the Government screen's before/after pattern.
- **Why the previous dashboard read as generic.** Structural, not stylistic: every screen was an equal-weight tile grid, one chart type used identically everywhere, three of 150 computed metrics reaching the browser, events warehouse-only, no time scrubbing, no run comparison. The 2026-09-06 spec banned glow and gauges and did not fix this.
- **Second-pass check.** After sign-off, a second Codex pass on `gpt-6-astra` (`docs/reviews/2026-09-24-frontend-redesign-spec-check2.md`) found no design reversal but corrected seven facts (benefits lower distress at newcomer scale, "exits" were declining ticks, food price shows no consistent separation rather than none, capacity is rejected at socket open, tick timing includes snapshots, provider failure has two cases, events and firms already partly reach the browser) and added two requirements: CONFIG action receipts so replay uses applied values, and a separate deterministic RNG for household sampling so browsing cannot change a matched comparison. The one-pager was corrected to say 1,000 households each by default and "about 30" metrics.
- **Signed off 2026-09-24.** Ayman approved the direction from the one-page overview and accepted legacy rules at 1,000 households per town as the interim default world; baseline calibration remains a backend task before phase 2 reaches newcomers.
- **Smoke runs at newcomer scale** (1,000 households, 260 ticks, seeds 1337 and 7): only the minimum-wage and benefits cards separate visibly; taxes, food subsidy and public works do not, so they wait in "Build my own". Every tested baseline world (legacy or `income_first` at 1,000 households, legacy at 5,000) ends five years as a depression, with unemployment 23 to 39 percent. `income_first` keeps the town hall solvent but raises household distress from 0.40 to 0.60 and sustains homelessness. Interim default stays legacy at 1,000 households; baseline calibration is a backend task before phase 2 reaches newcomers. Details in the spec, section 5.
- **Independent audit.** At Ayman's request the spec was audited by Codex (`gpt-6-sol`, high reasoning, read-only); the report is at `docs/reviews/2026-09-24-frontend-redesign-spec-audit.md`. Accepted and folded into the spec: the schema has 17 levers not 16; the frame already carries about 40 metric keys, the gap is which ones; closed firms leave the live collection and need an archive; the household cap must be a server-owned experiment record, not a per-socket claim; presets need a validated `initial_policy` on SETUP; the server needs horizon, FINISH and EXTEND; raw frames must not be retained in the browser; the story chart is a new component rather than the existing time series; the LLM rationale is truncated to 500 characters, rejection reasons are codes, the tax move cap is 5 points not 3, and the evidence audit checks cited figures not prose claims; "a week of groceries" is a posted unit price and the food-subsidy card needs an out-of-pocket metric. Lifecycle failure rules, non-functional requirements and a full-path benchmark were added.
- **Process.** Visual questions get mockups, not prose. Implementation is delegated to Opus 5.5 subagents; Sonnet handles reading and audits. Phases: backend widening and recording, then Setup and the policy Run screen, then the AI mayor screen, then saved experiments.
