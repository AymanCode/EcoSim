## Unreleased

### 2026-09-29: Agents/economy remediation, round 2 review fixes

Fixes from the round-2 final review of the remediation branch. One item per commit, newest first.

- **Phase 6 hygiene (round-2 review; golden-exact).** Before: in `Economy._match_labor_fast`, `employer_ids` and `switcher_order` were assigned only inside `if fix_switchers:` and read later under the same flag, so they were unbound names whenever the flag was off (correct at run time, but fragile and flagged by linters); and `SimulationConfig.__post_init__` did not check `CONFIG.households.tie_break_scale` or `CONFIG.labor_market.switcher_vacancy_cap_per_firm`, so a negative or non-finite scale or a negative cap was accepted silently. Now: both lists are initialised to `[]` before the flag block, and `__post_init__` raises `ValueError` unless `tie_break_scale` is finite and non-negative and `switcher_vacancy_cap_per_firm` is non-negative, next to the sibling elasticity checks. Evidence: new `test_phase6_flags.py::test_phase6_numeric_knobs_are_validated` (negative, NaN and infinite scale, negative cap; zero accepted). Goldens: seed 42 legacy and `income_first` match; seed 7 legacy matches the post-treasury-mirror-fix snapshot. Payment arm: same code on both arms.

- **Documentation polish (round-2 review; no behavior change).** Before: the docstrings of `FirmAgent._healthcare_labor_price_targets`, `adjust_wages_to_revenue_ratio` and `apply_price_and_wage_updates` said healthcare wages and distress cuts were pinned or floored at `minimum_wage_floor` (the config floor), which has not been true since B19 (phase 5b); `backend/tests_contracts/conftest.py` had three blank lines before `total_money_net_of_injections` (flake8 E303); the phase 6 entry had no OpenWiki note, its five "Money net of injections (t300)" rows said `201-300` in the Ticks column although the value is the tick-300 level, and the B13/B14 bullet did not say whom the own-employer skip affects or what the per-tick data shows about the money difference. Now: the docstrings say "the higher of the config floor and the policy minimum" (`_minimum_wage`); the extra blank line is gone; the phase 6 entry has an "OpenWiki refresh due" line naming the new flags, `plan_labor_supply(category_expected_prices=…)`, `_median_category_prices` and the trait-cache key; the five rows say `300`; and the B13/B14 bullet adds that the own-employer skip affects switchers in practice (hires and layoffs are mutually exclusive in the planners; the payment-arm Services-project +1 hire is the one exception) and that the money difference begins on the tick firm counts first differ (ticks 26, 30, 29 on seeds 42, 7, 11; checked again from the phase 6 raw per-tick CSVs), consistent with the firm-exit path (K02).

- **Rules doc v1.4 M05 amended in place (round-2 review; documentation only).** Before: M05 in `docs/ECONOMIC_AGENT_RULES.md` and the rules-doc v1.4 bullet in the phase 5b entry below said the legacy treasury direct loans, the no-bank medical loan and the affordability estimates use `v2_payment` and that only the payment arm's two direct treasury claims keep the one-year formula, which left out the bailout emergency loan (one-year formula on both arms until the bailout fix above). M05 also records that treasury direct loans are pooled per firm and that on legacy the installment mirror returns to zero at payoff (the treasury-mirror fix above). The v1.4 header gains one line noting the 2026-09-29 amendment. No code change.

- **Paid-off treasury loans stayed in the firm's installment mirror (round-2 review).** Before: the legacy treasury repayment at the top of `Economy._batch_apply_household_updates` paid `min(loan_payment_per_tick, government_loan_remaining, cash)` and lowered `government_loan_remaining`, but nothing ever lowered `loan_payment_per_tick`, so once the balance reached zero `FirmAgent._debt_service_per_tick` still counted the old installment (read by the housing rent-obligation path, the tight-labor wage ceiling and the revenue-per-worker wage path in `plan_wage`), and those firms planned as if they were still paying it. Now: when that repayment brings `government_loan_remaining` to zero, `loan_payment_per_tick` is set to 0.0. The treasury has no per-loan record: a firm's direct treasury loans are pooled in these two fields, so the pool reaching zero is the payoff of every loan in it, and the mirror loses all their installments once (the same rule commit d2544b1 applied to the bank mirror). The final payment is `min(installment, remaining)`, so the balance reaches exactly 0.0 and no residue is forgiven. Evidence: new `test_legacy_paid_off_treasury_loan_leaves_the_installment_mirror` in `test_loan_settlement.py` (before: `loan_payment_per_tick` stayed 100.09 after payoff and debt service 107.09 against 7.00). Ticks 1 and 80 are unchanged; the tick-200 and 300 rows are single-seed divergence from lower debt service in wage and rent planning. Seed 42 has no treasury payoff within 80 ticks.

- **Bailout emergency loans charged one year of interest on a two-year term (round-2 review, follow-up to A4).** Before: `Economy._execute_bailouts` booked `total_repayment = loan_amount * (1.0 + emergency_loan_interest)` into `government_loan_remaining` and `total_repayment / term_ticks` into `loan_payment_per_tick`, with `term_ticks = ticks_per_year * emergency_loan_term_years` (104 ticks at the default 2.0 years), so a two-year loan owed one year of interest on both arms. ### 2026-09-29: Agents/economy remediation, phase 6 (flag-gated macro fixes)

Five economy-wide behavior fixes, each behind a new config flag whose default (`False`) keeps the old behavior; all three goldens match exactly with every flag off. One fix per commit, newest first. No default is flipped here: the owner decides from the flag-off versus flag-on comparison tables (1,500 households, 300 ticks, legacy seeds 42/7/11 plus seed 42 on `income_first`). Plan: [docs/reviews/2026-09-28-agents-economy-remediation-plan.md](docs/reviews/2026-09-28-agents-economy-remediation-plan.md), phase 6. - **B10, category preferences applied once (`CONFIG.households.fix_preference_applied_once`, default `False`).** Before (flag off): `HouseholdAgent.__post_init__` stores `category_weights = normalize(base * preference)` for food, housing and services, and `Economy._household_static_traits` (the phase 2 cache `_batch_plan_consumption` reads) multiplies those weights by the same three preferences again before normalizing them into each household's budget fractions, so shares scale with the preference squared (in the new test, preferences 2/1/1 give food 4/6 of the budget instead of 2/4). With the flag on: the cached fractions are the stored `category_weights` normalized, with no second multiplication; the preference still acts once, through the constructor. The trait cache now keys on the flag as well as the household list and its length, so toggling the flag between calls rebuilds it (tested both ways). The unused-by-the-engine no-override branch of `HouseholdAgent._plan_category_purchases` (only tests call it without an override) still multiplies again and is unchanged. Test: `test_phase6_flags.py::test_b10_*`. Comparison read: the median household's static budget fractions barely move (food 0.357 → 0.352, housing 0.289 → 0.310, services 0.344 → 0.340 on seed 42; the other seeds agree within 0.005) and realized median spend shares stay inside the seed spread, but the spread across households narrows (housing p10-p90 0.11-0.46 → 0.20-0.39, food 0.25-0.49 → 0.29-0.42), so the preference ranges need re-tuning only if the owner wants to keep today's dispersion (roughly their squares: housing (0.16, 1.96), food and services (0.64, 1.44)); every macro row stays inside its seed spread (late median cash +30 against 58, Gini −0.040 against 0.080), so there is no macro signal at this size. Table: phase 6 comparison bullet.
- **B32, seller tie-break noise per firm and additive switching friction (`CONFIG.households.fix_seller_choice_noise`, default `False`; scale `CONFIG.households.tie_break_scale`, default 0.001).** Before (flag off): `HouseholdAgent._plan_category_purchases` adds to each seller's utility a noise vector from `_get_purchase_tie_break_noise`, drawn once per household and category, uniform in ±0.25 and indexed by the seller's position in the awareness-filtered array, so the same firm gets a different value whenever the pool is reordered or resized, and the ±0.25 swing is larger than the switching frictions (0.02 food, 0.05 services, 0.15 housing); switching friction is multiplicative, `best > current * (1 + friction)`, which for a negative current utility sets the bar below the current firm, so friction stops working (in the new test, a 0.5% gain at utility -2 switches the food primary despite 2% friction; reordering two equal sellers flips the chosen primary). With the flag on: the noise is a splitmix64 hash of (household id, category, firm id) mapped to ±`tie_break_scale`, so a firm keeps its value across pool changes and the noise only breaks near-ties; it is pure arithmetic and consumes no RNG, and the last vector per category is cached on the household by the firm-id bytes (uncached, the hash cost about 5.7 µs a call and made a 1,500-household, 300-tick run about 27% slower; cached it is about 0.7 µs against 0.5 µs for the old lookup, and the run output is byte-identical). Friction becomes additive: `best > current + friction * max(|current|, 1e-9)`, chosen over a plain additive margin because it is identical to the old rule whenever the current utility is positive, so the existing friction constants keep their calibrated meaning as relative advantages and only the negative-utility inversion changes. Both the ordinary category loop and the housing path (`_apply_switching_friction`) use the same rule. Tests: `test_phase6_flags.py::test_b32_*` (flag off: the pick follows position and the negative-utility switch happens; flag on: the pick follows the firm, noise is bounded and stable per firm, the shared RNGs are untouched, the small gain no longer switches). Comparison read: no legacy row moves beyond its seed spread; the most consistent direction is higher late cash (median +20 against a spread of 58, positive on all four runs) and a lower late Gini, both inside the noise. Table: phase 6 comparison bullet.

With the flag on: phase 0 (`_phase_pre_plan_state`) computes the median posted price per category from the market snapshot `_build_firm_market_views` already builds (Healthcare is not in it), passes it to `plan_labor_supply` as `category_expected_prices`, and living cost uses the `housing` and `food` medians (a category with no firms falls back to `default_price_level`); the post-warmup reset uses the same medians from a snapshot built at that point. Posted prices are public information at the planning phase. In the new test a worker whose cash sits between the old living cost (0.3 × 10 + 2 × 10 = 23) and the new one (0.3 × 200 + 2 × 8 = 76) starts searching only with the flag on. `price_beliefs` itself and the consumption planner are unchanged. - **B13/B14, job-switcher vacancies (`CONFIG.labor_market.fix_switcher_vacancies`, default `False`; cap `CONFIG.labor_market.switcher_vacancy_cap_per_firm`, default 1).** Before (flag off): in `Economy._match_labor_fast`, if any household plans to switch jobs, every private firm not planning to hire, including firms planning layoffs, gets `planned_hires_count = job_switch_count` written into the caller's production plan (so failed-hiring diagnostics later see vacancies the firm never planned); those vacancies are filled from the whole candidate pool, so two switchers let every idle firm hire two unemployed workers; a switcher can be "hired" by their own employer (counted as turnover); and a switcher who found nothing is put back with their old employer even when that employer laid them off this tick. In the new test, two switchers give three idle firms two vacancies each, all three unemployed non-switchers are hired, and the laid-off switcher is back at the firm that laid them off. With the flag on: switcher vacancies live in a local dict (the caller's plans are not written), at most the cap per firm, only for firms with no planned layoffs, and they are filled only from switchers (skill order, then household id) whose reservation wage the offer meets; any firm skips a candidate it already employs (for ordinary hiring firms the skipped candidate's bucket head is restored after that firm finishes, so later firms still see them); the fallback leaves a laid-off switcher unemployed. In practice the own-employer skip affects switchers: firm planners make hires and layoffs mutually exclusive, so an ordinary hiring firm does not meet a worker it is laying off, the only exception being the payment-arm Services-project +1 hire, which can add a vacancy to a firm that also plans layoffs. In the test the unemployed stay unemployed, the switcher moves to the one other idle firm, and the laid-off switcher stays laid off. The legacy matcher (`ECOSIM_LABOR_MATCH_MODE=legacy`) has no synthetic vacancies and is unchanged. Tests: `test_phase6_flags.py::test_b13_*`, `test_b14_*` (six firm orders). Comparison read: a strong signal, far outside the noise: unemployment rises by about 24 points in ticks 41-100 (seed spread 0.8) and 3.5 in 101-200 (spread 0.7), peaking at 35-40% on legacy and about 50% on `income_first` near tick 60 before returning to the flag-off level by about tick 150, and GDP falls by about 14,000 a tick in ticks 101-200 (spread 7,800); the old synthetic vacancies were a large hiring channel for the unemployed after warmup, so flipping this flag alone would produce a long post-warmup slump unless firms plan those hires as real vacancies. Legacy money net of injections ends about 20,000 lower (spread 7,400); the matcher moves no money itself, so this runs through the known unresolved legacy paths (K02, for example an exiting firm's negative cash), a hypothesis these runs cannot confirm. ### 2026-09-24: Frontend redesign, design decisions

Design phase only. No frontend code changed yet. Full design: [docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-24-frontend-learning-redesign-design.md](docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-24-frontend-learning-redesign-design.md). Approved mockups: [docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-24-mockups/](docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-24-mockups/).

Decisions:

- **Audience.** The dashboard is for newcomers learning economics by watching, not an analyst's console. Friendly names ("people out of work", "typical weekly pay", "town hall cash"), one-sentence definitions, a picture beside every number. The dense metric grid survives behind a "Show me all the numbers" link.
- **Unit of work is an experiment**, not a run: a mode, a seed, a shared world config, and one to four arms ("towns"). Arms run as live side-by-side backend sessions in one browser. The household budget is 10,000 per experiment, split evenly across arms. Fixed horizon, default five years, extendable a year at a time.
- **Three modes.** Compare policies (two to four towns, one policy difference each). Test an AI mayor (one LLM-governed town plus an optional frozen-policy control; comparing two LLMs happens later through saved runs). Just play (one town, all levers).
- **Run screen** is two matching columns, Town A and Town B: a live drawing of the town (houses coloured by work status, Main-street buildings sized by staff, struggling and closed flagged), businesses ranked richest-first with status tags, four random households as cards with a follow action, then a shared annotated full-horizon chart, a plain-language verdict ending in a question, four stat cards, an event feed, and a "how to read this" panel. The AI mayor variant adds a decision strip, an expanded decision card (what it saw versus the truth, what it asked for versus what the rules allowed, its reasoning, an evidence check, what happened next) and a report card.
- **Setup screen** leads with "What do you want to find out?": mode cards, question cards backed by simulation evidence, the towns, and shared world settings. Seeds are rolled automatically and hidden from newcomers.
- **Question cards must be evidence-backed.** Audited against mechanics and the checked-in policy sweep: minimum wage, benefits, wage-versus-profit tax, and food subsidy are backed; public works is wired but unmeasured and needs a smoke run before it ships. Two copy errors caught: high benefits raise distress rather than lower it, and a food subsidy is a voucher that lowers what households pay without moving the posted price.
- **Fiscal number.** The UI shows the town hall's cash balance, signed, so "owes" is visible without a separate debt metric. The frame's `govDebt` is derived from that balance and stays consistent; the internal `public_debt` metric is inert and is not used.
- **Backend widening**, not new machinery: experiment id and arm label on SETUP with a server-side household cap, a curated set of about 30 metrics out of the 150 already computed, a discrete event stream in the frame, all firms instead of top twelve, a larger reshufflable household sample with populated recent events, full LLM decision history with the true values stored beside what the model saw, and a run-recording tool for backend-free frontend development.
- **Frontend architecture.** New shell around an experiment store (one socket and full frame history per arm, a derived layer that aligns arms by tick), a single narration module of event templates, replay mode from recorded runs, and the existing token mechanism with new values: light theme, Bricolage Grotesque and Instrument Sans, colour-blind-validated arm and status colours. Keep the current time-series component, its by-tick alignment, and the Government screen's before/after pattern.
- **Why the previous dashboard read as generic.** Structural, not stylistic: every screen was an equal-weight tile grid, one chart type used identically everywhere, three of 150 computed metrics reaching the browser, events warehouse-only, no time scrubbing, no run comparison. The 2026-09-06 spec banned glow and gauges and did not fix this.
- **Second-pass check.** After sign-off, a second Codex pass on `gpt-6-astra` (`docs/reviews/2026-09-24-frontend-redesign-spec-check2.md`) found no design reversal but corrected seven facts (benefits lower distress at newcomer scale, "exits" were declining ticks, food price shows no consistent separation rather than none, capacity is rejected at socket open, tick timing includes snapshots, provider failure has two cases, events and firms already partly reach the browser) and added two requirements: CONFIG action receipts so replay uses applied values, and a separate deterministic RNG for household sampling so browsing cannot change a matched comparison. The one-pager was corrected to say 1,000 households each by default and "about 30" metrics.
- **Signed off 2026-09-24.** Ayman approved the direction from the one-page overview and accepted legacy rules at 1,000 households per town as the interim default world; baseline calibration remains a backend task before phase 2 reaches newcomers.
- **Smoke runs at newcomer scale** (1,000 households, 260 ticks, seeds 1337 and 7): only the minimum-wage and benefits cards separate visibly; taxes, food subsidy and public works do not, so they wait in "Build my own". Every tested baseline world (legacy or `income_first` at 1,000 households, legacy at 5,000) ends five years as a depression, with unemployment 23 to 39 percent. `income_first` keeps the town hall solvent but raises household distress from 0.40 to 0.60 and sustains homelessness. Interim default stays legacy at 1,000 households; baseline calibration is a backend task before phase 2 reaches newcomers. Details in the spec, section 5.
- **Independent audit.** At Ayman's request the spec was audited by Codex (`gpt-6-sol`, high reasoning, read-only); the report is at `docs/reviews/2026-09-24-frontend-redesign-spec-audit.md`. Accepted and folded into the spec: the schema has 17 levers not 16; the frame already carries about 40 metric keys, the gap is which ones; closed firms leave the live collection and need an archive; the household cap must be a server-owned experiment record, not a per-socket claim; presets need a validated `initial_policy` on SETUP; the server needs horizon, FINISH and EXTEND; raw frames must not be retained in the browser; the story chart is a new component rather than the existing time series; the LLM rationale is truncated to 500 characters, rejection reasons are codes, the tax move cap is 5 points not 3, and the evidence audit checks cited figures not prose claims; "a week of groceries" is a posted unit price and the food-subsidy card needs an out-of-pocket metric. Lifecycle failure rules, non-functional requirements and a full-path benchmark were added.
- **Process.** Visual questions get mockups, not prose. Implementation is delegated to Opus 5.5 subagents; Sonnet handles reading and audits. Phases: backend widening and recording, then Setup and the policy Run screen, then the AI mayor screen, then saved experiments.

### 2026-09-25: Bailout rule made consistent

- **The disagreement.** Three places read `bailout_policy: 'all'` differently. The economy ignores the target and lends to any distressed private firm. The AI mayor's prompt asks for target `none`. - **The rule, everywhere.** `off` needs target `none` and budget 0. `sector` needs a target of food, housing, services or healthcare and a budget above 0. `all` needs target `none` and a budget above 0. The target is checked before the budget, with one reason per group.
