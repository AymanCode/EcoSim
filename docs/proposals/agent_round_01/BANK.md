# Bank proposals: legible credit, scarce liquidity, bounded rate transmission

**Status:** proposed, independent bank-role review; no implementation selected. **Author/model:** EcoSim bank proposal writer, Sol/high. **Source:** live `origin/main` and HEAD `4f693890b5f133c2c6c1dcbec9b1ec91ced97b46`, with W01–W05 working-tree changes recorded in [round manifest](ROUND_MANIFEST.json). Relevant SHA-256: `backend/agents.py` `9d3d17ea01d35344df3fe693db3aa336540d530439ed7a7b2f5a010485ae73f1`; `backend/economy.py` `c0d95448181fd016ca4527be7f80897b5bb71f26432fffa6ccb83f5b83278092`; shared [rules](../../ECONOMIC_AGENT_RULES.md) v1.1 `8990f32d972ecef56f64abda137bfc31069aa36039b4ee9d9d2156e46d358dce`. **Scope:** bank contracts and decisions, with explicit requests to Economy and housing owners. Related queue: W06/W14, with W08, W10 and W12 interfaces. Binding R01–R12; especially M05 and limitations K02–K08.

The bank should make three things observable: what a loan costs over its stated term, whether a borrower can pay from plausible resources, and when limited bank cash prevents credit or withdrawals. These changes improve within-model comparisons without requiring a complete monetary system. EcoSim currently uses a **prefunded bank**: ordinary loans debit `BankAgent.cash_reserves`, deposits move household cash into reserves, and required reserves restrict new lending. This is a deliberate institutional assumption for these proposals, not a claim about contemporary commercial banking. The [Bank of England's money-creation account](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2014/q1/money-creation-in-the-modern-economy) describes real banks generally creating deposits when making loans rather than lending out pre-existing deposits; adopting that institution would require a separate balance-sheet, payment and public-money specification. None is smuggled into this round.

## BANK-P01 — Make ordinary loan terms and claims internally consistent

**Priority 1; readiness: bounded specification after one contract-choice decision.** Policy question: when a rate, term or borrower mix changes, does the model compare like contracts and route each payment to its funder? This is a source-grounded convention issue, not an executed finding that one convention improves outcomes.

**Current path.** `BankAgent.originate_loan` in [`backend/agents.py`](../../../backend/agents.py) labels `annual_rate` but sets `remaining = principal × (1 + annual_rate)` for the entire term and divides by `term_ticks`; a 26-tick and a 104-tick loan with the same principal/rate have the same total scheduled interest. `_risk_adjusted_rate` adds a score-dependent spread. `Economy._issue_firm_loan`, `_offer_consumption_loans`, `_offer_service_infrastructure_loans` and `_issue_medical_loan` mirror that full-term amount in firm/household fields. `Economy._collect_bank_loan_repayments` runs in phase 9.5, before phase-10 household wage/tax/transfer settlement, and calls `BankAgent.collect_repayment`; `govt_backed=True` means the treasury funded the principal and receives **both** actual principal and interest under W01. A registered medical loan has that one servicing path; `HouseholdAgent.make_medical_loan_payment` applies only to an unregistered no-bank fallback under W02. `Economy._compute_housing_pmt` and `_service_housing_mortgage_debt` instead use a separate amortizing `LoanContract` in phase 6.6b. Housing origination debits bank reserves directly and is not an ordinary entry in `bank.active_loans`; its principal cannot simply be added to `total_loans_outstanding` without defining how exposure and settlement totals reconcile. The housing firm also mirrors it into legacy debt fields.

**Proposed contract.** For **new ordinary registered loans only**, choose a fixed-rate, reducing-principal schedule: annual nominal rate `a`, per-tick rate `r = a/52`, `N` weekly installments, payment `P × r / [1 − (1 + r)^(-N)]` or `P/N` at zero rate. Track `principal_remaining`, `accrued_interest_due`, `scheduled_payment`, `rate_at_origination`, `term_ticks_remaining`, `funder` (`bank` or `treasury`) and `subtype` on the authoritative contract; compute interest from outstanding principal once per scheduled servicing tick. Actual payment first clears due interest, then principal; no cash payment means no principal reduction. Do not compound missed interest or invent fees in this slice; record an unpaid-interest claim separately until the explicit default/write-off rule resolves it. Bound `P > 0`, `N ≥ 1`, `a ≥ 0`, finite payments and payoff tolerances. The scalar mirror fields on households/firms are read-only projections from active contracts after settlement, not separately serviced obligations. Existing active loans retain their original frozen schedule until payoff, or a versioned one-time conversion must specify the claim change; silently reinterpreting `remaining` would alter old runs. Keep housing's amortizing schedule and phase-6.6b owner in place, while documenting whether its exposure joins a common lender-risk total; never collect it again at phase 9.5.

Illustration, **uncalibrated model currency**: a 100-unit 26-week loan quoted at 10% annual should have less contractual interest than the same loan over 104 weeks, all else equal. Today's ordinary formula charges 10 units in both cases; the proposed schedule varies total interest with time outstanding. A creditworthy employed household with a cash buffer may pay the schedule, while a low-cash household with the same initial rate may miss phase-9.5 payments despite wages arriving later. A housing provider with adequate occupancy may qualify for its distinct mortgage; if units or rental receipts are absent, a cheaper schedule does not create a viable project. The fixed-rate [CFPB amortization explanation](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-does-paying-down-a-mortgage-work-en-1943/) supports separating principal and interest over time, but supplies no EcoSim term, spread, default rule or universal rate. A simpler fallback is to relabel the current charge as a **full-term flat fee** and use it consistently in displays; that suffices if rate/term experiments are excluded.

**Flow and interfaces.** Existing: funder cash → borrower cash at origination; borrower cash → actual funder in the current servicing phase; unpaid claims → funder write-off on default. Proposed: separate principal/interest claims and receipts by funder, with no new payer. Hypothetical later feedback: clearer debt service changes firm investment, household consumption credit access and subsequent sales/earnings; supply capacity, borrower cash, and provider capacity can block it. The bank-owned `active_loans` writer is `originate_loan`/collector; Economy's issue methods read the payment and update `bank_loan_*`, `service_infrastructure_loan_*`, `consumption_loan_*` and `medical_loan_*` mirrors; `BankAgent.to_dict`, `Economy.get_economic_metrics`, server/warehouse loan telemetry and existing evidence consumers need the old-versus-new claim definition. Preserve no-bank medical fallback as explicitly unregistered, with its unresolved funding counterparty flagged under K02, not silently counted as a bank asset. A shared-contract revision is requested for the new ordinary contract convention, versioning/migration and housing-exposure definition; housing payment ownership remains with its current resolver.

**Acceptance and limits.** At zero rate, exactly `P` of principal is repaid; at positive rate the final payment clears both claims within tolerance. A partial payment credits only its funder once and leaves the correct balances; a registered medical loan never invokes the fallback; a treasury-funded medical/firm loan never credits bank interest; an existing housing mortgage is serviced only in 6.6b. A 52-week contract cannot have the same rate-derived total charge as a 104-week contract by construction. Compare matched pre-policy borrowers, terms, initial cash, seed/RNG state, execution mode and warmup, distinguishing contract-version cohorts; exclude legacy contracts from rate-comparative claims. Per-tick cost stays `O(L)` for active registered loans plus the existing `O(M)` housing pass, with `O(L)` extra scalar fields; measure the integrated 1k/10k workloads and report variance before claiming budget compliance. The unchosen issue is whether interest is accrued on missed payments or forgiven at write-off; the no-compounding simplification is proposed for this slice. Roll back the new convention if funder claims or term comparisons fail reconciliation; retain the explicit current-fee labeling fallback.

## BANK-P02 — Underwrite against cash flow and protect withdrawals

**Priority 2; readiness: needs a modeling choice about liquidity allocation and emergency public credit.** Policy question: which otherwise identical loan requests should clear when households/firms have different repayment capacity and bank liquidity is scarce? [Basel Committee credit-risk principles](https://www.bis.org/publications/202504-guidelines-principles-management-credit-risk) support assessing repayment ability and monitoring exposures as banking practice; they do not establish this simulator's threshold, guarantee policy or loss distribution.

**Current path.** `HouseholdAgent.maybe_request_consumption_loan` uses cash below `subsistence_min_cash`, score at least 0.4 and consumption debt below four times an income proxy; `Economy._offer_consumption_loans` checks `bank.can_lend`/`lendable_cash` and the full requested amount. Medical credit uses score at least 0.15, no current medical loan, then bank cash or treasury-funded fallback. Firm `_issue_firm_loan` uses a score floor, trailing-revenue debt ceiling and bank/treasury/direct-treasury sequence. Service upgrades test rolling revenue against wage/debt costs; housing expansion has its own DSCR/LTV checks. `BankAgent.required_reserves = total_deposits × reserve_ratio`, while `withdraw` can pay only available `cash_reserves`, so the rule protecting *new lending* does not itself guarantee all household withdrawals. Household planning counts 90% of deposits as accessible; `_withdraw_deposits_for_planned_consumption`, `_ensure_cash_for_payment`, healthcare, rent and end-tick deposit routines get the **actual** withdrawal, which may be short. Credit scores also change from employment/revenue signals at phase 11.4, after this tick's applications.

**Proposed minimum.** Keep the prefunded institution. At each existing origination phase, use only then-observable cash, settled prior-period earnings/sales, bank score/history, existing **scheduled** debt service and current price/rate quote. Make a conservative capacity test against an estimated post-essential-expense cash buffer over a short fixed trailing window, using the same risk formula for every applicant type but separate expense proxies: household basic food/rent and employment stability; firm payroll/essential operating costs, trailing sell-through/revenue and runway. Preserve the existing loan-purpose gates, housing DSCR/LTV and score as coarse prior signals; the new test checks whether proposed weekly payment fits an explicitly bounded cash-flow envelope, rather than substituting an invented “safe/unsafe person” trait. Avoid future wage, next-tick sales, unseen care outcomes or another actor's private state. When the envelope is too small, offer a smaller amount only if it still funds the indivisible purpose; otherwise decline with a reason. For medical care, partial credit that cannot cover the visit is not a completed visit. Bank liquidity availability is rechecked at disbursement, with `required_reserves` still protected; withdrawals stay callable in the existing phases and return actual amounts. Choose and document a small **liquidity buffer** for expected withdrawals in addition to the reserve floor, based on bounded recent aggregate outflows, rather than claiming the present reserve ratio alone guarantees deposit access. No automatic recapitalization or new money is implied. Treasury-funded fallback remains gated by actual `GovernmentAgent.cash_balance`; ordinary bank-funded and treasury-funded claims stay distinct. For housing mortgages, the housing owner must either include separate housing principal in a bank exposure/liquidity view or explicitly exclude it from portfolio-risk claims; current direct reserve debits already affect cash.

Profiles are defined by **observables**, not labels. A household with stable settled wages, small required expenses and a deposit buffer can service a modest consumption loan; another with the same score but a recent rent increase, missed installments and low cash should receive less or none. A services firm with sustained receipts and unused slot capacity can finance an upgrade; one with a long zero-revenue streak and payroll due should not obtain the same amount solely because its score has lagged. A solvent borrower can still be denied when `lendable_cash` minus the chosen withdrawal buffer is exhausted; conversely, a high deposit rate cannot induce deposits from a household with no excess cash. Traits already affecting requests (`saving_tendency`, `deposit_buffer_weeks`, `deposit_fraction`, firm personality) remain in their existing readers; financial differences here come chiefly from changing balances, earnings, occupancy, debt and history. No new personality field is proposed.

**Flow and interfaces.** Proposed direct chain: an eligible household/firm requests principal → bank checks borrower capacity and free cash → its reserves fall and borrower cash rises if funded → the borrower purchases scarce goods, care or expansion inputs under existing resolvers → seller/provider receipts affect next-tick earnings and borrower repayment capacity. Existing settlement then collects at 9.5 or housing 6.6b; later defaults reduce the actual lender's claim. A competing effect is that tighter buffers preserve withdrawals but ration productive or medical credit; where a provider has no visits/units, credit cannot add physical capacity. Read `cash_balance`, `bank_deposit`, `last_consumption_spending`, rent, prior settled wage/benefit and debt fields for households; `last_revenue`, `revenue_ema`, `trailing_revenue_12t`, wage bill, runway and existing loan payments for firms. New bounded aggregate outflow state is bank-owned and updated at end tick from actual withdrawal telemetry, with a stated initial value; per-request rejection reason and offered amount may be ephemeral diagnostics. Readers include Economy's scalar origination methods, bank metrics, optional server/warehouse evidence, and no extra per-household batch scan beyond existing request iteration. `Economy._batch_plan_consumption` already includes 90% deposit liquidity; its performance-mode cached plan must be invalidated or its affordability interpretation narrowed if bank withdrawal availability changes materially. This requires a shared bank/Economy/household/health/housing contract on whether inaccessible deposits remain planned spend, and a fiscal choice for treasury rescue. Keep the same default in both scenario arms until explicitly selected.

**Acceptance and limits.** Test distinct decline reasons for borrower shortfall, bank liquidity, treasury shortfall and physical-purpose insufficiency. For every disbursement, funder cash falls exactly by principal and borrower cash rises; on partial withdrawal, bank liability and household deposit fall only by the actual amount, and no purchase is attributed to unfunded cash. Observations should use pre-intervention cohorts by initial cash-buffer, score, settled-income and firm sector, then separately count entrants/exits and transitions. Compare identical initial loans/deposits, contracts, shocks, policy timing, mode/cadence and end-of-phase-16 balances, reporting loan approvals **and** denied requests, missed payments, withdrawal shortfalls, completed care, housing units and firm survival. A low-approval result is not automatically a success. Falsification includes no heterogeneity after the observable capacity signals differ, or approval when the funder's usable cash is insufficient. One pass over existing applicants plus a bounded bank-outflow accumulator is `O(A + L)` time and `O(1)` aggregate state aside from optional bounded diagnostics; existing `BankAgent._firm_existing_debt` is `O(L)` per call and should reuse the phase-11.4 debt index if material, without a new population-by-loan scan. Defer if integrated measurement exceeds the cumulative speed budget or the no-bank fallback cannot be accounted for. A simpler fallback is an observable payment-to-income gate layered on existing credit-score gates, with no dynamic buffer; use it if withdrawal data do not justify the extra state.

## BANK-P03 — Test one bounded new-loan/deposit rate channel

**Priority 3; readiness: requires an institutional/policy choice, then evidence.** This is a deliberately narrow W14 slice, not a claim that EcoSim has monetary policy. `GovernmentAgent.target_inflation_rate` is stored/displayed, and `Economy._adjust_government_policy` passes a zero inflation placeholder; neither is a working feedback controller. `BankAgent.base_interest_rate` prices new ordinary loans, `current_annual_rate` prices new housing mortgages, and `update_deposit_rate` sets a share of the base rate, with thin-loan-book and interest-income limits. End-tick `_process_bank_deposits` pays interest only from current bank loan-interest income above a margin and changes deposit sweeps with the quoted rate. Those are existing partial channels, not evidence that an inflation-target slider changes realized inflation. The [Bank of England's transmission review](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2024/2024/about-a-rate-of-general-interest-how-monetary-policy-transmits) describes policy rates affecting financial conditions and then spending with delays; [Federal Reserve analysis of euro-area deposits](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/what-determines-passthrough-of-policy-rates-to-deposit-rates-in-the-euro-area-20230728.html) finds deposit pass-through sluggish and incomplete in that setting. Neither gives an EcoSim pass-through coefficient or warrants a mechanical inflation response. [Federal Reserve research on household consumption](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/household-consumption-does-not-respond-directly-to-interest-rates.htm) also cautions against assuming a large direct consumption effect from the quoted rate alone.

**Proposed experiment.** Introduce a bounded, externally set *scenario funding-rate path* at a safe tick boundary, separate from `target_inflation_rate` and from fiscal `GovernmentAgent` levers. A published fixed schedule changes **new** bank loan quotes by a bounded pass-through and deposit quotes after a declared lag; it does not reprice existing fixed loans, pay unfunded deposit interest, change treasury loan terms automatically, or alter bank reserves by fiat. All annual rates convert to weekly within BANK-P01's contract. Existing BankAgent liquidity and risk gates still decide quantity. Choose the pass-through and lag as scenario assumptions, sweep plausible ranges and retain a zero-change baseline; no universal calibration is asserted. If P01 is not selected, present this only as a contract-safe diagnostic of existing full-term-fee quotes, not a credible annual-rate experiment.

For an uncalibrated contrast, a debt-free depositor with excess cash could receive more interest and sweep more cash after the lag, while a cash-poor household with no deposit cannot benefit; a services firm at the margin of a positive-return expansion may forgo the loan when its **new** scheduled payment rises. A firm with abundant self-finance is unaffected, and a bank at the liquidity floor lends nothing even when the quote falls. The chain is **proposed** scenario rate → bank new-loan/deposit quote → **existing/proposed** borrower capacity and deposit sweep → **hypothetical** investment, sales, wages and firm prices in later ticks → **hypothetical** observed inflation. Supply limits or fixed contracts can block the chain; bank interest receipts and deposit payouts redistribute model currency, and any bank loss is allocated to its own claim rather than to a fictitious public fund. The experiment makes no claim that a rate change reliably controls the current non-CPI price metric.

**Interfaces and checks.** Scenario path writer belongs at the server/config safe boundary; BankAgent reads the current exogenous rate once per tick for new quotes, with a tiny lagged scalar and bounds. Readers include firm and household loan-offer paths, housing `current_annual_rate`, deposit-rate and sweep logic, dashboard/LLM capability descriptions, run manifests, telemetry and any warehouse/forecasting consumer that compares interventions. If user-facing control is selected later, it needs an explicit canonical schema/runtime/serialization decision; do not silently map `inflationRate` to it. Hold source state, ownership RNG, timing, mode, rates on existing contracts and fiscal closure matched. Observe quote, applications, approvals, deposit interest **actually paid**, bank/treasury cash, investment, output and specified price series at phase 16 and later fixed horizons; separate pre-policy depositors/borrowers and new applicants. Accept when only post-boundary contracts get changed quotes, funding is conserved, the paid deposit yield never exceeds its earnings budget, and zero/fully blocked exposure produces no invented direct demand or inflation change. `O(1)` rate state and `O(A)` quote reads are expected; benchmark jointly with P01/P02. Roll back the policy interpretation if the measured pathway never reaches an implemented decision or metric; retain a labeled exogenous quote stress test. The unresolved choice is whether EcoSim should represent an external central-bank policy rate at all, versus a bank funding-cost shock under its prefunded institution.

These are proposals, with no simulation or benchmark result claimed. They support coherent **within-EcoSim** financial comparisons after the shared choices are fixed; they do not establish empirical loan pricing, a regulatory reserve rule, monetary-policy effectiveness, a real-world inflation target or a country-level forecast.
