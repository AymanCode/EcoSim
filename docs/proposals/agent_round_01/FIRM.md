# Firm proposals: sellable investment, funded entry, and wage offers

**Status:** proposed, independent firm review; no engine change. **Author/model:** Codex, `gpt-5.6-sol` (high). **Source:** working tree of 2026-09-21 over `4f693890b5f133c2c6c1dcbec9b1ec91ced97b46` (verified equal to live `origin/main`), including W01–W05; manifest SHA-256: `backend/agents.py` `9d3d17ea01d35344df3fe693db3aa336540d530439ed7a7b2f5a010485ae73f1`, `backend/economy.py` `c0d95448181fd016ca4527be7f80897b5bb71f26432fffa6ccb83f5b83278092`, `backend/config.py` `eb97bc817fe698ede3b4b0e796866a1bd3c7c853f5345218d42817646051ae1f`. **Contract:** economic-agent rules v1.1, especially R01–R12, M01/M02/M05–M07 and K02–K08. **Role:** ordinary private Food/Services firms, with specialist and baseline paths identified where affected. **Queue:** W06, W08/W09 and W15; W12 is a fiscal dependency.

The first useful change is to ask whether new capacity can sell, using the firm's *existing* expectation before it spends. The second is to give every new firm's opening cash a real funder and record who owns the residual claim. The third removes an automatic private wage rise caused by the unemployment-benefit preset, leaving wage offers to respond to applicants, actual sales, costs, and legal floors. None assumes that firms know future sales or that a tax cut must increase output.

## Shared source map and evidence limits

At phase 1, `Economy.step` refreshes each firm's `FirmHealthSnapshot`, may supply long-term capital credit, then calls production/labor, price, wage and capital plans (`backend/economy.py:1589–1690`). `FirmAgent.plan_production_and_labor` already smooths completed sales plus **bounded** lost sales, caps growth and updates `expected_sales_units` (`backend/agents.py:2960–3028, 3603–3734`). Actual sales, inventory and prices come from household category choice and sequential `_clear_goods_market`; an ID target does not switch to another supplier when empty (`backend/economy.py:3771–3955`). `_update_statistics` writes last sales, unmet demand and sell-through for the next planning tick (`backend/economy.py:6827–6852`). This is a source finding, not a claim that demand is measured without error. A rejected high price can reduce purchases without becoming recorded lost sales; one firm's unsold stock does not reveal the whole category's willingness to pay.

`plan_pricing` already reacts to inventory and demand, applies a variable/full-cost floor and **receives the current profit-tax rate**; Services and Healthcare also gross up target margins for profit tax (`backend/agents.py:4476–4838`; call at `backend/economy.py:1643–1652`). `_apply_price_stabilization_to_plan` can cap rises (`backend/economy.py:295–325`). A separate “tax pass-through multiplier” would double a live response. `plan_wage` already reads failed vacancies, applicant reservation wages, category offers, debt service and margin (`backend/agents.py:4839–5070`). `set_personality` samples response speeds, risk tolerance, hiring limits and R&D, all with live readers, but `investment_propensity` is sampled and serialized without a decision reader: `plan_capital_investment` uses a common one-unit rule (`backend/agents.py:2795–2886, 4425–4474`). New randomness or a renamed personality field is unnecessary.

Phase 5 produces and pays costs; phase 6 allocates scarce goods; phase 7 assesses tax from sales and cost snapshots; phase 9 credits firm receipts, deducts profit tax, and commits planned offers; phase 9.5 services bank loans; phase 10 services direct public firm loans; phase 12 exits firms; phase 13 enters firms; phase 15 records statistics; phase 16 pays owner dividends (`backend/economy.py:1850–1955, 2070–2121`; `backend/agents.py:5308–5348, 5693–5743`). Price plans normally affect **later** purchases, while Services clearing can impose an effective price floor in the current tick (`backend/economy.py:3718–3736`). Normal and performance modes both execute firm plans each tick, but performance mode reuses household consumption plans for four intervening ticks and updates wellbeing every tenth (`backend/economy.py:1767–1779, 2066–2069`). These different cadences are comparison settings, not optimization equivalents.

Primary evidence is deliberately narrower than a simulator calibration. A [Duke CFO survey study](https://www.nber.org/books-and-chapters/nber-macroeconomics-annual-2015-volume-30/expectations-and-investment) links managers' expected earnings growth to investment plans and realized investment and finds predictable forecast errors; it supports a bounded expectation input, not a universal investment threshold. A [New Zealand firm-belief survey and information experiment](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21092) documents dispersed beliefs and updating after information; it does not identify EcoSim's weekly sales-smoothing coefficients. [Austrian linked vacancy data](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25118) finds a relationship between vacancy duration and entry wages, but a small one; it supports a response to *observed hiring difficulty*, not a large automatic wage elasticity. [Austrian unemployment-insurance reforms](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25230) found little wage response for initially employed workers; that setting cannot prove zero effect for all EcoSim new hires. [US banking-deregulation and startup data](https://www.nber.org/papers/w15499) find entry margins changed more than average startup size; the reforms do not dictate a founder's contribution or this model's loan share. No numerical coefficient below is presented as an empirical estimate.

## FIRM-P01 — Gate ordinary capital purchases by expected sellable output

**Priority 1; readiness: ready for a bounded specification.** The policy question is whether a loan-rate change, demand shock, or profit-tax change leads a viable firm to add useful productive capacity rather than buy capital solely because `price × marginal physical product` clears a threshold. Today `plan_capital_investment` tests a 10% smoothed margin, excludes survival/burn mode, values one more capital unit at its posted price times physical marginal product, compares it with depreciation plus a lending-rate term, and either pays one capital unit from cash or requests a loan (`backend/agents.py:4425–4474`). It does **not** test whether extra output has a plausible buyer. Self-financed capital is added immediately; a successful phase-1.5 loan also adds capital and debits the firm (`backend/economy.py:4958–4989`). Phase 8.5 credits the abstract capital purchase amount equally to households (`backend/economy.py:5371–5386`), so “equipment purchase” currently means a real productivity increment plus an aggregate income distribution, not a supplier order.

**Proposed decision:** keep the existing one-unit ceiling, margin gate, capital-cost rule, credit tests and realized tax/pricing paths. Before commitment, calculate the *incremental sellable units* from current expected sales, usable opening inventory and production already feasible with the planned workforce. Cap this by the capital unit's incremental output. Value only those incremental units at an expected contribution per unit based on the current posted price and known variable operating cost, then compare the bounded value with the existing weekly capital cost and scheduled debt service. A firm may use only its own lagged sales/lost-sales expectation and phase-1 workforce plan, not phase-6 realized customers or other firms' private books. Keep same-tick installation and existing numerical thresholds for this first slice; a delivery lag belongs to a separate W15 design because it changes both productive timing and the phase-8.5 income route. Simpler fallback: add only a positive expected-sales gap gate; that may suffice if a full contribution estimate proves noisy.

Illustrative, **uncalibrated** profiles: Firm A is a cash-rich, high-sell-through Food producer with low inventory and a persistent high `risk_tolerance`/fast expectation response; an expected shortfall makes one unit potentially sellable, so it can self-finance and produce more. Firm B is a conservative Food producer with two weeks of unsold stock and weak realized sales; even a low lending rate does not create customers, so it waits. A third firm has genuine lost sales but no free cash and fails the bank's reserve/credit gate; its desired investment stays unfunded. If workers cannot be hired, the phase-1 labor plan can also bind output and mute the capital response. Existing personalities still affect sales learning, pricing and hiring; this rule must not give `investment_propensity` an arbitrary new payoff merely to activate it.

**Transmission:** [existing] a lower bank base rate changes capital-cost estimates and possibly loan terms; [proposed] a firm with expected sellable demand chooses one unit; [existing] its cash or an actual bank/treasury loan funds the purchase, with lender reserves/treasury cash and repayment claims adjusted through current settlement; [existing] phase 8.5 gives households the abstract purchase proceeds; [hypothetical] higher future output lowers stockouts or prices, changes customer spending, revenue, taxable profit, worker demand and owner dividends. Competing effects include debt-service distress, a capital purchase displacing payroll, or a demand rebound that raises price. If consumer liquidity or awareness is absent, extra capacity may remain unsold. Profit-tax changes already alter `plan_pricing` and after-tax receipts; this gate must read resulting *existing* price and expectation state rather than add another direct profit-tax term.

**Interface:** firm-owned `expected_sales_units`, `inventory_units`, `capital_stock`, `cash_balance`, `price`, `pricing_operating_unit_cost`, workforce and debt service are read at phase 1; existing `needs_investment_loan`, `investment_loan_amount`, `capital_investment_this_tick`, cash and capital are the intended writers/readers in phases 1/1.5/8.5. Economy's phase-5 production and phase-8.5 recycle consume the output. No per-household scan, new trait or default new persistent state is needed; use one bounded arithmetic calculation per eligible firm per tick, O(F) time and O(1) added state per firm. If an installation lag is selected, one pending-unit scalar and its financing/delivery semantics need serialization and explicit owner. Audit diagnostics should distinguish *no expected buyer*, *labor bound*, *unaffordable*, and *loan denied* without changing the default warehouse schema unless a selected experiment needs durable event rows. Existing scalar firm and normal/performance paths use the same rule; household cached consumption still means the demand effect may arrive later in performance mode.

**Acceptance and falsification:** with fixed pre-policy firms and customer plans, a high-margin firm with ample unsold inventory must not invest only because posted price is high; the same firm with sustained bounded lost sales may invest if cash/credit and workers permit. A denied loan cannot add capital or distribute phase-8.5 proceeds. Check firm debit equals the household capital-recycle credit for successful purchases, bank/treasury principal and repayment stay with their actual funder, and production remains capacity-bound. Observe investment decisions, installed capital, sell-through, unmet demand, lender losses, firm survival and owner dividends at declared phases over several ticks; GDP as nominal sales is insufficient. A model where this gate suppresses genuinely profitable capacity even after repeated shortages would falsify the chosen sellability proxy. Roll back only the new investment gate if it does not distinguish overstock from shortage or exceeds the integrated cost budget; keep existing financing and accounting. No experiment has been run here.

## FIRM-P02 — Give entrant cash a funder and entrant profits an owner

**Priority 2; readiness: requires a modeling choice under W06.** The policy question is whether access to founder wealth versus bank/public credit changes entry, survival, jobs and taxable profits without silently manufacturing entrant purchasing power. At phase 13, `_maybe_create_new_firms` chooses a sector partly from unmet demand, rotates personalities by ID, assigns a CEO, and chooses bank, government-backed, direct-government, or “bootstrapped” funding (`backend/economy.py:4592–4837`). The loan branches record obligations and in W01–W02 repayments go to the actual funder; their fallback to bootstrapping and the bootstrapped branch simply set entrant `seed_cash` with **no debit to a founder or other counterparty** (`backend/economy.py:4725–4793`). The constructor supplies `ceo_household_id` but no `owners`. The large-economy factory assigns household owners to initial firms, including baseline firms (`backend/tools/runners/run_large_simulation.py:250–263`); `distribute_profits` pays only listed owners after a reserve test (`backend/agents.py:5693–5743`). Exit lays off staff and writes off registered bank loans, then removes the firm; residual cash, inventory and non-bank claims lack a fully specified settlement (`backend/economy.py:4510–4571`). These are source findings, not a measured estimate of money created by entry.

**Proposed minimal contract:** treat a bootstrapped entrant as a founder-funded equity contribution. Select an eligible existing household by a stable ID/tick keyed choice; require available *cash* above a declared subsistence reserve, debit exactly its contribution at phase 13, credit entrant opening cash once, and add that household ID to `owners`. If none can fund the minimum viable start, defer entry and keep unmet demand visible. Bank- and treasury-funded entrants receive only settled loan proceeds from the existing actual lender and must also have an explicit owner/controller (possibly a founder with zero monetary contribution); equity and debt are distinct claims. A failed credit offer may fall back only to an actually funded founder contribution, otherwise defer. Preserve preexisting factory opening endowments as labeled initial conditions and do not retroactively debit them. Before selection, choose whether one owner may control a loan-funded firm with no equity stake or whether a modest founder stake is mandatory; neither is an empirical conclusion of the US entry study. Housing's existing direct unit expansion and specialized construction/mortgage paths require separate W06/Housing coordination and are excluded from this first slice.

Illustrative, **uncalibrated** profiles: a liquid household can put model-currency 10,000 into an aggressive Services entrant, becoming its owner; its immediate consumption liquidity falls, while future dividends depend on profits. A low-cash household with the same sector signal cannot bootstrap; a bank-backed entrant can still appear if reserve, credit and sector-demand tests pass. A conservative entrant funded by a public loan faces the same real customer and staffing limits as the aggressive one, but its response speeds and payout behavior differ through existing readers. If no funder can advance the startup amount, target firm count does not authorize cash creation: entry is blocked. The illustrative 10,000 is not a calibrated requirement.

**Transmission:** [existing] a bank-credit or public-support policy changes available loan routes and terms; [proposed] eligible entry depends on a named founder payment or settled lender proceeds; [existing] the new firm competes for scarce workers/customers and later pays wages, taxes and bank/public installments; [proposed] only its recorded household owner receives an affordable residual dividend; [hypothetical] competition affects incumbents' prices, wages and future exits. Founder funding can cut consumption now; a public loan uses treasury cash/reserves and may crowd out other disbursements; default costs the actual lender. A fundable entrant can still fail to attract customers or workers. No automatic “entrepreneurship growth bonus” is proposed.

**Interface/closure:** `Economy._maybe_create_new_firms` owns phase-13 founder selection and the one cash transfer; `FirmAgent.owners`, `ceo_household_id`, `cash_balance` and existing loan fields record distinct claims. `BankAgent.originate_loan`/`issue_government_backed_loan` and the direct-government branch remain the financing writers; phase 9.5/10 repayment and phase 16 `distribute_profits` are readers. At exit, settle a bounded waterfall: pay only available residual cash against recorded due obligations to their actual funders once; record unpaid claims as loss/write-off; distribute any positive *cash* remainder to recorded owners, then remove the firm. Do not turn unsold inventory or abstract capital into money without a chosen liquidation rule; the simplest first rule is zero cash recovery on those assets, explicitly disclosed. Government loans and registered bank loans must not both collect the same claim. The banking/treasury writers and W06 integration owner must approve this common settlement contract before coding. No new RNG draw may use global state; ownership in the factory still requires caller-managed global RNG for matched runs. Reserve-checked founder lookup needs an indexed eligible set or one bounded pass over H at each attempted entry; do not scan H for every F. State is one to three owner IDs already supported, plus at most a bounded exit-event record. Added time is O(H) per entry candidate without an index, O(F+L) exit bookkeeping, not a per-tick H×F operation; measure actual entry frequency and memory. `FirmAgent.to_dict`, run evidence and optional firm snapshots must retain ownership/funding provenance if a selected policy comparison uses them; no new public lever is needed for the first repair.

**Acceptance:** a bootstrap debit and entrant credit must match exactly; bank/public loans must match their own reserves/treasury debit and claim, including fallback denial. An unfunded entrant has neither cash nor active ID. A profitable new firm with a recorded owner can pay a reserve-limited dividend; a CEO salary remains labor/management income, not proof of ownership. On failure, employment links, loans, treasury receipts and owner residuals reconcile once; creditor loss is not a second cash outflow. Compare founder-wealth and loan-access subgroups fixed **before** policy, plus entry/exit transitions separately. Observe funder balances immediately after phase 13, firm jobs and sales after later phases, and dividends after phase 16. A persistent entry boom without a funding debit would fail this mechanism. Rollback/exclusion: defer the entry rule until the exit waterfall and housing exception are agreed; do not ship only the founder debit while leaving an inconsistent residual-claim path.

## FIRM-P03 — Make private wage offers respond to vacancies without a benefit multiplier

**Priority 3; readiness: requires a modeling choice with Household/Government reviewers.** The policy question is whether changing unemployment benefits influences wages through workers' willingness to accept jobs and firms' hiring difficulty, rather than through an automatic private-firm wage floor. `FirmAgent.plan_wage` currently sets `floor_wage = max(policy_minimum_wage, 1.5 × unemployment_benefit)` and, at high unemployment, often returns that floor directly; a second branch repeats the benefit multiplier (`backend/agents.py:4907, 4977–4997, 5093`). `Economy.step` separately enforces the government's minimum wage on plans before matching (`backend/economy.py:1682–1690`). `HouseholdAgent.plan_labor_supply` takes the benefit into a downward market-anchoring target for some unemployed workers, despite a docstring saying it is unused; because the final value is also capped by the original reservation, this is not a general benefit-driven upward reservation rule (`backend/agents.py:1306–1407`). Failed reservation-wage matches and vacancy streaks already feed a limited wage raise (`backend/agents.py:4910–4966`). This is not a proposal to add another vacancy bonus.

**Proposed rule:** for ordinary private Food/Services offers, remove the benefit-based *firm* wage floor from both branches and keep the legal/configured minimum wage, actual vacancy/rejection reader, revenue/debt affordability ceiling and per-tick adjustment bounds. Benefit policy can still alter household cash, search/acceptance and thus *later* observed vacancy outcomes; any household reservation-rule revision is a separate decision. Do not change baseline Food/public fallback pay, Healthcare's dedicated wage/bonus/doctor pipeline, Housing's specialized constraints, or progressive wage-tax assessment. A simpler fallback is to keep the current formula but label the benefit-to-wage link an imposed institutional rule in comparisons; this is adequate only if that rule is deliberately part of the scenario.

Illustrative, **uncalibrated** profiles: a profitable Services firm with repeated applicant rejections and a healthy cash runway may raise its offer within existing caps; an indebted conservative Food firm with slow sales keeps or lowers its offer despite the same benefit change. A firm at the legal minimum cannot cut below it. A firm with no vacancies has no vacancy-mediated reason to raise pay when benefits change. Existing personality readers still vary adjustment speed and vacancy patience; matched constraints can make two profiles post the same legal floor.

**Transmission:** [existing] a funded benefit increase transfers treasury money to eligible unemployed households, raising their cash and possible goods demand; [proposed] no automatic contemporaneous firm wage multiplication; [existing] workers' plans and actual labor matching may change accepted offers or rejection counts; [existing] next-tick firm wage planning can react to observed rejections when customer demand and cash support hiring; [hypothetical] later wages, prices, labor tax receipts, profits and owner dividends change. Higher transfers can strain the treasury under the selected W12 fiscal closure; higher demand can raise firm revenue even with no labor-channel effect. If no vacancy opens, no applicant sees the offer, or a wage ceiling binds, the wage effect is blocked. Price and wage tax channels remain separate: the existing profit-tax pricing gross-up stays, and this proposal makes no new direct response to tax rates.

**Interface and verification:** `GovernmentAgent.get_unemployment_benefit_level` remains the phase-1/2 policy read; `FirmAgent.plan_wage` writes `wage_offer_next`, `Economy._run_labor_matching` reads it against household `reservation_wage`, phase 9 commits it, and continuing wages/phase-10 payroll follow current paths. No new state or trait is needed, O(F) arithmetic unchanged, and neither household scalar nor `_batch_plan_consumption` needs a new wage field. Normal/performance mode and five-tick consumption cache must be held fixed across arms. Use matched pre-policy firms/workers, actual applied policy boundary, identical exogenous disturbances, and distinguish posted offers, *actual paid wages*, failed vacancies, employment, unemployment-benefit payments, wage-tax assessment, firm profit and treasury balance at the post-settlement phase. A one-tick benefit rise with unchanged vacancies and legal minimum should not mechanically reprice all private offers; repeated demand-supported rejections should still permit bounded raises. Check a legal-minimum and a cash-constrained case. If offers become detached from observed hiring failure or frequently violate the existing affordability guard, revise the rule. Exclude this slice if W12 financing or the intended benefit-wage institution remains unresolved; do not infer real-world wage elasticities from simulator signs.

For every selected comparison, keep the same initialization, ownership draws, warmup, active agent capabilities, mode and definitions; intervene at a recorded safe boundary. Track pre-policy firm liquidity/debt, personality, sector and owner liquidity as fixed subgroups, then report entrants, exits and survivors as transitions rather than redefining the subgroup after policy. Report customer quantities, prices, paid wages, investment units, lender/treasury balances and failures alongside it. Phase-15 statistics precede phase-16 dividends, so owner cash is observed after finalization. The current source supports the problems and decision paths; it does not establish parameter magnitudes, external forecasts, or that these three changes are jointly feasible within the rules' **cumulative** 5% median/p95 tick-time target. Integrated checks should use interleaved matched 1,000- and 10,000-household runs, normal/performance modes, developed private firms, an uncertainty range and memory counts. If the timing interval crosses the budget, report inconclusive and simplify or measure more; no such run is claimed here.
