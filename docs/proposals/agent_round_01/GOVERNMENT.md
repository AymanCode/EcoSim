# Government proposals: fiscal closure and delivered public capacity

Status: proposed, 2026-09-21. Author/model: government role writer, GPT-5.6 Sol (high). Source: live-main commit `4f693890b5f133c2c6c1dcbec9b1ec91ced97b46`, with the W01–W05 working-tree snapshot recorded in `ROUND_MANIFEST.json`; relevant SHA-256 hashes are `backend/agents.py` `9d3d17ea01d35344df3fe693db3aa336540d530439ed7a7b2f5a010485ae73f1`, `backend/economy.py` `c0d95448181fd016ca4527be7f80897b5bb71f26432fffa6ccb83f5b83278092`, and `backend/policy_schema.py` `8126d06615aab24690b448a296a6b49fd444d7c1c6ac9aace2f9f00448f02dce`. Agent rules: v1.1. Assigned role: government. Related queue: W12 and W11, with W08/W14 dependencies. These are design proposals, not implemented or simulated results.

The government currently collects progressive wage taxes and several firm taxes, pays transfers, and can fund subsidies and investments. The missing comparison rule is what a change in receipts *buys*, and the missing delivery rule is how an authorized public outlay becomes a scarce service or productive asset. I propose declaring a small fiscal-use institution for each experiment and replacing one direct infrastructure gain with a funded, delayed, capacity-limited project. Both retain the existing agent roles, tax schedule, and weekly tick.

## GOV-P01 — Declare the revenue-use and financing rule before a tax comparison

**Priority:** first. **Readiness:** requires a modeling choice for the experiment's use of revenue; the initial cash-funded variant is ready for a bounded specification once that choice is made. This is W12, not a bracket redesign.

### Problem and source evidence

`GovernmentAgent.plan_taxes` applies a wage-rate scaler based on contemporaneous wage percentiles, including zero wages, to the *whole* wage (`backend/agents.py:6877–6920`). `Economy.step` takes household and firm snapshots in phase 7, plans transfers in phase 8, debits firm tax in phase 9 and household tax in phase 10, then credits treasury and debits transfers in phase 11 (`backend/economy.py:1911–1984`; `GovernmentAgent.apply_fiscal_results`, `backend/agents.py:7010–7044`). Baseline unemployment transfers can exceed the transfer budget; gap-filling is proportionally limited (`backend/agents.py:6802–6875`). The treasury can therefore go negative. In contrast, sector subsidies require positive cash and a per-tick cap (`backend/economy.py:1049–1079`; `backend/fiscal_guards.py:91–97`), infrastructure and technology require the whole budget in cash, and social spending is cash-limited (`backend/agents.py:7075–7168`). A surplus above a fixed reserve is partly routed as a nominal “bond purchase” into the miscellaneous pool (`backend/agents.py:7170–7210`; `backend/economy.py:2017–2030`). This creates no identified bond issuer, bond asset, liability, coupon or maturity in that path. The pool takes a random 0–20% tax and later pays up to 50 selected households, so the recipient of “public spending” is currently partly an unrelated beneficiary (`backend/economy.py:6031–6098`).

These are source findings, not measured economy-wide effects. Another source defect matters to *measuring* the experiment: `_build_firm_tax_snapshots` omits the `cash_balance` and housing-property fields read by `plan_taxes` (`backend/economy.py:4360–4409`; `backend/agents.py:6926–7002`). The phase-9 property debit and phase-11 treasury credit do exist; this is a snapshot producer/consumer mismatch, not proof that all profit taxation is inert. It belongs to the integration owner as a narrow source correction, separately from the fiscal-use model choice. The dashboard's negative-cash “debt” (`backend/server.py:2462–2463,2574`) is an observation convention, not evidence that a creditor and debt instrument have been created.

The [2008 stimulus-payment study by Parker and colleagues](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16684) used randomized disbursement timing and found that spending responded after payment; response varied by expenditure type. The setting supports distinguishing an actual rebate receipt from an announced tax change and allowing consumption responses to differ. It does **not** supply EcoSim's weekly marginal propensity or a general fiscal multiplier. The [IMF's analysis of public investment](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/001/2015/095/article-A001-en.xml) distinguishes debt-financed and budget-neutral investment and finds suggestive differences in its advanced-economy sample. It supports naming financing in comparisons; it does **not** prove one closure universally superior or validate EcoSim's monetary sector.

### Smallest useful rule

For the first controlled tax comparison, freeze an **ex ante revenue-use rule** in scenario configuration, rather than adding another freely changing government personality. The rule operates on realized tax receipts, so it does not need an unavailable online counterfactual. Define a bounded allocation share `u` in `[0,1]` and one named destination: `treasury_reserve`, `cash_rebate`, or `project_fund`. Apply it to *eligible receipts actually collected this tick*, after the phase-11 aggregate tax settlement. Existing contractual transfers, sector subsidies already paid, and restoration of negative treasury cash have first claim. A cash rebate or project authorization may spend only the positive, uncommitted remainder and is announced for delivery no earlier than the next tick. If collections miss expectations, scale the variable allocation down; never fabricate a transfer or output. The initial comparison should use `treasury_reserve` versus one alternative while holding rates and all other rules fixed, then vary the tax rate within a selected rule. `u=0` reproduces the no-earmark allocation choice; it does not silently erase existing automatic bond/pool behavior, which must be either explicitly retained in both arms or replaced and labeled as an additional intervention.

Illustrative, **uncalibrated** arithmetic: an extra 100 currency units collected in a week is not assumed to exist at the household's morning plan. Under a 50% cash-rebate allocation with 20 units of prior unpaid commitment, at most 40 units are available for next-tick rebates after the commitment and allocation rule. A reserve rule retains the comparable amount in treasury. A project rule can commit it but produces no physical benefit until GOV-P02's delivery gate. Exact priority between reducing a negative treasury balance and current entitlements is a modeling choice to publish; it must be identical across matched arms.

The government sees its current cash, prior revenue and spending, authorized obligations, trailing GDP and policy settings at the pre-plan boundary, but cannot know this tick's profit taxes before firms sell. It can set rates through the existing bounded policy schema (`backend/policy_schema.py:13–86`; `GovernmentAgent.set_lever`, `backend/agents.py:6460–6516`) on the runtime's safe boundary. This proposal adds a deterministic institutional allocation rule, not an invented “prudent” or “generous” personality. If the user later wants to change that rule during a run, it becomes a canonical schema action with fiscal guards; first experiments can freeze it in the manifest. Retain progressive wage taxation and publish assessed liability, collected revenue and rate separately. A simpler fallback is to leave all extra receipts in treasury and report them as such. That is sufficient for a pure tax-incidence experiment, but cannot answer whether taxes fund services or rebates.

**Institutional profiles:** a reserve institution retains available receipts and can keep next week's cash-funded subsidies from being denied; a rebate institution pays eligible households after the cash arrives, which may raise their later planned purchases. These are different explicit rules with the same private agents. Among households, a cash-poor unemployed person with little accessible deposit is more likely to spend a received rebate on essentials than a cash-rich employed owner; existing `spending_tendency`, cash, deposits, employment and obligations are the relevant persistent traits/state, without a new government-to-household response bonus. Both may buy nothing if their chosen supplier has no inventory, and a zero rebate because collections were absorbed by commitments mutes both responses. Current `_batch_plan_consumption` reads gross wages, benefit and last dividends, not explicit expected wage tax (`backend/economy.py:736–805`; rules K03), so an immediate anticipatory tax response is **hypothetical** and depends on W08.

**Causal chain:** tax rate → assessed and settled household/firm liability **existing** → treasury receipts **existing** → committed-cash allocation and next-tick rebate/project authorization **proposed** → household cash and consumption plan, or provider procurement, **proposed where the allocation differs** → supplier sales, labor, prices and later tax base **hypothetical conditional feedback**. Higher tax can also reduce household liquidity or firm cash/investment before any benefit arrives; supplier scarcity or household debt service can absorb the gross transfer. The bank is a counterpart only if a recipient deposits or withdraws through its existing paired liability path. GOV-P01 creates no new loan. A debt-financed variant is **excluded pending W14**: choosing whether a bank, bond holder or settlement authority lends, at what rate and with what money/deposit effects is an economic choice, not a software default. A negative treasury cash observation cannot be relabeled as newly issued debt.

### Interfaces, verification and cost

The integration owner would specify `eligible_receipts_this_tick` and `uncommitted_treasury_cash` (currency/tick flow and currency stock), the allocation policy (fixed scenario input), `rebate_due_next_tick` or `project_fund_cash` (currency stocks), and one settlement owner in `Economy.step`. `GovernmentAgent` owns the allocation decision; `Economy` owns actual household credits and counterpart debits. Reuse household ID lookup and existing batch income update; if paid after phase 10, the receipt must be visible to next-tick plans and must not be double counted as phase-10 transfer. Project funds are encumbered cash, not an extra treasury asset. A deficit memo, if displayed, is **not** an additional debt stock without a modeled creditor. Any replacement of the bond/pool route needs explicit compatibility mapping. Config, runtime policy normalization, LLM action mask/observations, frontend controls, warehouse policy context, forecasting frozen arms and serialization are consumers if the rule becomes a live lever; if frozen as scenario metadata, record it in the W03 manifest and metric definitions first. Existing performance mode caches consumption plans for four ticks, so policy receipt timing and cache invalidation must be declared in both arms.

Acceptance examples for later implementation: tax receipts credited once; rebate debits treasury and credits named households exactly once; no rebate when uncommitted cash is zero; project allocation stays a liability/encumbrance until spent; gross receipts, earmarks, unspent balance and actual service outlay reconcile without counting the same cash twice. Compare matched pre-policy cohorts, with cash-poor versus cash-rich membership frozen before intervention; separately report transitions. Observe receipts and obligations after phase 11, household cash/ledger after lifecycle close, and spending plans on the following tick. Hold initializer/global ownership RNG state, shock specification, seed, warmup, execution mode, policy timing, supplier population and metric definitions common (rules K07); a common seed alone is insufficient. An observed identical household budget immediately after a tax announcement may be correct under current planning, not a failure. A failed reconciliation or a rebate larger than available cash would falsify the mechanism; a preferred GDP sign is not a test.

This is O(1) treasury arithmetic plus O(H) only for an actual universal rebate, reusing existing household iteration and ID lookup; state is a handful of government scalars and, if rebates are queued, one due amount per eligible household or an aggregate deterministic rule. No runtime saving is claimed. Measure engine median/p95 tick time and memory in the selected integrated package at fixed active populations and modes; the shared 5% target is cumulative. Roll back the new allocation rule alone if cash accounting fails; keep the source-defect finding separately. No economic runs or benchmarks were performed for this proposal.

## GOV-P02 — Pay for a project, wait for delivery, then receive a bounded public benefit

**Priority:** second. **Readiness:** needs a bounded project/output and parameter evidence; do not implement a broad capital-goods sector first. This is a narrow W11 candidate, not all of W15.

### Problem and primary evidence

`GovernmentAgent.invest_in_infrastructure` subtracts a configured amount when cash is sufficient and immediately increases a global worker-productivity multiplier (`backend/agents.py:7075–7101`), which `Economy._calculate_experience_adjusted_production` later reads (`backend/economy.py:4411–4500`). Technology similarly increases a quality multiplier; social spending raises a happiness multiplier, with cash limits and decay (`backend/agents.py:7103–7168`). `Economy.step` routes these outlays to `_collect_misc_revenue` *after* that tick's miscellaneous distribution, so the money reaches its selected beneficiaries later; the multiplier and the payment recipient are not tied to a contractor, labor or asset (`backend/economy.py:1904–1909,1999–2030,6031–6098`). The `spending_efficiency` multiplier falls mechanically with a smoothed deficit/GDP ratio (`backend/economy.py:6459–6507`), but that shortcut does not establish a real project delay or financing channel. The current initial public-works firm is treasury-capitalized and authorizes jobs subject to an affordable-budget check (`backend/economy.py:5595–5666`); it is an existing alternative capacity path, not automatic proof that infrastructure is delivered.

The [IMF's 2015 public-investment-management study](https://www.imf.org/external/np/pp/eng/2015/061115.pdf) uses cross-country infrastructure and institutional data and identifies differences in how investment spending becomes useful assets. It supports a delivery gate and explicit institutional capacity, but its estimated efficiency gaps are not a weekly productivity coefficient. The [World Bank's public-investment-management framework](https://documents.worldbank.org/curated/en/461121468164052711/pdf/The-power-of-public-investment-management-transforming-resources-into-assets-for-growth.pdf) identifies delays, cost overruns, incomplete projects and maintenance as concrete failure modes. It does not establish the probability of any one of them in EcoSim. The [OECD study of publicly provided services](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/the-impact-of-publicly-provided-services-on-the-distribution-of-resources_5k9h363c5szq-en.html) shows that adding in-kind services changes measured household resources and distribution in its country sample. It does not prove that every household receives equal service or that service cost equals household welfare.

### Minimal project rule and contrasting cases

Pick **one** output for the first package: a shared service-capacity asset measured as weekly slots, with an explicit beneficiary rule, or a small shared productive-capacity index with a declared set of firms that can use it. Do not mix the two in a single effect parameter. My recommendation is a service-capacity project attached to an *existing* provider category, because completed slots, provider receipts and unmet requests can be observed without claiming an economy-wide multiplier. Healthcare is an attractive example only if its payer/provider contract is coordinated with the care owner; a generic services provider may be safer for the first slice. Keep existing infrastructure multiplier behavior as the labeled baseline arm and introduce delivery as an explicit alternative intervention; do not attribute the full baseline-versus-new difference only to “efficiency.”

At the phase-11 close, the government may authorize at most a configured number of concurrent projects (start with one), subject to GOV-P01's uncommitted cash and any preexisting obligations. Authorization records a planned currency cost and capacity target, not delivered output. On subsequent weekly ticks, an existing provider/contractor can accept funded work only up to its available labor and real input/service capacity. Each accepted installment debits the encumbered government cash and credits that firm as revenue, with normal worker wages and firm taxes; it does **not** pass through the miscellaneous beneficiary pool. Completion after a bounded delay adds slots to the named provider or provider group. Unfilled work remains pending, with a bounded expiry/refund or explicit cancellation rule; maintenance consumes an explicit future cash/service flow or the slots depreciate. No free productivity boost is awarded at authorization. The government knows its prior unmet-request counts, treasury cash, authorized commitments and published provider capacity. It does not see future applicant demand or future firm labor supply.

Illustrative, **uncalibrated** case: a clinic with two trained doctors and a financed extra room cannot serve more patients if doctors already bind the visit-capacity rule. A clinic with idle doctors but insufficient room capacity could gain slots after a room project completes. A service firm with cash and employees can accept a contract earlier than a struggling firm with vacancies, even under the same project rule. A low-cash household already requesting care may benefit only if it can pay its share or the separately funded coverage rule pays it; a high-cash household can use the added slot sooner if current queue/access rules permit. These are real differences in stocks, skills, requests, cash and contracts, not random beneficiary identities. If the project has no suitable supplier, no workers, no budget, or no eligible users, the benefit is blocked. If government procurement bids up a scarce worker's wage, private production may fall; even successful delivery can crowd out other orders. Such displacement is a hypothesis to test, not a guaranteed result.

**Chain:** tax/revenue-use choice and cash availability **existing plus GOV-P01 proposal** → project authorization, earmark and delayed contractor payment **proposed** → contractor hiring/production under existing labor and market limits **existing resolver, proposed contract demand** → completed service slots and household access **proposed** → later health/working ability, provider revenue and future tax base **hypothetical conditional feedback**. For a clinic example, current care queue, doctor/resident capacity, government subsidy cap, household deposits/medical loans, affordability rejection and provider revenue remain the specialized resolver (`backend/economy.py:6243–6385`). Public payment for a building is not the same as payment for visits. The caregiver, bank and household proposal owners would have to agree on that boundary. If the chosen output is generic services, define its actual request and clearing path just as explicitly; no “social spending” scalar should be renamed a service without delivery.

### Shared map, falsification and cost

`GovernmentAgent` would own policy objective and authorized ceiling; `Economy` would settle one project queue at a named phase. Minimal state: project ID, provider/sector ID, authorized cost and remaining encumbered cash (currency), weeks remaining, delivered slots (visits or service units/tick), expiry and maintenance schedule. The provider owns labor, capacity, accepted contract revenue and the resulting slot increase; households own request, queue/affordability and realized service state. Public-project obligations must be deducted from available cash in all fiscal guards and GOV-P01's revenue rule. Initial project parameters belong in frozen scenario configuration and the comparison manifest; a later live lever would need the canonical schema, runtime/LLM sanitizers and frontend control. Metrics must distinguish authorized, paid, unfinished and delivered amounts; current state needs snapshot/serialization coverage, while optional contract events belong in the warehouse. Avoid an independent O(H×F) search: use the existing provider index and at most a bounded candidate list per project. This requests a shared-rule revision to R03/M06 only if a new settlement phase or an explicit provider-contract transaction is approved; no writer should insert it unilaterally. It also needs M03 coordination if healthcare is selected and W06 accounting review if a provider exits with an unfinished contract.

The acceptance case must distinguish authorization, payment and output: with cash but no qualified worker, project cash stays encumbered and service slots stay flat; with a worker and funded installment, contractor cash/revenue rises by the exact government debit but slots rise only on completion; after an unfunded maintenance period, slots decline by the declared rule. A completed slot cannot exceed named provider capacity or produce more visits than the care/service resolver allows. In matched arms, freeze pre-intervention provider and household cohorts, report entrants/exits separately, and observe project cash after institutional close, provider staffing after labor resolution, completed slots after delivery, household access after market clearing and health/wellbeing on its normal cadence. Hold tax policy, insurance/visit subsidy, bank mode, performance mode and shocks fixed. Project speed, slot yield and maintenance cost need plausible ranges from a selected empirical setting; the cited studies justify the *possibility* of delay/efficiency differences, not numerical defaults. Failure to see a benefit when a different resource binds is not falsification; free slots before payment, unexplained cash creation, or unlimited care despite doctor constraints is.

Default state cost is O(P) for bounded active projects and O(1) per-provider slot fields; procurement candidate checks should reuse existing indexes, while optional event history belongs in the warehouse rather than per-tick in-memory objects. Measure the new phase and end-to-end median/p95 at fixed active counts, with normal and performance-mode cadence; do not treat the 5% cumulative package target as this idea's private allowance. If a measured integrated cost or evidence burden is too high, fall back to one aggregate delayed-capacity stock **with an explicit supplier payment and labor gate**. Exclude the project mechanism if that reduced model cannot identify a recipient and an affected scarce resource.
