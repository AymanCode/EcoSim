# Healthcare proposal: price, payer and scarce care

**Status:** independent proposal, 2026-09-21; no implementation selected. **Writer:** healthcare role, `gpt-5.6-sol` (high). **Baseline:** commit/live `origin/main` `4f693890b5f133c2c6c1dcbec9b1ec91ced97b46`, plus W01–W05 working-tree changes recorded in `ROUND_MANIFEST.json`; relevant working-tree SHA-256: `backend/economy.py` `c0d95448181fd016ca4527be7f80897b5b83278092`, `backend/agents.py` `9d3d17ea01d35344df3fe693db3aa336540d530439ed7a7b2f5a010485ae73f1`, `backend/config.py` `eb97bc817fe698ede3b4b0e796866a1bd3c7c853f5345218d42817646051ae1f`, `backend/fiscal_guards.py` `79b74b31308e72196fbcf6e48cc15c68005e7bc12e04bd42e773f1ce06c1576c`. **Rules:** v1.1, especially R03–R12, M02–M03, M05–M06, K02–K03, K05, K07–K08. **Queue:** W13; fiscal W12 and paired comparisons W10 are dependencies.

The smallest useful comparison keeps a visit, provider, worker and health response recognizable while changing who faces a charge and who pays the provider. A second change makes nonurgent requests react to the expected patient charge; a third makes access and capacity legible when queues grow. These are proposals about a synthetic economy, not clinical recommendations or calibrated forecasts.

## Shared source baseline and evidence limits

`HouseholdAgent.should_request_healthcare_service` (`backend/agents.py:1756–1800`) generates a need episode from health and a household-specific baseline chance, decrements pending visits and stages healing when it requests. It never reads a visit price, coverage or liquidity. The annual-visit-plan methods below it exist, but the live enqueue route uses `should_request_healthcare_service`, not those methods. Price can still affect **completed** visits through ability to pay, and treatment changes health and future episode probability. Those are indirect feedback and settlement effects, not direct demand-price sensitivity. `Economy._enqueue_healthcare_requests` (`backend/economy.py:6169–6194`) picks a provider using queue pressure and a small price term for noncritical patients, then records a single queue placement. Critical patients ignore that price term. Provider selection is not a decision whether to seek care.

`Economy._process_healthcare_services` (`backend/economy.py:6243–6424`) attempts trained-worker capacity, draws public subsidy from `apply_sector_subsidy_payment` (`:1049–1086`), then tries patient cash, up to 90% of accessible deposits, and medical credit. On failure, it refunds any reserved subsidy, removes the person from the queue and records an affordability denial; the person may later re-request. On success the provider earns the full posted price and the household heals. A positive healthcare-targeted sector subsidy share takes precedence; otherwise configured `healthcare_visit_subsidy_share` applies, default zero (`backend/config.py:668`). Every actual public payment is capped by both remaining treasury cash and the shared sector envelope, initialized using `get_sector_subsidy_cap` (`backend/fiscal_guards.py:91–98`: minimum of 5% trailing GDP and 2% current cash). The default therefore is no **direct visit subsidy**, not proof that everyone self-finances: deposits and credit can pay. Bank medical loans and treasury-funded loans registered with the bank are collected through `_collect_bank_loan_repayments` (`backend/economy.py:6550–6601`); `_issue_medical_loan` (`:6735–6794`) also has a no-bank fallback whose funder/repayment counterparty must be resolved before making it part of a new regime. W01–W02 protect the registered actual-funder, single-service path.

`_prioritize_healthcare_queue` (`backend/economy.py:6196–6217`) moves sick **doctors** ahead of others, not all clinically urgent patients. The subsequent scan rotates when no priority doctor leads (`:6291–6313`), so it is not simple FIFO either. `_healthcare_effective_capacity` (`:6219–6241`) sums employed doctors' and residents' visit capacities but falls back to headcount times nominal capacity if the sum is zero, including a known worker with no clinical qualification; this is a real-capacity ambiguity. Ordinary healthcare hiring is disabled in `Economy.step` (`:1665–1668`), while `FirmAgent._plan_healthcare_service_labor` (`backend/agents.py:3393–3470`) has backlog-based desired staffing. `HouseholdAgent.start_medical_training`, `update_medical_training_progress`, and `medical_visit_capacity` (`:1878–1920`) define student/resident/doctor states and a four-year default training interval, but a repository call-site search finds no live callers for the first two methods. The large-economy factory seeds doctors directly (`backend/tools/runners/run_large_simulation.py:208–255`). Do not describe that as a functioning endogenous pipeline.

Provider pricing already reacts to wage cost and queue overload (`FirmAgent._healthcare_labor_price_targets`, `plan_pricing`, `backend/agents.py:3135–3165,4611–4644`); revenue affects provider cash/profit and a later worker bonus (`:5747–5800`). Health affects work eligibility below 0.10 (`HouseholdAgent.can_work`, `:1219–1231`), service-worker productivity (`Economy._calculate_experience_adjusted_production`, `backend/economy.py:4411–4463`) and wellbeing (`_batch_update_wellbeing`, `:4115–4270`). The magnitude of any economy-wide labor effect remains hypothetical; the Oregon randomized Medicaid study found more care and less medical debt but **no statistically significant employment/earnings effect** in its setting ([utilization and debt](https://www.nber.org/papers/w17190), [labor result](https://www.nber.org/papers/w19547)). It cannot validate a synthetic health-to-output coefficient.

Primary evidence supports the directions worth modeling, with limits. The [RAND randomized insurance experiment](https://hcp.hms.harvard.edu/publication/health-insurance-and-demand-medical-care-results-randomized-experiment) studied varying cost sharing in US fee-for-service coverage and found utilization response; it does not supply a universal elasticity for this model or distinguish our single visit type. The [Oregon insurance lottery](https://www.nber.org/papers/w17190) found higher use and less financial strain for low-income adults in early coverage, not a guarantee that broader public payment improves all health or employment outcomes. The [WHO UHC monitoring framework](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/tracking-universal-health-coverage) measures service coverage and financial hardship separately; coverage/payment and delivered access should therefore be observed separately here. The [OECD waiting-times review](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/waiting-times-for-health-services_242e3c8c-en.html) links queues to demand and treatment capacity; the [WHO workforce strategy report](https://apps.who.int/gb/ebwha/pdf_files/EB156/B156_15-en.pdf) documents training and workforce constraints. Neither provides an EcoSim visit capacity or training parameter.

## CARE-P01 — Charge-sensitive nonurgent requests

**Priority 1; readiness: ready for a bounded specification after the care episode convention is chosen.** Policy question: with the same latent health needs, does a zero patient charge increase attempted care, reduce affordability denials and move pressure into waiting? Current source finding is the price-blind request above; the behavioral response below is a model hypothesis informed by RAND and Oregon, not an executed result.

At pre-plan request time, an eligible household observes current health, its own cash, accessible deposit allowance, existing medical debt, prior/current posted provider prices and **announced** coverage terms. It cannot see future queue openings, tax receipts or whether a shared public cap will survive preceding patients. Separate latent episode generation from the choice to request the next due visit. For health below the existing critical threshold, always request; for other episodes, use a bounded monotone request probability that falls with *expected patient price relative to accessible near-term resources*, with a fixed calibrated-later slope. Existing `price_sensitivity` may scale the slope if the coordinator accepts that its cross-sector meaning applies to care; otherwise use no new trait initially and let cash, deposits, health and posted prices generate differences. A price of zero removes the price term, not the health-based episode trigger. Retain an unrequested due visit for a bounded retry window rather than consuming or healing it; record deferral separately from a later queue denial. Do not predict the actual public disbursement from a future cap. If the announced contract cannot guarantee the payer share, show the potential patient residual or funding-wait rule before the decision.

Illustrative **uncalibrated** profiles at one 30-currency visit: A has health 0.55, cash 3, accessible deposits 0, existing medical debt and high price sensitivity; B has health 0.55, cash 300, accessible deposits 90 and low sensitivity. A may defer a patient-paid noncritical visit while B requests. With a covered zero-charge visit both may request. If both have health below their critical thresholds, urgency mutes the price response; if every doctor slot is occupied, extra requests increase queue depth but not completions. A provider with a shorter queue but higher price may draw fewer noncritical patients in the paid arm; a low-price provider with qualified staff may fill slots. Identical patients can still face different providers because `Economy._choose_healthcare_provider` already weighs queue and price.

Chain: **proposed** lower announced patient charge → more noncritical requests; **existing** single-provider queue and fixed current trained slots → more waiting or completed visits, depending on slack; **existing** completed visits debit patient/payer, credit provider and heal the household; **existing** later health may change future requests, labor eligibility, productivity and consumption resources; **hypothetical** firms elsewhere may gain output from healthier employees or lose sales when taxes finance visits. Higher volume can instead crowd out urgent patients absent CARE-P03, raise provider queue-based posted prices, or exhaust government funds. No assumed GDP gain.

**Interface:** household owner writes existing `pending_healthcare_visits`, `pending_visit_heal_delta`, `next_healthcare_request_tick` and one bounded deferred-due indicator/reason at pre-plan; the care resolver alone writes queue ID/enter tick and completions. It reads public quote/coverage terms and existing household `health`, `cash_balance`, `bank_deposit`, `medical_loan_remaining`, `price_sensitivity`. A denial must not create a second due slot or a second heal. Keep request checks O(H), provider selection at present O(H × care providers); do not add a provider sweep to every household beyond actual due requests. There is no healthcare request path in `_batch_plan_consumption`, but review scalar `HouseholdAgent` planning, performance-mode cached consumption and `Economy.step` to keep policy observation timing consistent; wellbeing may update only every tenth tick in performance mode.

**Acceptance:** paired same pre-policy episode/trait states; changing only covered charge changes some noncritical requests, never the latent illness count; critical requests stay; a queued household is not duplicated; deferral and inability-to-pay denial remain distinct. Compare requests per due episode, deferred episodes, cash/medical debt and completed visits, fixing subgroup membership by pre-policy cash/deposit/health bands. Observe requests before phase 6.8 and health/cash after completion/close; report both normal and performance modes separately. A flat or reversed conditional price response under slack capacity would falsify this chosen rule; external elasticity still needs calibration. Roll back the new response if it dominates care by arbitrary slope or violates episode accounting; simpler fallback is keep price-blind requests and compare affordability only, sufficient if the question is strictly payer settlement rather than care-seeking.

## CARE-P02 — Explicit visit payer contract with budget exhaustion

**Priority 1; readiness: requires a financing/coverage choice.** Compare two explicit scenarios for the **same covered basic visit** and posted provider price: (A) patient pays the full bill, with existing deposits and actual-funder medical credit available; (B) public payer pays the full bill at point of use, so the patient owes zero **for an authorized visit**. Keep provider ownership/pricing and trained slots initially common; call B public purchasing, not an assumed public-provider system. Partial cost sharing is a later sensitivity case, not a third hidden institution. Unlike the current cash/cap-limited subsidy, B promises a zero charge only when funding has been authorized; if the appropriation is exhausted, the person waits in a distinct **funding queue** or receives an explicit uncovered-service notice under a chosen eligibility rule. Never silently turn a covered bill into surprise patient debt. A patient with cash 2 facing a 30-currency price is denied in A if deposits/credit fail; with a funded B claim and an open slot, the public payer remits 30 to the firm and no medical debt arises. If no slot exists, both wait even when B is fully funded.

At each tick boundary a declared healthcare appropriation (currency/tick) is carved from available treasury cash or a separately specified financed claim; at the visit resolver, reserve the whole price before treatment, settle once on completion, and release a reservation on failed/no service. The existing `apply_sector_subsidy_payment` cap is shared across sectors and cash-limited; it **cannot** alone implement a guaranteed unlimited B entitlement. The bounded first slice should instead retain its cap and state plainly that B is *free for funded visits*, with unmet need classified as funding-limited. A true entitlement through cap exhaustion requires a separate W12 government financing instrument and liability, with its payer and repayment specified before implementation. For a controlled first comparison, fix an announced appropriation rule and make its opportunity cost explicit: reallocate the same currency from another named discretionary outlay, or raise declared progressive-tax parameters, but do not blend both in an effect attributed to coverage alone. Which closure is chosen is an open coordinator/government decision. Provider receipts must remain exactly the posted visit price; a tariff change would be a second intervention. Existing profit tax, wage costs, retained profit and worker bonuses then respond endogenously; under low funds, providers may lose revenue and later capacity incentives.

The counterparty ledger is patient cash/deposit or correctly funded loan → patient → provider in A; treasury appropriation/reserve → provider in B. A loan is a financing claim, never another subsidy: registered bank and government-backed medical loans keep W01–W02 one collection path, principal and interest recognized to the actual funder. No-bank fallback must either gain a specified creditor/receivable or be excluded from this comparison. Public spending enters the treasury's fiscal flow once; any signed negative treasury balance is identified as an unresolved financing gap, not silently named public debt. Cap exhaustion is not physical-slot exhaustion, and queue wait due to the latter is not an affordability refusal. The contrast among a low-cash indebted patient, a high-cash patient, and a full-capacity provider tests who benefits and which response is blocked.

**Interface:** government owns announced appropriation/reserved/paid/unused currency per tick and its financing closure; care resolver owns per-visit payer status, price, subsidy claim and completion; firm sales receive one full-price revenue entry; household health/spend/debt reflect only realized visit. Existing `last_tick_gov_subsidies`, treasury cash, per-firm sales and event rows need reconciled extensions or a separate nonduplicating care-purchase account. `GovernmentAgent.set_lever`, `backend/policy_schema.py`, config/defaults, server mapping, run manifest, dashboard and warehouse adapters must be mapped by integration owner if a new policy lever or durable metric is selected; no field should be merely displayed while inactive. If B uses the existing sector lever, record its precedence and the cap limitation in every comparison. Reserve before patient settlement; do not allow future phase-7 tax revenue to fund a phase-6.8 visit without an explicit advance or prior treasury balance.

**Acceptance:** at a posted 30-currency price and one open slot, A pays 30 from cash/deposits or a single actual-funder loan while B pays 30 from authorized public funds, with identical provider receipt and one heal; zero appropriation yields no B claim and a funding-limited outcome; zero trained slots yields no visit regardless of payer. Government paid + patient paid = firm visit revenue for each completed visit; reservations plus paid never exceed appropriation; compare government spend, medical debt, affordability denial, funding denial and waiting separately. Report provider revenue/margins, doctor wage/bonus, other discretionary spending or progressive-tax incidence, household consumption and health at defined post-close phases; GDP is only nominal firm sales. A matching failure or unintended double subsidy falsifies the settlement specification. Simpler fallback: label the present subsidy as a **capped cost-share experiment**, not free-at-point-use, if fiscal closure is not selected. This proposal requests R03/M03/M06 shared interface selection, not a unilateral rules change.

## CARE-P03 — Qualified capacity and broad clinical priority

**Priority 2; readiness: capacity correction ready for specification, triage and training need modeling choices.** Coverage expansions can raise requests faster than qualified supply. NHS England's [elective access policy](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/national-elective-access-policy/) uses clinician-assigned clinical priority on waiting lists; that supports modeling clinical urgency separately from arrival order, but its specialty codes and waiting targets do not map to this one-visit simulator. The OECD waiting review supports tracking delay and capacity, not an assumption that every public system waits longer.

First, count only employed doctors and residents through `medical_visit_capacity`; unknown or unqualified employee IDs supply zero visits and flag an invariant, while a test-only scaffold may explicitly opt into nominal fallback. Keep fractional carryover, provider price, idle/backlog signals and non-storable visits. Second, rank queued patients by a coarse **current** health-urgency band using existing `healthcare_critical_threshold` and `healthcare_urgency_threshold`, then wait age within a band and stable household ID for ties. Re-evaluate band each tick; do not award doctors unconditional priority based solely on occupation. If preserving the current sick-doctor exception is desired for workforce continuity, state it as a separate policy and measure displaced patient wait, since it is not general triage. This is an explicit requested revision to M03's *current behavior*, with common triage across A/B comparison arms. Reserve a bounded anti-starvation escalation after a declared wait only if evidence or experiment question warrants it; no unbounded full sort history.

Third, if expanding long-run capacity is selected, wire existing student → resident → doctor state transitions into a bounded annual enrollment rule with named training seats, wages/tuition payer and vacancy budget. The current training methods alone do not create staffed visits; students leave other employment, residents have limited capacity, and doctors appear only after the configured delay and actual healthcare placement. A public seat or scholarship must debit a stated payer; medical-school debt needs an actual creditor. The cheapest initial slice can **omit endogenous enrollment** and hold the seeded qualified workforce fixed while measuring how access policy affects queues. This is preferable to instantly adding nominal employees in response to demand.

Profiles: a critical low-cash patient may receive priority but still lack funding in A; a noncritical wealthy patient may pay but wait behind urgency; a clinic with two qualified doctors supplies visits while a clinic with two untrained employees supplies none. When both clinics are full, a payer change cannot increase completed visits this tick. Chain: **proposed** coverage/charge alters requests; **existing** backlog affects provider pricing and desired staffing; **proposed** qualified capacity and priority allocate scarce slots; **existing** provider receipts/payroll and patient healing follow; **hypothetical** training supply, wages, opportunity cost in other firms and later tax revenue feed back only after seat funding and years of lag. Training could draw skilled workers away from other production or raise public cost before capacity arrives.

**Interface and checks:** firm roster and household `medical_training_status`, `skills_level`, capacity cap and training start tick feed care capacity; care resolver writes service outcomes and queue wait; household training transition and labor roster owner must reconcile student departure, resident placement and doctor promotion. New seats/funding state, if chosen, has integer units and explicit budget; reuse existing queue IDs/enter ticks. At most O(queue length) classification per provider per tick; a stable bounded-band pass can avoid O(Q log Q) sorting. One status transition scan is O(H) per tick if naively run, so prefer only active trainees O(T) and annual enrollment O(H) only on enrollment ticks; memory O(T) indexes plus small per-provider counters. Verify no visits from unqualified roster entries, no duplicate employee/queue IDs, every completed visit within qualified capacity, and worse-health bands normally wait no longer under equal funding; measure displacement and starvation. Compare initial rosters and training status before policy, report requests, qualified slots, completions, queue age, funding/affordability denial, and health at phase 6.8 and after wellbeing. Clinical priority and workforce dynamics are hypotheses, not clinical validity. Roll back triage if starvation/displacement dominates without a defensible rule; omit training from the first package if its creditor, seat funding or enrollment criterion remains unsettled.

## Integration, observation and cost gate

All three ideas preserve a weekly synchronous tick and the provider's existing economic role. They require a **shared specification** for (1) due episode versus request versus service accounting, (2) the payer claim and treasury closure, and (3) whether sick-doctor priority is retained. No role writer can independently change `Economy.step`, bank contracts, government balance semantics, policy schema or metrics. This is a request under R03/R04/R05/R12, not a claim that rules v1.1 already select those institutions. W12/government decides fiscal closure; W01–W02/bank conventions determine medical-debt routing; household cash/consumption and firm wage/price plans are read dependencies. Preserve progressive taxation.

Predeclare paired scenarios with identical source snapshot, initial traits, doctor rosters, provider prices, treasury/bank balances, random streams and exogenous shocks through the intervention boundary; record actual applied coverage, fiscal closure, stabilizer state and normal/performance mode. Factory owner allocation uses global Python randomness, so config seed alone is insufficient; audit pairing before causal within-model claims. The request rule's keyed draw and later price/amount-dependent randomness may diverge endogenously, which is an outcome to report rather than conceal. Define subgroups **before** policy by cash plus accessible deposits, debt, health urgency and provider qualified-capacity/queue ratio; track transitions separately. Report attempts, nonrequests, funding refusals, affordability removals, physical waits, completed visits, waits by priority, public and patient outlays, provider receipts/margins, worker payments, medical debt and health. Measure at request, phase-6.8 service, fiscal close and tick-end health; avoid treating nominal sales GDP as welfare or claiming external health/economic calibration.

Expected incremental default work is O(H + Q + T) for one request pass, bounded queue partition and active-trainee transitions; provider selection remains O(due requests × care providers), and an optional cached provider quote would help only if profiling shows that term matters. New persistent state is a bounded per-household deferred-due flag/age at most, per-provider priority/funding counters, plus O(T) trainee index only if enrollment is selected. Optional event detail must not grow unbounded in memory. Measure median/p95 engine tick time and memory at matched 1,000 and 10,000 households, normal and performance modes, with the **integrated** package's cumulative 5% target and uncertainty method from rules v1.1; no performance result is claimed here. The narrowest first selection is CARE-P01 plus a capped, explicitly labeled CARE-P02 pilot with fixed qualified capacity; CARE-P03's qualification correction and observations should accompany it, while endogenous training remains a later choice.
