# Household proposals: spend from expected take-home resources, protect commitments, compare jobs on net gains

**Status:** independent proposal, 2026-09-21. **Author/model:** household reviewer, `gpt-5.6-sol` (high). **Source:** verified `origin/main`/HEAD `4f693890b5f133c2c6c1dcbec9b1ec91ced97b46`, plus the round's W01–W05 working tree. Relevant manifest SHA-256: `backend/agents.py` `9d3d17ea01d35344df3fe693db3aa336540d530439ed7a7b2f5a010485ae73f1`; `backend/economy.py` `c0d95448181fd016ca4527be7f80897b5bb71f26432fffa6ccb83f5b83278092`; `backend/config.py` `eb97bc817fe698ede3b4b0e796866a1bd3c7c853f5345218d42817646051ae1f`. **Rules:** v1.1. **Role:** household plans and responses; shared settlement, tax assessment, credit, rent and market allocation belong to integration/counterparty owners. **Queue:** W08 chiefly, with W07/W10/W12 dependencies. No engine, experiment or benchmark was run for this document.

The useful first correction is for households to budget from an estimate of money they can keep, rather than their full stated wage. A second, narrower correction is to account for rent and scheduled debt payments before treating cash as free to spend. A labor response should be considered only after those two interfaces are clear: a worker can compare the expected net gain from an available job without inventing hours of work or rewriting tax brackets. These are decision rules, not a calibrated forecast of tax policy.

## HH-P01 — Use expected take-home income in the consumption plan

**Priority 1; readiness: ready for a bounded specification after the shared tax-information interface is chosen.** Applicable R03–R12, M01/M05/M06, K03/K05/K07/K08. The policy question is whether a wage-tax or benefit change alters *desired* spending this week, before the resulting end-of-tick cash change has accumulated. This is distinct from whether supply or liquid balances allow the desired purchase.

**Current source finding.** [`Economy._batch_plan_consumption`](../../../backend/economy.py) (lines 736–865) uses the current `household.wage` if employed, configured unemployment benefit otherwise, previous dividend income, source-specific MPCs, a bounded `spending_tendency/frugality` multiplier, a `savings_drawdown_rate` share of cash plus 90% of deposits, and an accessible-liquidity cap. It has no explicit tax input. [`HouseholdAgent.plan_consumption`](../../../backend/agents.py) (1536–1640), the scalar API, also uses gross wage and benefit, but its savings adjustment calls `compute_saving_rate` rather than using the batch path's drawdown-rate array. Neither existing comment claiming realistic MPCs nor claimed scalar/batch equivalence is empirical validation. At phase 7, [`Economy._build_household_tax_snapshots`](../../../backend/economy.py) (4344–4358) provides current wages to [`GovernmentAgent.plan_taxes`](../../../backend/agents.py) (6883–6924), which applies whole-wage rates varying by contemporaneous wage percentiles, including zero wages. [`Economy._batch_apply_household_updates`](../../../backend/economy.py) (1190–1222) then adds wages/transfers and deducts assessed wage tax in phase 10. Cash and deposits therefore can transmit taxes in later plans, but the phase-2 budget does not anticipate them. Actual phase-8 transfers can differ from the advertised benefit: the transfer planner also considers unemployment and a cash-gap top-up. No claim of zero current tax response follows.

**Economic evidence and limit.** Kaplan, Violante and Weidner's [*The Wealthy Hand-to-Mouth*](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20073) documents households with little liquid wealth despite illiquid assets across eight countries and finds evidence of a high response to temporary income changes in US PSID data. That supports separating spendable liquidity from ownership; it does not calibrate this simulator's MPCs, bank withdrawal fraction or weekly timing. Ganong and Noel's [US bank-account study of spending during unemployment](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25417) finds a sharp spending fall when predictable UI benefits expire; its evidence also challenges a pure rational liquidity-constraint account. It supports a bounded current-income response, not treating all households as identical or importing its welfare ratios here.

**Chosen simplification.** At phase 2, compute one **expected net ordinary inflow in model currency per tick**: current employed wage minus expected wage withholding, or the publicly announced baseline unemployment payment if unemployed, plus a bounded lagged owner-income expectation. Keep existing MPCs and per-household preferences; substitute *net wage* for gross wage in the wage component only. This inflow is a **budget target input, never a credit to opening cash**. Rent and registered bank repayments can be due before phase-10 wages arrive; goods plans remain capped by opening cash and a conservative deposit-withdrawal estimate, then by actual accessible cash when payments resolve. The current phase-2a loan originates *after* the plan: its disbursement must not be included in that plan without an explicit post-origination replan. Do not add the income estimate to that liquidity cap, count a deposit twice, or spend a prospective cash-gap transfer, unapproved loan, future medical reimbursement or this tick's as-yet-unknown dividend. A household knows its own last assessed wage tax/last wage receipt and the announced headline wage-tax rate; it does not know this tick's population percentile or future matching result. One workable estimator is its lagged effective withholding share, capped to a declared valid interval, adjusted proportionally when the announced headline rate changes; when the old rate is zero, use the new headline as a coarse prior. The estimator is a model choice to test, not an empirical formula. Persist only a bounded lagged withholding belief (fraction) if existing last income/tax ledger values cannot be read safely at planning; update it **once after phase-10 settlement**. New workers use the public-rate prior. The coordinator must choose whether policy announcements affect beliefs immediately or after one realized paycheck and keep that rule common across arms. Actual assessed tax and treasury revenue remain owned by the current government paths. This proposal neither redesigns progressive brackets nor presumes their relative-percentile rates are exact household knowledge.

Uncalibrated example: two employed people each receive a gross wage of 100 per week. One with an expected 10 tax, 20 cash and no deposit has at most 20 accessible liquidity under the current planning cap; the new 90 net-wage estimate changes desired spending but may not change purchases. The other expects 25 tax and holds 300 accessible cash; the lower net-wage input changes its desired discretionary budget more visibly. Their different existing `frugality`, `spending_tendency`, `savings_drawdown_rate`, category weights, prices and awareness sets still affect both amount and seller choice. If all preferred suppliers have no stock, both may buy zero despite different plans. For an unemployed recipient, a benefit change can affect the existing benefit component, but actual gap transfers and treasury closure must be reported separately.

**Transmission, with status.** **Existing:** a validated policy changes the government's tax headline at a safe boundary; government assesses wage tax at phase 7 and settles household debit/treasury credit at phases 10–11. **Proposed:** a phase-2 household lowers/raises its wage-spending plan based on expected withholding; the existing goods resolver allocates available food/services among submitted firm targets. **Existing:** sellers receive sales, update cash/profit and later output/wage decisions; household receipts/beliefs and later cash affect future plans. **Hypothetical:** a tax cut may raise sales or, if supply is fixed, redistribute scarce goods/raise future prices. A tax increase may also finance a compensating transfer or service, depending on the *declared W12 fiscal closure*; no benefit is presumed. A transfer or treasury overdraft cannot be silently described as financed borrowing. Firm wage responses and changes in household tax rank are separate later feedback, not inputs known at phase 2.

**Interfaces and acceptance.** Household writer/reader: lagged tax-belief fraction (optional), existing wage/cash/deposit/dividend/trait fields; phase-2 planner reads and phase-10 settlement updates. Government writer: actual `wage_taxes` and published headline; household planner reads only announced/lagged information. Integration owner passes identical expected-net-resource input to scalar and batch paths, accounts for performance mode's four intervening cached plans, and checks the current cache invalidation when a policy takes effect. The simpler phase-2a-credit convention is to let new loan cash affect **later plans only**; same-tick credit-funded demand requires an expressly coordinated plan refresh after disbursement, followed by normal market/settlement checks. No new UI policy lever is needed. A new optional diagnostic can record expected gross, expected withholding, expected net and planned budget; default warehouse household snapshots currently have wages and last transfers/dividends but no tax-belief/deposit fields ([`HouseholdSnapshot`](../../../backend/data/models.py), 119–140; [`SimulationManager._build_household_snapshot_rows`](../../../backend/server.py), 1244–1275). If exposed persistently, the integration owner must version schema, adapters and readers; sampled snapshots alone cannot prove a phase-2 decision.

Acceptance examples should show (i) identical cash, stock and profile with higher *announced* withholding yielding lower wage-funded desired budget after its declared information delay, while settled tax still equals the government's plan exactly once; (ii) a zero-cash case where desired budgets differ but purchases converge; (iii) unchanged tax/benefit behavior before the intervention and scalar/batch agreement on the common budget input; (iv) cache behavior at the first effective policy tick in both normal and performance modes. Falsifiers include a second tax debit, spending growth from a tax increase with all other inputs fixed and no modeled income effect explaining it, or a measured response driven wholly by changed initial profiles. Compare paired pre-policy profiles and intended external shocks, not only seeds (K07); report planned budget at phase 2, allocated units at phase 6, settled cash after phase 11, and sales at the same phase in both arms. Fix the pre-policy cohorts by employment, tax-belief range and *cash plus accessible deposits*; report job and liquidity transitions separately. Roll back the tax-belief path if it cannot be made consistent across both planners/caches; the simpler fallback is to use last settled net ordinary income for next week's plan, accepting a one-week policy-recognition delay.

## HH-P02 — Reserve known rent and debt obligations when deciding discretionary purchases

**Priority 2; readiness: requires a shared settlement choice.** Applicable R03–R12, M01/M04/M05, K02/K03/K05/K07. The policy question is whether a household that appears to have cash for goods actually has cash left after committed payments, and whether a support or credit change reaches firms rather than being absorbed by bills.

**Current source finding.** The batch planner counts cash plus 90% of bank deposits and caps its goods budget there (`_batch_plan_consumption`, 850–865). [`_withdraw_deposits_for_planned_consumption`](../../../backend/economy.py) (3736–3769) attempts an actual bank withdrawal only for planned spend above cash. Rent has a specialized phase-6.5 affordability check and payment from household to housing firm ([`_clear_housing_rental_market`](../../../backend/economy.py), 5777–5945). Registered bank-loan installments collect in phase 9.5 **before** household wages settle ([`_collect_bank_loan_repayments`](../../../backend/economy.py), 6460–6598); fallback medical payment and ordinary goods debits occur in phase 10. [`_process_bank_deposits`](../../../backend/economy.py) (6600–6679) keeps a personality-derived cash buffer based on *past consumption*, minimum wage and 50, but only after the payments. Current planning therefore does not reserve rent, installments or scheduled care cost. Goods are allocated and firm sales recorded before the final goods cash debit, which may even produce negative household cash after earlier payments; this is a cross-market settlement issue, not something a household preference alone can fix.

**Economic evidence and limit.** Kaplan et al.'s [liquid versus illiquid wealth evidence](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20073) supports treating cash and transferable deposits differently from housing claims; it does not specify which EcoSim obligations should have legal priority. Ganong and Noel's [UI-exhaustion evidence](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25417) supports tight cash-flow sensitivity but cannot establish the order of EcoSim rent, medical and loan payments. The payment-order rule is therefore an **institutional modeling choice**, with more than one defensible convention.

**Chosen simplification.** Before goods planning, calculate **committed cash this tick** from existing active-rental rent and the scheduled installment on each *registered* household loan, in model currency per tick. Deduct it once from **opening cash plus conservatively estimated withdrawable deposits** when capping the discretionary goods plan; use `max(0, ...)` for a planning capacity, while preserving signed actual cash/distress. At phase 5b and each specialized payment, use only the *actual* amount withdrawn; an inaccessible deposit cannot finance an obligation. The HH-P01 expected-net-income estimate shapes desired spending but is not available to pay rent at phase 6.5 or bank installments at phase 9.5; phase-10 wages cannot be borrowed from time without an explicit credit contract or coordinated timing change. Do not count the face value of a loan as income, reserve principal plus the full remaining loan, or charge a bank-registered medical loan again through the fallback. Do not reserve a speculative future care visit; when a visit is actually offered, the healthcare resolver keeps its existing price, subsidy and capacity checks. A proposed later extension could reserve a confirmed appointment's patient share, but needs the care owner to define when it becomes a commitment. Deposits enter only to the extent the bank can actually release them under current withdrawal rules, and any requested withdrawal must use `BankAgent.withdraw` with corresponding household and bank-liability entries. Existing `saving_tendency`, deposit buffer, debt histories, rent status and medical state generate real differences without a new fixed “prudence” trait.

Uncalibrated example: two households each have 90 cash and expect 60 net income. One owes 40 rent and 15 registered debt service; the other owns its home and has no installment. At identical preferences, the former has 55 less discretionary capacity, provided these payments are actually collectible. A third household also owes 55 but has zero accessible cash and no approved credit: both old and new rules may buy zero, and the obligation remains unpaid or triggers the existing eviction/credit outcome. A bank with inadequate reserves can prevent a nominal deposit balance from funding a payment. The example is not a claim about realistic rent-to-income ratios.

**Transmission, with status.** **Existing:** rent payment transfers household cash to a housing firm, and bank collection transfers borrower cash against a registered claim; seller supply, housing occupancy and bank liquidity are finite. **Proposed:** the household offers fewer discretionary goods orders before those obligations are due. **Existing:** realized seller revenue influences future production/wages; rent nonpayment can lead to loss of tenancy; missed debt service affects credit. **Hypothetical:** an increased unemployment benefit might preserve rent and reduce eviction yet leave little extra for food; a lower tax may instead free goods spending. A provider's rent hike or a higher lending cost could offset a transfer. The government's funding of a transfer and a bank's contractual claim must be reported in the same comparison, rather than attributing every observed change to household thrift.

**Shared-rule request and acceptance.** The integration owner must choose an authoritative reservation/settlement convention: does the planner merely withhold desired goods demand, or is payment capacity reserved and reconciled at the market resolver so firm sales never exceed collectible household payment? A planning-only slice is safe as a demand change but must explicitly retain the existing possible negative-cash/seller mismatch. A true guarantee requires an atomic quantity/price and payer/recipient adjustment shared with goods, rental, care, bank and subsidy owners; the household writer should not silently reorder phases or debit escrow. Field map: no new trait; read `monthly_rent`, `renting_from_firm_id`, active household-loan schedules, cash, bank deposit, bank availability and announced/expected inflow at phase 2; actual rent/loan/withdrawal writers remain their present resolvers; optional `committed_cash_plan` is a **per-tick diagnostic flow**, not a second debt balance. The bank owner should aggregate scheduled amounts for active loans **once per tick by borrower ID** and expose that bounded view to household planning, rather than scan the loan ledger separately for each household; registered medical loans enter that sum once and retain the W02 single servicing path. Update scalar/batch budget paths and cached-plan validity in performance mode. If reservation affects allocations, adjust `per_household_purchases`, seller revenue, government subsidy and ledger together. The bank and housing writers own their contracts; no assumption about their proposals is required.

Distinguishing checks: with identical cash/preferences, a known rent installment lowers planned discretionary budget exactly once; debt payoff removes that reservation next tick; no-bank/no-loan households behave as before; an unaffordable obligation remains visible as arrears, missed payment or eviction under the selected institution, not a synthetic cash top-up. Reconcile household, landlord, bank and treasury at the completed settlement boundary, including partial installments and subsidy caps. Observe planned budget at phase 2, actual rent at 6.5, registered repayment at 9.5, goods debit at 10 and balances at 11; define pre-policy cohorts by rent/registered debt and liquid resources, with entries/exits reported separately. A source claim that merely reserving cash prevents negative settlement would be falsified by the current phase order. The fallback is a **diagnostic-only obligation-pressure measure** alongside the current planner until the integration owner resolves payment priority.

## HH-P03 — Compare available jobs by expected net gain, within the existing labor market

**Priority 3; readiness: needs evidence and a modeling choice about search timing.** Applicable R03/R06–R12, M02/M06, K03/K07/K08. This is a smaller and less certain response than HH-P01: it changes job search/acceptance only where a choice exists, not hours, productivity or eligibility.

**Current source finding.** [`HouseholdAgent.plan_labor_supply`](../../../backend/agents.py) (1306–1448) uses a gross `reservation_wage`, current gross wage, gross posted offer signal, cash stress, health, unemployment duration, benefit floor and a cooldown for employed search. Medical trainees/doctors have separate restrictions; `can_work` can block search. [`Economy._run_labor_matching`](../../../backend/economy.py) (2759 onward) takes the gross reservation into fast/legacy matchers; [`HouseholdAgent.apply_labor_outcome`](../../../backend/agents.py) (2031–2124) updates wages and expectations after assignment. The `unemployment_benefit` parameter is described as unused at the start of `plan_labor_supply`, yet the unemployed market-anchor branch *does* use its value to form a benefit floor; any change must follow the executable branch, not that stale comment. The government assesses taxes only later; workers currently do not compare offers on expected take-home gain.

**Economic evidence and limit.** Chetty's [US unemployment-insurance study](https://www.nber.org/papers/w13967) finds that liquidity constraints account for a substantial share of benefit-related longer unemployment duration, so a benefit–job comparison should preserve the existing cash/health/duration pressure rather than label every delayed job “shirking.” Eissa and Liebman's [US EITC study](https://www.nber.org/papers/w5158) finds a relative participation increase for affected single women with children, but no hours response for those already working; EcoSim has neither family structure nor hours, so its magnitude and subgroup cannot be transplanted. Saez's [US tax-return bunching study](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7366) finds clear bunching at one first kink and weak evidence at most others; it argues against adding a universal sharp bracket optimization. These sources motivate sensitivity checks, not a specific search elasticity.

**Chosen simplification.** Keep one ordinary employer, existing gross firm offers, skill matching, vacancy limits, cooldown and all health/medical exclusions. When a posted offer is visible, compare its **estimated after-tax weekly amount** to the known baseline benefit and to the worker's current expected net pay; convert the existing reservation threshold back into a *gross* figure for the current matchers. Keep the same bounded lagged-tax belief as HH-P01, with no household access to contemporaneous wage percentiles or employer private plans. A one-time search/acceptance decision can vary only when a job or outside option is present. Do **not** add hours, effort, migration, tax evasion or a new preference trait. The existing reservation, skill, cash, health, unemployment duration and cooldown already provide persistent and changing differences. If HH-P01 is deferred, a simpler fallback is a diagnostic of net offer minus estimated net current pay/benefit, with no labor-rule change.

Uncalibrated example: a healthy unemployed worker with a 30 benefit, 200 cash, skill eligible for a posted 45 gross job and expected 5 withholding may accept; another with the same offer but 10 cash and longer unemployment may accept through the current desperation path despite a high previous reservation; an employed worker on cooldown cannot switch this week even if a 60 gross offer has a higher net payoff. If firms have zero vacancies or the person cannot work, tax-policy signals cannot create a hire. Conversely, a higher benefit can ease liquidity stress as well as alter the payoff from work; the net sign and duration are hypotheses.

**Transmission, with status.** **Existing:** announced tax/benefit policies alter settlement and visible benefit floor; firms post gross wages and vacancies; matching allocates actual jobs, firm production/wage costs follow, and later household wages/taxes settle. **Proposed:** the household submits a gross reservation consistent with its estimated net gain and the matchers' contract. **Hypothetical:** changed acceptance affects hiring, firm output, tax base, transfer rolls and later prices. A binding vacancy, low skill or medical restriction stops this chain. Compare the same fiscal-financing rule and support settings across arms, since transfer financing and firm demand can offset a labor-supply effect.

The household writer owns only the planner/expectation request; the integration owner owns matcher normalization and both fast/legacy implementations, firm roster consistency and tax-information timing. No new lever is needed. Log the pre-match offer signal and estimated net comparison only as optional diagnostics; do not add per-firm offer scans per household. Acceptance cases compare two eligible profiles under one offer, the cooldown/zero-vacancy block, unchanged special-medical exclusion, identical gross wage actually paid by firms and tax debited once. Falsifiers include a hire without a vacancy, a gross/net unit mismatch, a universal monotone employment change attributed to tax despite vacancy constraints, or inconsistent fast/legacy results. Observe plan at phase 2, match after phase 4 and settled wages/tax after phase 11; fix pre-policy cohorts by employment, liquidity, skill, health and benefit eligibility, and report entrants separately. Roll back the behavioral rule if supported empirical ranges or within-model sensitivity do not distinguish it from a decorative multiplier.

## Integration, measurement and decisions left open

**Common field and owner map.** `cash_balance` and `bank_deposit` are model-currency stocks: settlement owners remain household update and `BankAgent.withdraw`/deposit paths. Wages, benefits, tax, rent, goods spend and loan service are currency-per-tick flows with separate counterparties. `saving_tendency`, `spending_tendency`, `frugality`, preferences and skills persist across a policy comparison; wealth, health, employer, rent, debt, tax belief and price/wage beliefs evolve. The proposal adds at most one float per household (`lagged_expected_wage_tax_share`) plus optional ephemeral plan diagnostics. No independent household-by-firm or household-by-loan scan is justified. With H households and L active loans, the ordinary computational increment is O(H + L): one loan aggregation, one or two vectorized household arrays and existing labor plans, on top of market operations. Memory is O(H) for one persistent float, a per-borrower due-amount view and temporary arrays, with optional diagnostics off by default. Cached plans must respond at a declared cadence to policy changes and due-contract changes. No runtime or memory result is claimed.

The first matched experiment should hold initialization, owners, config, firm support capabilities, execution mode, warmup and announcement/intervention tick fixed, use a predeclared fiscal closure, and verify pre-policy state plus relevant RNG streams rather than equating equal seeds with a counterfactual. Report nominal plans, quantities and cash/deposit stocks separately; use a fixed pre-policy subgroup for each policy contrast and separately tabulate unemployment, renting, loan and liquidity transitions. Mechanism tests may use uncalibrated illustrative numbers; external claims require a chosen economy, measured data and reserved validation targets (W17). At implementation time, profile normal and performance modes at 1,000 and 10,000 households with developed private economies, interleaved matched runs and a predeclared uncertainty method. The **5% median/p95 tick-time target is cumulative for the selected package**; inconclusive timing is not a pass. No tests or benchmarks were executed in this proposal round.

**Unresolved choices for the coordinator:** whether an announced headline rate immediately changes household withholding belief; whether HH-P02 is a planning-only approximation or a shared atomic payment-priority rule; whether to defer HH-P03 until household income and fiscal closure are settled; and which first tax comparison funds or forgoes what when receipts change. The proposals cannot establish a real-world tax multiplier, optimal bracket, national forecast, or that one policy must improve GDP or wellbeing.
