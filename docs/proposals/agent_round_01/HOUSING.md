# Housing proposal: stable tenancies, visible arrears, delayed new units

**Status:** independent proposal, 2026-09-21; no implementation authorized. **Author/model:** housing writer, GPT-5.6 Sol, high reasoning. **Baseline:** HEAD and live `origin/main` `4f693890b5f133c2c6c1dcbec9b1ec91ced97b46`, plus the W01–W05 working tree identified by [`ROUND_MANIFEST.json`](ROUND_MANIFEST.json); source line references below refer to that working tree. **Rules:** [economic-agent rules v1.1](../../ECONOMIC_AGENT_RULES.md), especially R01–R12, M04–M06 and K02–K08. **Related queue:** W12 for a publicly financed support variant; W14 for bank contract conventions; W18/X03 for later housing extensions.

The rental market already moves cash between tenants and providers and limits occupancy to firm-owned units. It has no renewal rule for the rent stored when a tenant signs, immediately removes a tenant who fails a one-tick affordability check, and makes financed construction rentable as soon as money moves. Three bounded changes would make policy comparisons better reflect contract duration, payment distress, and physical delivery time. They do not add a spatial property market, household demographics, or a construction industry.

## Source-grounded starting point and interpretation

One tick is about a week (`TimeConfig.ticks_per_year = 52`; [`Economy.step`](../../../backend/economy.py#L1885)). [`HouseholdAgent.monthly_rent`](../../../backend/agents.py#L127) is explicitly commented as **paid per tick**, and both incumbent and new-tenant payments occur each rental-clearing tick ([`_clear_housing_rental_market`](../../../backend/economy.py#L5777)). All numbers below are currency **per weekly tick**, including illustrative examples; the field's name and server-facing `monthlyRent` need compatibility treatment, not an implicit multiplication by four. The household's `renting_from_firm_id`, provider `current_tenants`, and `max_rental_units` constitute the active tenancy and capacity records. The misleading `owns_housing` flag is also set for renters; `met_housing_need` is set from an active rental later in the household update ([`_batch_apply_household_updates`](../../../backend/economy.py#L1368)). Those names must not be read as tenure ownership.

The current resolver examines incumbent affordability using wage or benefit plus 25% of accessible liquidity, evicts immediately if the rent exceeds that ceiling or available liquidity, then orders unhoused households by effective income and assigns the cheapest affordable available unit. It debits household cash (after an actual bank withdrawal when needed) and credits provider cash. The posted firm `price` changes with occupancy and possible rent stabilization **after** these payments, so an asking-price change does not enter that incumbent's stored `monthly_rent`; however, the existing stored rent has no declared renewal or adjustment rule. Firm housing pricing proposes an obligation-covering price during planning ([`FirmAgent.plan_pricing`](../../../backend/agents.py#L4558)), but [`Economy.step`](../../../backend/economy.py#L1949) applies that precomputed plan in phase 9, **after** the phase-6.5 occupancy ratchet and rent cap. That later write may overwrite the cap's posted-price result. Whether intended incumbents should ever receive the updated asking price is a missing contract choice, rather than evidence that they currently do every week. `plan_pricing` also appears to sum both a mirrored `bank_loan_payment_per_tick` and active housing-loan PMTs; that possible duplicate cost signal needs an accounting audit before either pricing rule is changed ([`plan_pricing`](../../../backend/agents.py#L4568), [`_offer_housing_expansion_loans`](../../../backend/economy.py#L5257)).

Rent moves directly to firm cash, while goods-market sales rows and firm-revenue snapshots are separate paths; [`test_contracts_housing_revenue.py`](../../../backend/tests_contracts/test_contracts_housing_revenue.py) checks the direct cash transfer. [`_apply_housing_repairs`](../../../backend/economy.py#L6004) draws a weekly charge proportional to posted price times units, bounded by provider cash, and sends it through the miscellaneous pool. There is no damaged-unit stock, repair backlog, restored quality, or restoration decision in that method. Self-financed expansion immediately increments units and sends the cost to that pool ([`invest_in_unit_expansion`](../../../backend/agents.py#L5492)); phase-6.6b mortgages immediately increment units after a bank-reserve transfer, firm construction payment, DSCR/LTV tests, and a loan contract ([`_offer_housing_expansion_loans`](../../../backend/economy.py#L5174)). A separate earlier long-term bank-loan route can also add housing units immediately ([`_maybe_offer_long_term_capital_loan`](../../../backend/economy.py#L4990)). Mortgage service runs before new phase-6.6b origination, using a 52-week annual-rate conversion ([`_compute_housing_pmt`](../../../backend/economy.py#L5127), [`_service_housing_mortgage_debt`](../../../backend/economy.py#L5135)). Existing `property_tax_rate` is assessed against `units × posted weekly rent × 52`, then divided by 52 in the tax plan; it is a rent-derived proxy, not a property transaction value ([`GovernmentAgent.plan_taxes`](../../../backend/agents.py#L6991)). Current property-tax settlement exists, subject to the snapshot limitation K01.

## HOUSE-P01 — A renewal date and contract rent for each active tenancy

**Policy question and evidence.** How does a cap on rent increases protect sitting tenants, change provider receipts, and affect entrants when asking rents can differ from contract rents? Source finding: the existing `monthly_rent` is a stored contract price, whereas stabilization caps an interim provider `price` increase after matching ([`_clear_housing_rental_market`](../../../backend/economy.py#L5934), [same method](../../../backend/economy.py#L5983)); a phase-9 price write may overwrite it. The engine has no explicit renewal event, so the cap cannot be interpreted as a general limit on incumbent rent changes. Empirical support is narrower: [Diamond, McQuade and Qian's San Francisco study](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24181) finds greater address stability for treated tenants and reduced rental supply by affected landlords under that city's expansion. It does not establish that every rent cap yields those magnitudes, or that this synthetic market has San Francisco's conversion options. [Research on U.S. market rents and CPI shelter](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34113) identifies long leases and smoothed renewals as sources of slower incumbent rent adjustment; it does not supply EcoSim's contract term or price formula.

**Smallest rule.** Treat `monthly_rent` as a legacy-named **contract_rent_per_tick**, keep the provider's `price` as a new-tenant ask, and add a bounded `renewal_tick` per active tenancy. At new match, record the ask and a fixed 52-tick term as an **uncalibrated design default**, not an estimated typical lease. At renewal only, the provider proposes the next contract rent from its visible ask, occupancy, wage bill, and known debt service. Government `off/monitor/soft/strict` rent-stabilization levels apply a stated maximum increase **at renewal** for a continuing tenant; `monitor` only records the counterfactual cap. Existing `soft/strict` multipliers are per-tick bounds and cannot be reused as if they were annual caps. A shared contract must choose per-renewal caps and whether any vacant-unit ask is regulated. Before expiry, the tenant pays the locked amount; if HOUSE-P02 is not selected, the current payment-failure rule remains as the explicit fallback. A provider's general `price` still responds to vacancy and debt. No household sees its provider's future revenue, other tenants' private deposits, or next tick's realized income. The tenant knows its own contract, cash, deposit access, wage/benefit and announced policy; the provider sees its roster, asks, received rent, capacity and obligations. No new personality trait is needed: tenure age, cash, wage, provider occupancy and debt already create meaningful differences.

**Two cases and feedback.** Illustrative, uncalibrated: Tenant A has a 90/week contract and cash for four payments; Tenant B seeks a vacancy at a 110/week ask with only 80/week available. If the provider's ask rises to 110, A retains 90 until renewal and B cannot take that unit. A cap at renewal can help A remain housed; B gains nothing if there is no affordable vacancy. Provider X has spare cash and low debt, so it can absorb a slower receipt increase; Provider Y has a mortgage payment and little cash, so the same cap may delay a new unit or increase insolvency risk. **Existing**: stabilized posted-price adjustment, rent transfer, bank debt service and eventual wellbeing effect of shelter. **Proposed**: contract expiry determines when the cap affects A's paid rent and provider revenue. **Hypothetical**: lower expected net receipts reduce later investment or alter vacancy asking rents; the sign depends on competition and financing. A cap has no direct effect on a locked rent below the cap, a cash-constrained B without a unit, or a provider whose asking rent is falling. Keep supply and selection metrics separate from tenant-retention metrics; the San Francisco estimate is not a model calibration target.

**Owners and checks.** `Economy._clear_housing_rental_market` alone would write `renewal_tick`, contract rent and rental association at signing/renewal/termination; `FirmAgent.plan_pricing` retains the provider asking-price plan. The integration owner must resolve the two current ask-price writers and the possible double-counted mortgage price input before changing contract adjustment. `HouseholdAgent` holds the compact contract fields (currency per tick, integer tick), `FirmAgent` retains roster/capacity, and the bank's deposit withdrawal remains the only source of bank liquidity. Scalar firm pricing and phase-6.5 rental clearing must agree; the phase-8 household batch path must still recognize shelter from a surviving contract. Performance-mode cached consumption plans must not delay actual rent renewal or spend availability. `server.py` currently serializes `monthlyRent`; choose a versioned weekly-unit alias and warehouse/telemetry migration before public renaming. Add offered ask, realized continuing/new rent, renewal count, and cap-bind count to completed-tick evidence, with denominators and pre-policy tenant status. Acceptance examples: ask rises during a term without changing A's due amount; renewal applies the announced cap once; a new entrant sees the current ask; no unit holds two tenants; paid rent equals household debit and provider credit. Roll back to one-tick terms if the added distinction changes no chosen comparison at justified settings.

## HOUSE-P02 — Partial payment, short arrears, and a funded relief variant

**Policy question and evidence.** Does a temporary earnings interruption need to end a tenancy immediately, and what does assistance buy if cash and housing capacity are scarce? Source finding: a failed affordability gate can erase a tenancy before any payment attempt, and a failed bank withdrawal triggers immediate eviction; even a tenant with cash for the contractual amount can fail the income-plus-buffer heuristic ([`_clear_housing_rental_market`](../../../backend/economy.py#L5848)). The current metric records an affordability eviction after resetting `monthly_rent`, so its computed `metric_value` loses the original rent gap ([same method](../../../backend/economy.py#L5857)). [Corbae, Glover and Nattinger's rental-market model](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32898) studies delinquency, landlord expectations, eviction and rent support, but its welfare findings are theoretical and do not set a universal grace period. [HUD's evaluation of homelessness-prevention programs](https://www.huduser.gov/portal/sites/default/files/pdf/HPRP-report.pdf) documents actual assistance for arrears/current rent, eligibility based partly on sustaining housing after help ends, and program budget limits; it does not show that an EcoSim weekly grant of a chosen size is effective.

**Smallest rule.** On each phase-6.5 rent due, use actual cash plus only deposits the bank will release, then record payment from available cash up to due rent. The unpaid difference becomes a nonnegative tenant obligation and matching provider receivable, with `missed_rent_ticks` and a short notice/grace counter (initial candidate: two ticks, subject to scenario evidence). No interest, fees, or court process initially. A provider can retain a distressed tenant during the grace interval only under a common bounded rule based on observed partial payment, accumulated arrears, vacancy, its cash runway and debt due; a strict provider under cash pressure can decline renewal or terminate after notice, while a cash-rich provider with costly vacancy can offer a repayment schedule. No provider can collect more than current due plus recorded arrears, and no arrears are silently forgiven. A missed payment is not provider revenue or tax base. If the tenant exits or provider fails, a specified write-off reduces both sides of the claim; write-off moves no cash. The shelter flag stays true while a valid tenancy provides a usable unit, even if the current payment is partial, and becomes false on actual exit.

An **optional**, separately named arrears-relief policy arm would pay a capped amount directly from treasury to the provider against verified arrears, with the tenant obligation reduced by exactly that payment. It requires a new bounded policy/schema item and a chosen W12 fiscal closure before use: default off; candidate cash-limited envelope funded from identified revenue or a separately specified public borrowing mechanism. A cash-limited arm stops when the appropriation is exhausted and leaves unpaid arrears visible. It must not label existing negative treasury cash a new loan. Existing unemployment benefits and transfers remain unchanged, so relief is not also added to household cash. Eligibility may use an income interruption and small arrears threshold observable at the decision tick; no future job return may be assumed. Report application, approval, disbursement, denial and arrears after support distinctly.

**Two cases and feedback.** Illustrative, uncalibrated: A continuing tenant owes 100/week, can pay 65 after losing a job, and has one week of deposit access; Provider X has vacancies and enough reserves to wait, so records 35 arrears and retains the contract. Another tenant owes 100/week, has no accessible deposits and no expected near-term payment; Provider Y has a mortgage PMT and cannot cover operating cash with repeated zero receipts, so notices termination at the bounded deadline. A treasury grant of 35 can clear A's arrears only if appropriated cash remains; Y's unit can still be lost if no grant is available, and neither grant creates a unit for an already unhoused applicant. **Existing**: partial bank withdrawal is limited by reserves, tenant eviction changes roster and next wellbeing, provider cash pays mortgage, progressive taxes finance general treasury activity. **Proposed**: arrears and notice delay the housing loss; a grant is treasury debit/provider receipt/tenant-liability reduction. **Hypothetical**: retained housing may support labor search and later income; lower provider defaults may preserve stock; relief may also bid against scarce units if extended to entrants. A provider's ongoing loss or a truly unpayable rent can defeat the grace period; a tenant who can pay in full produces no arrears. Do not infer a net GDP or welfare gain from either illustration.

**Owners and checks.** Phase-6.5 `Economy` owns one due-payment settlement; `HouseholdAgent` owns arrears currency and missed-tick state, with a paired provider receivable or a compact per-tenant contract record owned by the resolver. Government relief, if selected, settles at a declared phase after eligibility is known but before provider debt service only if the shared tick contract explicitly permits it; otherwise it arrives next tick and cannot rescue same-tick debt service. `BankAgent.withdraw` and `household.bank_deposit` remain paired, while a firm mortgage miss stays a bank-credit event. Rent ledger, provider cash, tax snapshots, dashboard household record and warehouse rows need the same paid-versus-due distinction. The household consumption batch planner uses gross wages and cached plans in performance mode (K03); it must see the rent obligation in the budget path or the change could simply displace food and worsen negative cash. This is an interface request to the household and integration owners, not an assumption their proposals are adopted. Acceptance: a 65 payment yields +65 provider cash and 35 paired arrears; a second payment cannot collect the same 35 twice; exhausted treasury produces no grant; a full payment closes arrears without duplicate rent; a provider exit resolves receivable and tenancy explicitly. Observe all active tenants at pre-policy intervention, then track realized eviction, arrears and support at completed ticks; separately count new tenants and exits. The simpler fallback is a one-tick warning without partial-payment accounting, useful only if the policy question is tenancy timing rather than financing or provider losses.

## HOUSE-P03 — Financed construction commitments mature into rentable units

**Policy question and evidence.** When financing improves, how soon can housing capacity respond, and who carries debt while a unit is unavailable? Source finding: self-financed, mortgage-financed and early long-term-loan paths each add `max_rental_units` on the spending/origination tick ([`invest_in_unit_expansion`](../../../backend/agents.py#L5539), [`_offer_housing_expansion_loans`](../../../backend/economy.py#L5247), [`_maybe_offer_long_term_capital_loan`](../../../backend/economy.py#L5100)). The latter two have different loan contracts/rate conventions; this proposal does not silently merge them. [U.S. Census construction-duration tables](https://www.census.gov/construction/c30/length.html) directly distinguish construction start from completion for multifamily projects; they establish a nonzero delivery interval, not an EcoSim universal number of weeks. [Baum-Snow and Duranton's housing-supply review](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33694) documents supply constraints and alternative development models, but does not calibrate this simulator's per-unit cost or bank rule.

**Smallest rule.** After a funded expansion decision, record a bounded `units_under_construction` and `completion_tick`; increase rentable `max_rental_units` only at completion, before that tick's rental matching. Preserve each route's actual lender and construction payment, but record the project commitment once so no route pays or adds the same unit twice. Begin debt service according to the existing contract unless an explicit interest-only construction convention is separately chosen. Under the first slice, a provider may have at most one project with at most a small configured unit cap; a fixed positive delivery lag, expressed in ticks, is a scenario assumption requiring sensitivity analysis. The construction payment currently goes to `_collect_misc_revenue`, which skims 0–20% to treasury and redistributes the rest to selected households; call these modeled recipients of a **construction-spending proxy**, not real builders with material capacity. Because the model has no construction input market, the funded commitment plus completion lag is a capacity rule, not proof that labor/materials were purchased. If physical inputs are required for the selected policy comparison, stop at a separate W15 design. Keep the existing weekly repair charge as a cost proxy; adding quality decay, damaged stock or a repair workforce is later scope because the current method has no such state.

**Two cases and feedback.** Illustrative, uncalibrated: Provider X has high occupancy and cash sufficient for self-funding one unit; it pays construction cost now, receives no extra rent until completion, then can match a tenant. Provider Y has equally high occupancy but needs a bank loan; if the bank lacks lendable reserves or DSCR/LTV fails, there is no commitment or new unit. If approved, Y owes payments during the lag and may become distressed before the unit opens. **Existing**: bank reserve debit/firm receipt, construction-proxy pool and treasury skim, mortgage PMT, property-tax and rent formulas. **Proposed**: only the capacity increase and associated property-tax base wait for completion; the obligation and cash transfer remain visible immediately. **Hypothetical**: a rent cap can weaken expected coverage and delay an expansion, while a housing shortage may persist for several ticks even after financing approval. A cap has no construction effect if the provider never meets the current expansion gates; a bank rate change has no direct effect on X's fully self-financed project. Counterparties and tax effects must be measured, not stipulated to favor a regime.

**Owners and checks.** `FirmAgent` holds integer pending units and completion tick; `Economy` alone converts completed units into `max_rental_units` and updates production-capacity mirrors, then phase-6.5 matching consumes the new capacity. The integration owner maps all three creation routes, the phase-6.6 mortgage stack, early long-term-loan ledger, bank reserves/claims and phase-7 property-tax snapshot. An agreed ownership rule must prevent a project begun before rental clearing from opening that same tick. Keep project fields finite and serialized only if active; include pending versus completed units, cash paid, bank principal/interest and tax receipts in evidence. Existing phase-6.7 miscellaneous redistribution may occur before any new rent; this timing is part of the measured policy effect. Acceptance: a funded project changes bank/firm/treasury-pool balances now but leaves rentable capacity and occupancy unchanged until completion; a rejected loan changes neither; a provider cannot start unbounded projects; debt service and property tax use their specified stocks and phases; completed units enter matching once. A zero-lag fallback recovers the current abstraction and is adequate for comparisons unrelated to housing supply timing.

For a matched comparison, fix initial households, providers, vacancies, contracts, bank balances, policy timing, warmup state, random disturbances, execution mode and cadence. Record the active-tenancy cohort **immediately before** intervention, including baseline wage, accessible deposits, tenure age and provider debt/cash; keep that cohort fixed for subgroup comparisons and separately show entries, exits and transitions. At the end of each completed tick, observe contract rent, asking rent, due and paid rent, arrears, eviction reason, occupancy, affordable vacancies, pending/completed units, provider cash, loan principal/interest, property-tax receipts, support appropriation/denials and government net flow. Distinguish posted and paid price distributions; current `housing_rent_to_median_wage` is not by itself a rent-burden distribution ([`_compute_statistics`](../../../backend/economy.py#L7180)). Do not use nominal GDP or cash-only inequality as a shelter outcome without their K05 limits. An intervention should be falsifiable: if the specified path says a locked contract changes before renewal, a provider receives unpaired rent, unapproved construction adds capacity, or relief pays after its envelope is exhausted, the mechanism is wrong. If effects depend entirely on an unsupported parameter, report that sensitivity and leave the idea unselected.

Expected computation is one pass over active tenancies and housing firms plus bounded pending projects. Reuse household ID lookups and current firm/tenant indexes; the present incumbent path linearly scans housing firms per household, so an ID map would avoid adding another household-by-firm scan. Persistent state is a few scalars per tenancy and provider plus at most one small project record per provider; no historical micro-ledger or actor network call is needed. No runtime claim is made here. A later selected implementation must measure median/p95 tick time and memory at matched active counts in normal and performance modes, including phase 6.5–6.6 and the integrated package's cumulative 5% target under rules v1.1. No tests, simulations, benchmarks, installs or code changes were run for this document.

**Priority and readiness.** HOUSE-P01 is first and **ready for a bounded specification** after the contract-cap and two-price-writer choices; its simpler one-tick-term fallback may suffice if tenancy protection is not the policy question. HOUSE-P02 is second and **requires a modeling choice** for notice duration, provider response and W12-funded relief; the no-relief arrears ledger can be specified independently. HOUSE-P03 is third and **ready for a bounded specification** only after the integration owner chooses how the two bank expansion routes coexist and what a funded project means without construction inputs. All evidence supports investigating mechanisms, not importing empirical effect sizes or claiming that the resulting simulator predicts a city's rents, eviction rate or welfare. Roll back or exclude any slice that fails its accounting/resource acceptance cases, materially exceeds the cumulative runtime budget, or adds no interpretable distinction for the chosen policy comparison.
