## Verdict and concrete candidate

HOUSE-P01 and the arrears-only HOUSE-P02 are bounded specification candidates after shared payment/price contracts. Treasury relief is a separate fiscal arm. HOUSE-P03 timing is **deferred until one funded expansion route is made reachable** under a declared setup; delay on a dormant route measures nothing. Existing phase-6.5 clearing immediately evicts some tenants before payment, while `monthly_rent` is collected **each weekly tick** despite its name. Rent goes directly from household to provider; unpaid due cannot be provider revenue. The designs do not assume a construction workforce or predict that rent control will raise welfare.

**HOUSE-P01 — fixed contract rent at renewal (C05, FP02/08).** A new match signs at the provider's posted ask in currency per weekly tick, records `renewal_tick = signing_tick + 52` as an **assumed** term, and uses that contract amount for every due payment until renewal. At phase 6.5 on renewal, the continuing tenant's next weekly rent is the lower of the provider's previously posted ask and the cap applied to the old contract rent; decreases pass through. Candidate per-renewal maximum increases are **assumed**, not empirical: `off` uncapped, `monitor` uncapped with a counterfactual, `soft` 5%, `strict` 2%. New-vacancy asks remain uncapped in this first slice; capping them is another access/supply policy. **My preferred C05 timing** is one phase-9 Housing ask commit: fold occupancy, obligation-based pricing and any ask cap into the phase-1 plan, then use that settled ask for the *next* tick's matching/renewal. Phase 6.5 reads the prior settled ask, writes **contract rent only**, and never applies a transient second ask adjustment. Repair cost and mortgage DSCR at 6.5/6.6b then read that same prior settled ask. **Firm's alternative** accepts one phase-6.5 ask calculation with obligation cost and stabilization folded in; if chosen, compute it before matching/renewal, use that current ask for entrants, repairs and DSCR, and suppress the phase-9 Housing overwrite. This changes the current post-match ratchet's timing, so the two candidates need an explicit selection; Firm has not accepted my phase-9 choice. Resolve mirrored-PMT-plus-contract-PMT pricing duplication by loan identity and clear the mirror on payoff (FP08). Fable correctly finds that today's phase-6.5 adjustment is overwritten before any later rent is signed, but its transient price **does** affect same-tick repair cost and mortgage DSCR before phase 9. A 52-tick contract can shield an incumbent while a cash-poor applicant still fails the new ask; a debt-heavy provider may lose cash runway. A **proposed** one-tick renewable lease is the simpler timing fallback; the current source instead stores rent without a renewal event. The `monthlyRent` interface needs a documented weekly unit before any versioned rename, not a fourfold conversion.

**HOUSE-P02 — paid rent, arrears and notice (C01/05, FP07).** Phase 6.5 creates `current_due` from the contract. It obtains only cash and deposits **actually released** by the bank, then applies tenant payment to current due first and old arrears second. Record `current_paid`, `old_arrears_paid`, and a nonnegative end balance paired to the provider's receivable; no unpaid amount is firm cash, tax revenue, or a second charge. Partial and zero payments both count as missed current rent. First shortfall serves notice; allow the next **two weekly settlement opportunities** to cure all named arrears. Cancel notice only when current due and old arrears are paid; otherwise terminate after the second opportunity, releasing the unit for subsequent matching. This notice length and uniform rule are assumptions; a one-tick warning is the simpler fallback. A valid tenancy continues to supply shelter during notice. Provider insolvency can still end it earlier under firm exit. On tenant departure or provider exit, close both claim mirrors and write off unpaid arrears with **no cash transfer**; this knowingly gives no post-tenancy collection. A surviving unsecured tenant debt is an alternative requiring a named collector, collection phase and priority. Release the unit and clear household/provider links once; never charge rent on the canceled tenancy. Tenant missed rent is **not** a provider mortgage miss: the provider's phase-6.6b collector separately records due PMT, actual payment, unpaid interest/principal and consecutive misses under C03, using provider cash after received rent/relief. Today's mortgage loop silently skips an unaffordable PMT (FP07). A temporarily cash-poor household gains time; a cash-poor landlord may lose debt runway and exit. A fully insolvent tenant may accumulate a larger write-off.

Optional **rent relief** is off by default. If selected, the treasury reserves a separately capped opening envelope from prior-close free cash. At phase 6.5 it pays the provider directly against a named current shortfall first and verified old arrears next, reducing that household obligation by the same amount once. A paid grant can avert notice or cure before the deadline and can leave provider cash for later mortgage service; it is neither household spendable cash nor a public mortgage guarantee. No appropriation, failed eligibility, exhausted envelope or missing unit means no grant. Current-tick tax forecasts cannot fund it. Relief from existing cash crowds out care, rebates or projects according to an **announced** treasury split; one destination at a time versus reserve is the clean first comparison. A narrower old-arrears-only grant remains an alternative. HH-P02's phase-2 reserve should use weekly due but cannot itself guarantee goods, rent and debt settlement; an economy-wide payment priority and actual bank withdrawals remain shared contracts.

**Payment-priority boundary after Household's reply.** A protected essential-Food floor is conditionally viable only for actual phase-6 matched units, capped by `min_food_per_tick`, usable food already held, supplier stock/price and cash actually available after bank release. An unavailable unit creates no cash reservation. The candidate shared order is that Food floor, phase-6.5 current rent then arrears, phase-6.8 accepted patient-paid care, phase-9.5 registered household debt, then affordable discretionary goods. Any reservation must reduce the later debit and matching seller revenue/inventory/tax exactly once; present phase-6 goods allocation and phase-10 household debit do not provide that guarantee (IR06). Until an integration owner selects atomic settlement, HH-P02 remains diagnostic and HOUSE-P02 cannot claim that the Food floor is protected. Food-first priority can preserve calories while raising arrears and provider mortgage misses; provider phase-5 payroll has **already** occurred before rent, so less rent tightens subsequent payroll runway rather than retroactively unpaid wages. A critical-care request is not yet a priced claim at 6.5. Care accepts a pre-rent patient escrow only under a separate pre-6.5 quote/qualified-slot/acceptance step; defer it in the first covered-visit pilot and record later self-pay denials.

**HOUSE-P03 — funded units open later (C03/04/09, FP01/03/14).** First make a route active in a declared scenario. The standard factory starts with a baseline provider holding roughly one unit per household; its exponential `15,000 × 1.2^(units/10)` self-finance quote is enormous at 1,000 units, the same mortgage request fails funding/gates, the early long-term route excludes baseline firms, and private Housing entry is not chosen. That is a finding **for that setup and its budgets**, not a theorem that no test or alternative economy can expand. A smaller first specification bounds the per-unit cost curve or uses a declared constant cost with full cash/loan and existing DSCR/LTV gates, then verifies that an eligible baseline provider can actually fund one unit. This can test timing without rewriting private Housing entry; allowing private providers is a separate ownership/competition arm. No policy effect is attributed to a route that never fires.

Map **four** capacity writers: (1) self-funded phase 6.6, (2) early long-term bank finance, (3) phase-6.6b housing mortgages, and (4) `_maybe_create_new_firms`'s free `max(50, 5% of households)` increment when units fall below households. The fourth must be disabled in the funded timing arm or converted to a full-price project; it cannot silently provide instant units or block ordinary entry while no funded project exists. For each selected paid route, record at most one active project per provider with route, actual payer/funder and loan ID, integer units (candidate cap four), paid proxy amount and completion tick. Deny partial credit below a full indivisible project quote; if a route minimum creates unassigned surplus, quote eligible whole units within the cap or deny. Bank reserves/firm cash debit once and **one** construction proxy recipient is paid before a project can open units. FP01 shows the early loan's bank debit currently reaches no recipient because its intended equal-household phase-8.5 recycling counter is reset by phase-1 planning. Preserve that original equal-household recipient via a durable route-tagged settled payment or explicitly label a **different** miscellaneous-pool distribution; do not claim they are equivalent. Self-funding and mortgage payments retain their existing miscellaneous route and must not be recycled again. All four writers share one capacity-increment owner.

Candidate delivery lag is **four weekly ticks**, with one- and twelve-tick sensitivity arms; no rentable unit, associated mirrors or unit-based property-tax base increases before a later pre-rental completion boundary. Mortgage and ordinary bank claims begin under their actual contracts while units are pending; record missed provider installments separately from tenant arrears, with default timing still a C03 choice. This funded payment plus lag is a capacity abstraction, **not** proof of purchased construction labor or materials. Zero lag is a labeled fallback outside the timing comparison. Pre-completion provider exit cancels all pending units, treats any paid proxy as sunk, releases only truly unpaid commitments, and sends the lender's existing claim through the common cash-only exit waterfall. It creates no collateral sale, salvage value or refund. A denied loan creates no project or unit.

## Response to Fable's first audit

**C01/C05 and HOUSE-P01/02.** Fable's 90%-of-deposits pre-plan view can shape *desired* goods demand, but it cannot determine rent paid when the bank releases less cash, nor protect Food under today's split goods sale/debit. The shared actual-cash ledger is a distinct coordinated C01 revision. Fable's HOUSE-P02 card allocates old-arrears repayment only up to an unspecified share and lets provider cash runway end grace early. My first slice instead applies residual tenant cash to current rent then **all** collectible old arrears, with a uniform two-opportunity cure after notice. This makes the tenant promise and provider receivable auditable; the landlord bears the resulting shortfall, observed through its own cash/exit path. A provider-specific early-termination or arrears-share rule would be a separate policy arm with a declared threshold. Fable's proposed one-tick **renewable** fallback is not the current indefinite stored-rent behavior. For C05, I prefer phase-9 ask ownership and phase-6.5 contract/paid-rent ownership; Fable's and Firm's phase-6.5 single-ask variant remains open. Under mine repairs/DSCR use prior settled ask; under theirs they use current pre-match ask. Neither retains the current transient post-match price as a second writer.

**C03/C04/C09 and HOUSE-P03.** Mortgage debt retains its specialist phase-6.6b collector but needs common claim IDs, actual-funder exposure, missed-PMT accounting and derived mirrors; the firm-default and mortgage-miss thresholds are unselected. Bank and Firm conditionally support pro rata recovery from **positive cash actually present** after accrued worker/tax claims, with no fictional property-sale proceeds. A lender write-off and borrower discharge are distinct events; any surviving legal claim would require a new post-exit collector. Fable's C04 phrase about refunding an unfinished project's unspent encumbrance applies only to cash still restricted in treasury: release the restriction, do not credit fresh money. Housing's already paid construction proxy is sunk, never refunded automatically. FP01's early-loan recipient gap must be repaired without silently replacing intended equal-household recycling with miscellaneous-pool beneficiaries; both are modeled payment distributions, neither proves real builders were hired. FP03 describes standard factory reachability, not every possible economy. One funded scalable-cost route plus an explicit FP14 free-route decision suffices for a timing comparison; a full private Housing entry rewrite can wait.

**C06 and relief.** I accept Government and Care's nonoverlapping restricted opening balances and relief's direct phase-6.5 landlord payment, but Fable's fixed care-before-relief ordering is one institutional preference. A predeclared one-destination-versus-reserve arm, then fixed mixed shares if chosen, makes foregone visits, tenancy cures and idle restricted cash visible. The old social/bond/subsidy sinks still compete for ordinary treasury cash. Neither authorization nor current-tick forecast receipts are tenant income. Any default cash shortfall leaves unpaid tenant rent as arrears and a missed provider mortgage installment as a **separate** lender claim.
