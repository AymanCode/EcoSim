# Orchestrator audit of the income/payment contract

**Owner:** the primary orchestrator, independently reviewing source and accepting/rejecting recommendations. **Scope:** [PS3.1](PAYMENT_SEQUENCE.md) as a bounded policy-comparison pilot; not a full claim that EcoSim is economically validated. No code, simulation or benchmark was run. Fable's [audit](../../reviews/ECONOMIC_PAYMENT_SEQUENCE_FABLE_AUDIT.md) and [dispositions](PAYMENT_AUDIT_DISPOSITIONS.md) are separate evidence, not substitutes for this review.

**Judgment:** income before bills is the more coherent selected weekly convention. The old source splits employer wage debit at5 from household wage receipt at10 and goods allocation from payment. Fixing those seams requires named cash/claim owners, rather than moving one function call. I retain the actor models and cadence, but explicitly label cash-limited wages/benefits, Food-first payment, public caps and credit/payout restrictions as chosen institutions. None establishes that one tax rate or government system is better.

## Adversarial paper traces

- **Wage cut persistence:** freeze gross100 at5, pay/withhold from that record at5a, then commit contract90 to both mirrors at9. Next tick's sync reads90, while the previous paid wage stays100. A5a-only mirror update fails this trace.
- **Exit recovery:** firm has10 recoverable cash against30 worker claims. Move10 to a protected funded worker hold, write off20 once. Next5a the10 splits into net plus withholding; the removed firm cannot be debited again. This trace needs an explicit supported positive-cash scenario; default exits often recover0.
- **Rent and arrears:** a tenant pays current rent50 plus old arrears10, of which treasury relief funds5. Landlord cash and operating receipts rise60 total, not65. Cash-tax income follows the declared paid-receipt rule; generic goods units remain0 for this rent line; a valid tenancy supplies one occupied unit. The10 old claim reduction is not new housing output.
- **Paid versus promised income:** firm owes80 but has60. Current workers receive60 gross in aggregate, split into net/withholding;20 is a named payable. Contract wages need not fall merely because cash is short. Household spending uses paid net, not the80 promise.
- **Treasury restrictions:** total cash100 with B60 and A20 leaves20 free before other claims. A generic cap or a firm-seed/working-capital helper cannot read100 as spendable. A return of unused A removes its restriction; it does not make total cash120.

These traces are manual source/arithmetic reasoning, not executed tests. Later implementation must test the actual public interfaces and source routes, both cache modes, conserved cash/claims/quantities, complete comparison provenance and the cumulative performance budget.

## Accepted limitations, not hidden findings

The fixed payment priority and caps are normative pilot settings. Planned demand/request timing can lag newly paid income. Employed unpaid workers remain baseline-benefit-ineligible; protected baseline firms can prolong that state. Existing9.5 firm loans may be paid while wage arrears wait until next5a, although owners/bonuses are blocked. Deposits can be unavailable when a bank is illiquid; covered care can run out of cash or staff. Cash-only exit recovery may be negligible. The retained GDP series is a limited sales proxy. These are explicit scope limits, not measured policy effects or claims that later labor/monetary/national-account work is already solved.

**Final judgment after Fable's recheck:** the selected pilot has no unresolved payment-order/owner ambiguity identified by either audit. I adopted its final upstream-fallback clarification after checking source, while retaining my stronger wage fix and recovery-tax clock. Both Fable results are verbatim; this is my independent disposition. Remaining limits above require future implementation and economic validation, not more claims of agreement.
