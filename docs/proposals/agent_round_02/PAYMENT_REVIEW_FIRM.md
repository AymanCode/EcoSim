## Two passes, one sale book

At 5b, one actual Bank withdrawal may add to signed household cash, subject to 90% of deposits and available bank reserves. Compute the requested shortfall using signed opening cash; a negative balance is neither clamped away nor a purchasing allowance. No second care withdrawal occurs at 6.8. A purpose-bound medical loan activates only with an accepted funded visit; the existing no-bank fallback cannot create discretionary cash in PS1. Later wages, capital recycling, rebates, dividends and miscellaneous redistribution are outside the payment window.

The goods resolver should open **one tick-local supply, price and committed-sale book** before 6a, then run essential Food at 6a and residual goods at 6.95 against that same book. Current `_clear_goods_market` initializes `firm_remaining`, effective Services prices and per-firm accumulators on each call; calling it twice unchanged would replenish finite Services throughput and Food stock. Freeze each seller's effective transaction price once, including the Services operating-cost floor, and preserve original firm-ID/good-name targets and household/target order. In the first pass, iterate planned Food targets only until the lesser of planned quantity and unmet `min_food_per_tick` is **actually bought**. An empty or unaffordable target does not consume Food need; other already planned Food targets may be attempted in their original order, without inventing a supplier substitution. Unfilled planned quantities, including cheaper partial stock, remain candidates in the residual pass, where cash and supply are rechecked. Subtract first-pass accepted quantity from each target before the second pass. Reject repeated attempts against exhausted supply without counting the same unmet quantity twice; final unmet by firm/category is measured after both passes.

For every accepted line, `quantity × locked transaction price = household cash paid + actual generic subsidy paid`; transfer both cash shares to one Economy clearing account and create one payable to the named firm. The subsidy cap and treasury free cash are finite; if either share cannot be funded, shrink the continuous Food/Services quantity to what actual cash buys or reject it. Only accepted units reduce shared supply. Decrement the seller's stored inventory once at commitment and add purchased stored goods once to household inventory; Services consume this tick's `last_units_produced` throughput and create no storable inventory. At phase 10, read the committed receipt for beliefs, category spending, consumption and spoilage without repeating the purchase debit, inventory addition or service delivery. This is a deliberate replacement of the present `_batch_apply_household_updates` purchase loop, which can scale subsidized quantity and debit cash **after** seller sales were allocated. Rent keeps its direct phase-6.5 provider cash route, outside goods clearing.

At market close, combine both goods passes and care's completed visits into one `per_firm_sales` record and retain line prices for price-ceiling tax. Phase-7 firm profit/price-ceiling snapshots use committed, fully funded revenue only. At phase 9, consume each clearing payable and credit the seller exactly once through `apply_sales_and_profit`; its current inventory subtraction must be bypassed because stock was removed at commitment. The tax debit and `last_units_sold`, `last_revenue`, profit and wage-adjustment updates then occur once on **combined** sales. Phase-15 `_update_statistics` takes that same record for `last_tick_sales_units`, revenue and sell-through. Its existing `last_tick_prices` averages firms' **post-phase-9 posted prices**, used for later planning; preserve that meaning and report weighted settled transaction prices separately from the sale book. Care's phase-6.8 full posted-price receipt follows the same payable path; a denied visit creates no sale. The clearing cash balance equals outstanding firm payables before phase 9 and returns to zero after release. Firm receipts held until 9 are available for firm registered-loan collection at 9.5, but not phase-5 payroll or phase-6 costs. Only a separately approved working-capital loan can bridge that gap.

## Service upgrades and timing effects

Move the one Services upgrade consideration to combined market close. `FirmAgent.consider_service_infrastructure_upgrade` currently reads lagged `last_units_sold` and `last_sell_through_rate`, although it is called at 6.1; merely moving that call would still test yesterday's utilization. Pass **current committed Services units**, current `last_units_produced`, and once-counted final unmet demand as explicit decision inputs without writing `firm.last_units_sold` early. Advance its five-tick utilization/unmet windows once, then process any investment loan once. Underwriting continues to read prior settled `last_revenue`/EMA, plus actual bank reserves and debt; held current sales are neither firm cash nor settled revenue for credit. An approved upgrade changes future slots, never the supply book already closed for this tick. If Bank's phase-6.9 household repayments replenish reserves before upgrade disbursement, those actual receipts may pass its normal reserve/buffer gate; report changed approvals as a PS1 timing consequence.

Moving miscellaneous redistribution from 6.7 to after 6.95 removes cash that could previously buy care or service debt in the same tick. It does not withhold a firm sale payment, but it may reduce current customer purchases and later firm revenue. The no-early-firm-cash rule remains workable because the existing generic seller receives sales in phase 9 and pays production wages in phase 5 already. It can still fail when starting cash cannot cover payroll; PS1 should report such firm distress, not silently advance a payable. Goods-first Food allocation can also displace residual customers of a scarce firm. In a cash-rich case, all committed lines clear and phase-9 revenue matches gross value; in a cash-poor or empty-shelf case, only actual funded stock moves and no seller earns a claim from denied quantities.
