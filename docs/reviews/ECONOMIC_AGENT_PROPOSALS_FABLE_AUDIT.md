## 3. Prioritized findings (FP IDs)

Severity: **High** = changes what a policy comparison measures or moves money without a counterparty; **Medium** = wrong or missing rule that a proposal depends on; **Low** = precision/documentation.

**FP01 — High — Long-term capital loan principal leaves the bank and reaches no one.** New (extends IR04). `Economy._maybe_offer_long_term_capital_loan` calls `bank.originate_loan` (debits `cash_reserves`, `backend/agents.py:6013–6014`) and adds capacity, but never credits `firm.cash_balance`; the intended household credit via `capital_investment_this_tick` (`backend/economy.py:5112–5113`) is zeroed by `plan_capital_investment` (`backend/agents.py:4433`) in the same phase-1 loop iteration (`backend/economy.py:1615` then `:1673`). Firm mirrors (`bank_loan_*`) are not updated either, so wage-affordability rules ignore the debt. Live when unemployment ≥ 0.10 for non-baseline Services with sustained demand (`:5026,5038–5061`). Affects FIRM-P01, HOUSE-P03, BANK-P01/P02, GOV-P02 (route reuse). Consequence: any rate/credit comparison during high unemployment silently destroys model money proportional to loans issued. Smallest correction: credit the firm, then debit the firm and route the construction payment through `_collect_misc_revenue` (matching the mortgage route), update mirrors, and stop using the reset counter for this route.

**FP02 — High — Rent stabilization and occupancy rent adjustment never reach a paid rent.** New (IR05 is correct but understated). Phase 6.5 modifies `firm.price` (occupancy, floor, shortage, cap: `backend/economy.py:5964–5992`); phase 9 unconditionally sets `self.price = max(price_plan["price_next"], min_price)` from the phase-1 plan (`:1949–1953`; `backend/agents.py:5470`); `_apply_price_stabilization_to_plan` returns early for housing (`backend/economy.py:302–303`). New tenants are matched at `f.price` *before* the phase-3 adjustment (`:5917–5936`); incumbents keep their signed `monthly_rent`. So `rent_stabilization_level` affects only `rent_increase_limited_count`, the same-tick repair charge (`:6013`) and the same-tick DSCR test (`:5236`). Consequence: a rent-control comparison today measures nothing about rents. Correction: C05 (single ask-price owner); then HOUSE-P01's renewal cap.

**FP03 — High — Housing capacity cannot expand in the factory economy.** New. `invest_in_unit_expansion` costs `15000 × 1.2^(units/10)` (`backend/agents.py:5535–5537`); with the baseline provider holding `units = num_households` (`backend/tools/runners/run_large_simulation.py:78`) that is ≈1.25×10¹² at 1 000 units, so self-funding never happens and the loan request of the same amount fails LTV (`backend/economy.py:5222–5229`). The long-term route excludes baseline firms (`:5021`); no private Housing firm is ever created (factory `:133`; entry chooses only Food/Services when units ≥ households, `:4671–4676`); the unfunded free-expansion branch (`:4641–4653`) is dormant while units ≥ households. Consequence: HOUSE-P03 and any "financing → supply" comparison is unanswerable without first making one route live and funded; HOUSE-P01/P02 operate on a fixed single-provider stock. Correction: decide the intended cost curve (or per-unit constant cost) and whether private housing entry is allowed, before specifying delay.

**FP04 — High — Firm profit tax is flat and property tax is zero.** Already covered in principle by K01 and GOV-P01; sharpened here. `_build_firm_tax_snapshots` supplies only `firm_id`, `profit_before_tax`, `price_ceiling_tax` (`backend/economy.py:4404–4408`). `plan_taxes` reads `cash_balance` (default 0 → every firm ≤ q1 = 0 → `poor_rate` = base − U(0,0.05), `backend/agents.py:6926–6989`) and `good_category`/`property_tax_rate`/`max_rental_units`/`price` (absent → `property_taxes = {}`, `:6993–7002`). Consequence: a "profit tax rate" comparison is a flat-rate comparison with a fixed random discount; property tax revenue is exactly zero. Correction: add the fields to the snapshot; separately decide whether cash-percentile firm brackets are the intended institution (they are not a progressive *profit* tax).

**FP05 — Medium — Benefit-to-wage floor scope is narrower than FIRM-P03 states, and it dominates benefit comparisons.** New. `floor_wage = max(policy_min, 1.5 × benefit)` (`backend/agents.py:4907,5093`) is returned outright when `unemployment_short_ma > nairu` (`:5002–5004`) for non-baseline, non-healthcare firms that reach that branch — but generic Services firms exit earlier with the legal minimum only (`:4965–4980`); the benefit floor reaches Services only in the reservation-blocked raise (`:4956`). Under defaults the floor (45) exceeds the minimum wage (36). Consequence: `benefit_level` moves private Food wage offers mechanically, confounding any benefit comparison. Correction: C07 config switch, default preserving current behavior.

**FP06 — Medium — No-bank medical fallback creates money.** Known limitation in `docs/ECONOMIC_CONCRETE_PACKAGE.md`, restated because CARE-P02 depends on it. `take_medical_loan` credits household cash with no funder debit (`backend/agents.py:1965–1994`); repayments credit the treasury (`backend/economy.py:1224–1227`). Consequence: a no-bank arm A has an unfunded lender whose receipts become treasury revenue. Smallest correction: gate the fallback on `government.cash_balance ≥ amount` and debit the treasury at origination (treasury-funded, like `issue_government_backed_loan`), or exclude bank-less runs from care comparisons.

**FP07 — Medium — Skipped mortgage payments and missed firm installments have no consequence.** New (extends IR11). `_service_housing_mortgage_debt` skips the contract when `cash < pmt` with no miss counter, no accrual and no term decrement (`backend/economy.py:5152–5153,5162`); ordinary firm loans on missed payments only lose score (`:6548–6550`) and are written off only at bankruptcy (`:4566–4571`). Consequence: BANK-P01's "default/write-off rule" and HOUSE-P03's "debt service during the lag" have no existing failure semantics to build on. Correction: C03 defines `missed_payments` for every contract type and a firm default rule.

**FP08 — Medium — Mortgage PMT double-counted in housing pricing; mirror never cleared.** Verified IR05. Origination mirrors `bank_loan_payment_per_tick += pmt` (`backend/economy.py:5259–5261`), servicing sets it to the PMT sum (`:5171–5172`), and `plan_pricing` adds the mirror **and** the PMT sum (`backend/agents.py:4568–4575`). After full amortization the mirror is not zeroed (`:5171` guarded by `if firm.housing_active_loans`). Latent in the factory (FP03) but live in tests and small economies. Correction: derive cost from contracts only (C03/C05).

**FP09 — Medium — Exit drops claims without recording losses.** New beyond FIRM-P02. `_handle_firm_exits` writes off only `bank.active_loans` entries (`backend/economy.py:4566–4571`); `government_loan_remaining` (direct treasury loans) vanishes without a treasury write-off record; `housing_active_loans` are not written off at all; residual cash and inventory vanish; no owner residual. Entrants also never distribute profits (no owners), so entrant profits accumulate and are destroyed at exit. Correction: C04.

**FP10 — Medium — The "held fixed" fiscal baseline already spends cash-sensitively every tick.** Strengthens IR08. Default `social_spending="medium"` (`backend/agents.py:6333`) → `social_investment_budget=750` (`:6368`) spent `min(cash, budget)` per tick (`:7158–7162`) into the misc pool; bonds 12 % of surplus above 50 000 (`:7199–7210`); `transfer_budget` stays at the factory's 0.0 unless phase-14 `adjust_policies` runs (only with stabilizers on and LLM off, `backend/economy.py:2081–2082`; `backend/agents.py:7069–7073`), so gap-filling transfers are off in LLM-government runs. Consequence: GOV-P01's `treasury_reserve` arm is not "no spending"; recipients of induced outlays are the ≤50 misc beneficiaries (`backend/economy.py:6037`). Correction: C06 enumerates every existing sink and the manifest records which are active.

**FP11 — Low — Scalar `HouseholdAgent.plan_consumption` is not on the tick path.** New. `Economy.step` always uses `_batch_plan_consumption` (`backend/economy.py:1767–1778`); the scalar method has callers only in tests/tools (grep). HH-P01's scalar/batch unification cost is smaller than stated; the batch planner is the sole live reader. The two planners already differ (`compute_saving_rate` at `backend/agents.py:1596` vs `savings_drawdown_rate` array at `backend/economy.py:826–830`), so "identical results" in the batch docstring is not true today.

**FP12 — Low — Documentation drift confirmed.** `plan_labor_supply` docstring says the benefit is unused (`backend/agents.py:1322,1332`) but `:1388` uses it (HH-P03 correct). `step`'s phase-9.5 comment "borrowers have income before repayment" is false (`backend/economy.py:1956`, rules v1.1 already says so). HOUSING's "phase-8 batch path" should read phase 10 (IR05 correct).

**FP13 — Low — Infrastructure multiplier is uncapped.** New. `infra_multiplier_hard_cap` exists in config (`backend/config.py:640`) but has no reader; `invest_in_infrastructure` adds without bound (`backend/agents.py:7098`). GOV-P02's labeled baseline arm can drift without limit over long runs.

**FP14 — Low — A fourth, unfunded housing-unit route exists.** New. `_maybe_create_new_firms` adds `max(50, 5 % of households)` units to the first housing firm with no payment whenever `units < households`, and returns without creating a firm (`backend/economy.py:4641–4653`). ## 5. Cross-proposal conflicts and minimum shared contracts

### Conflicts found beyond the coordinator's table

1. **Two rent-affordability rules.** HH-P02 reserves rent at phase 2; phase 6.5 already applies `rent ≤ 0.3·income + 0.25·liquidity` and evicts (`backend/economy.py:5851–5857`). Without C01/C05 a household could be demand-restricted at phase 2 and still evicted at 6.5, or HOUSE-P02's partial-payment ledger could make the phase-2 reserve stale.
2. **Three writers of "what the household can spend":** HH-P01 (expected net inflow), HH-P02 (committed cash), BANK-P02 (withdrawal buffer). Each reduces or reinterprets the same 90 %-deposit liquidity term at `:849–852`.
3. **Two payer paths for one visit:** CARE-P02's appropriation and the existing sector-subsidy cap could both fire in phase 6.8 unless one is disabled per arm (FP18).
4. **Five treasury claimants without priority:** GOV-P01 rebate/project, GOV-P02 installments, CARE-P02 appropriation, HOUSE-P02 relief, plus existing subsidies/bailouts/public-works/social/bonds/gov-backed loans. `apply_sector_subsidy_payment` and `issue_government_backed_loan` both read raw `cash_balance` (`:1061–1067`; `backend/agents.py:6085`).
5. **Capital routes:** FIRM-P01 (ordinary), long-term (FP01), service infrastructure (`:5290`), mortgages, self-funded units, GOV-P02 projects — each with a different payment recipient (firm cash, misc pool, nobody, households via recycle).
6. **Loan schedule versions:** BANK-P01's amortizing contract vs entrant seed loans that compute `total_repay = seed × (1+rate)` themselves (`backend/economy.py:4733–4737,4766–4770`) and mirror it into firm fields — three places compute the same claim.
7. **Labor channels:** HH-P03's net-gain reservation and FIRM-P03's floor removal both change the accepted-wage margin; tested together they cannot be attributed.

### Minimum shared contracts (stable IDs for reviewer responses)

**C01 — Spendable-resources and obligations view.** Owner: integration (Economy). Phase: computed once at pre-plan (after healthcare enqueue, before phase 1). Fields per household (model currency/tick, ephemeral arrays, not persisted): `due_rent = monthly_rent if renting_from_firm_id else 0`; `due_installments` = bank-supplied sum of `payment_per_tick` over registered active loans by borrower, built once per tick O(L) (reuse the `_update_credit_scores` pattern); `accessible_deposits = 0.9·bank_deposit`, optionally scaled by BANK-P02's bank-wide availability factor; `planning_liquidity = max(0, cash + accessible_deposits − due_rent − due_installments)`. Readers: `_batch_plan_consumption` (cap), CARE-P01 (expected patient price relative to resources), BANK-P02 (capacity test), HOUSE-P02 (partial-payment expectation). Writers of actual cash remain the current resolvers. Rule: this view shapes *desired* demand only; no phase reorders; negative actual cash remains possible and visible. Mutual exclusion: no other proposal may subtract obligations again.

**C02 — Household policy-information contract.** Owner: household + integration. Observable at phase 2: announced `wage_tax_rate`, announced benefit level, announced coverage terms, own `last_other_income` (assessed wage tax, `backend/economy.py:1210`) and `last_wage_income`. Persistent per-household `expected_wage_tax_share ∈ [0, 0.6]` (one float), updated once after phase 10 from the settled ratio; initialized to the headline rate. One scenario constant `policy_belief_lag_ticks ∈ {0, 1}`: 0 = announced headline shifts the belief proportionally at the next plan; 1 = only after one settled paycheck. Percentiles, other households' state and future matches are not observable. HH-P03 and CARE-P01 read the same belief object.

**C03 — Loan contract convention v2.** Owner: bank. **C04 — Entry funding and exit waterfall.** Owner: integration (Economy) with bank/government sign-off. **C06 — Treasury spendable-cash and encumbrance register.** Owner: government agent (decision) + Economy (settlement). `spendable_cash = cash_balance − encumbrances − reserve_floor_for_current_purpose`; every discretionary outlay (sector subsidy cap, gov-backed loans, infra/tech/social, bonds, bailouts, public works, GOV-P01 rebate/project fund, CARE-P02 appropriation, HOUSE-P02 relief, GOV-P02 installments) draws from this one function in a declared priority order (candidate: contractual transfers already planned → subsidies/care already authorized → relief → projects → rebates → bonds). ## 6. Realism, heterogeneity, evidence and computation

**Credibly abstracted (mechanism matches the policy question with small state):** expected take-home budgeting (HH-P01); committed-cash planning (HH-P02); demand-gated one-unit investment (FIRM-P01); founder-funded entry and an exit waterfall (FIRM-P02); fixed ex-ante revenue-use rule (GOV-P01); amortizing contracts with an actual funder (BANK-P01); explicit payer with appropriation and funding-wait (CARE-P02); qualified capacity (CARE-P03 slice); contract rent with renewal (HOUSE-P01); partial payment and bounded arrears (HOUSE-P02). Each preserves the actor's role and adds ≤ 3 scalars per actor.

**Still hypotheses (no source or model determines them):** the withholding-belief estimator and lag (HH-P01); the request-probability slope and whether `price_sensitivity` (a goods elasticity with live readers at `backend/agents.py:781,982`) transfers to care (CARE-P01); the grace period and provider retention rule (HOUSE-P02); founder stake requirement (FIRM-P02); firm default threshold (C03); pass-through and lag (BANK-P03); project delay/yield (GOV-P02); any labor elasticity (HH-P03/FIRM-P03). None of the proposals presents a preferred aggregate outcome as an acceptance criterion; GOV-P01, CARE-P02 and HOUSE-P02 correctly refuse to relabel negative treasury cash as debt.

**Heterogeneity:** the proposals correctly reuse live traits (`saving_tendency`→`deposit_buffer_weeks`/`deposit_fraction` at `backend/agents.py:465–472`, `price_sensitivity`, `healthcare_request_base_chance_pct` `:489`, firm personality readers) and changing state, and add at most one persistent float (HH-P01). The two inert firm traits (IR01) should stay inert rather than be given decorative readers. Equal decisions under binding constraints are preserved in every card below.

**Evidence:** the coordinator's representative checks stand. My four additional checks: the Austrian vacancy paper reports duration–wage elasticities of about −0.04 to −0.10 and concludes wage policy explains little of vacancy-filling variation ([NBER w25118](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25118)) — supports FIRM-P03's "respond to observed hiring difficulty, weakly", observational not causal; the Federal Reserve note finds no evidence of a direct household consumption response to interest rates in a HANK-calibrated framework ([FEDS note](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/household-consumption-does-not-respond-directly-to-interest-rates.htm)) — supports BANK-P03's refusal to add a direct consumption channel, but it is a structural estimate, not a measurement transferable to a weekly toy economy; Ball & Koh document long leases and renewal smoothing as reasons market rents lead CPI shelter ([NBER w34113](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34113)) — motivates HOUSE-P01's contract/renewal split, US 2015+ setting; Corbae, Glover & Nattinger is a theoretical equilibrium model showing landlords evict when expected future rent no longer covers cost and that rent support can dominate eviction limits ([NBER w32898](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32898)) — motivates HOUSE-P02's provider-expectation rule but supplies no grace-period number. Material unverifiable claims: none of the numeric defaults (52-tick term, 2-tick grace, 10 000 founder stake, 0.4/0.15 score floors) has a source; all are design defaults requiring sensitivity runs.

**Computation (unmeasured):** every accepted slice is O(H) or O(F) arithmetic plus one O(L) aggregation per tick; new persistent state is ≤ 1 float per household (HH-P01), ≤ 2 per tenancy (HOUSE-P01/P02), ≤ 6 per loan (C03), ≤ 1 record per project (GOV-P02/HOUSE-P03). Risks: C04's founder search if implemented as a full sort per entry; C08 if provider selection is repeated per state transition; CARE-P01 if it re-evaluates all households rather than only those with due episodes. Performance mode caches consumption plans for four ticks (`backend/economy.py:1767–1778`), so HH-P01/HH-P02 policy responses arrive up to four ticks later there; that is an economic difference to record, not a bug. Acceptance evidence later: interleaved matched 1 000/10 000-household runs in both modes with a predeclared uncertainty method; report each cell as within/over/inconclusive against the cumulative 5 % target; RSS delta.

**Identification (question 8):** current arms are independently initialized with the same seed, factory ownership uses global `random` (`run_large_simulation.py:253–260`), `_collect_misc_revenue` keys randomness on the amount (`backend/economy.py:6091`), so post-policy divergence in the misc pool and in owner assignment is expected unless C10 records and pairs them; statistics precede dividends (`:2085` vs `:2097`), so household cash metrics must declare the phase; GDP is `sum(last_tick_revenue)` (`:7122`) and Gini is cash-only (`:6990`).

## 7. Design cards

Each card: state/units · decision information · trigger/phase · payer→recipient · resource gate · exhaustion/default/exit · interacting actors · discriminating check · simpler fallback · assumption needing sensitivity or user choice.

**HH-P01 — Expected take-home planning** (C01, C02). *State:* `expected_wage_tax_share` float [0,0.6] per household. *Info:* announced headline rate, own last settled tax/wage, announced benefit. *Trigger:* phase 2 batch planner: replace `wage_income` with `wage × (1 − share)` in the wage term only (`backend/economy.py:833,837`); benefit term uses the announced level; dividends unchanged. *Flows:* none new; settlement unchanged. *Gate:* budgets still capped by C01 `planning_liquidity`. *Exhaustion:* no cash → same purchases as before. *Actors:* government (headline announcement), sellers (demand). *Check:* two households identical except announced rate: lower desired budget after `policy_belief_lag_ticks`; settled tax equals the plan exactly once; zero-liquidity pair converge. *Fallback:* use last settled net ordinary income (one-week recognition lag), no belief state. *Choice:* `policy_belief_lag_ticks` 0 vs 1 (recommend 1 as default, 0 as sensitivity).

**HH-P02 — Committed-cash reserve** (C01). *State:* none persistent; per-tick `due_rent`, `due_installments`. *Trigger:* pre-plan C01 build; phase-2 cap = `planning_liquidity`. *Flows:* none new. *Gate:* deposits count only at the 0.9 accessibility used by the actual withdrawers. *Exhaustion:* if obligations exceed liquidity the plan is zero and the existing eviction/miss paths apply — no top-up. *Actors:* housing (rent due), bank (installment view), care (no reservation until an authorized visit exists in C08). *Check:* identical households with/without 40 rent differ by exactly 40 in planned budget once; payoff removes the reserve next tick; seller allocations still may exceed collectible cash (documented, unchanged). *Fallback:* diagnostic `obligation_pressure = obligations / (cash + accessible deposits)` only. *Choice:* planning-only (recommended first) vs resolver-level reservation (a shared market change, deferred).

**HH-P03 — Net-gain job comparison.** *Now:* diagnostic only — log `net_offer − net_current` using the C02 belief at phase 2 for households with a visible offer. *Later rule:* reservation_gross = max(existing reservation, (benefit + premium) / (1 − share)) using C07's declared premium; never adds hours or new traits. *Check:* no hire without vacancy; fast/legacy matchers agree; tax debited once. *Fallback:* keep current behavior. *Choice:* whether the benefit–reservation premium is part of the compared institution (C07).

**FIRM-P01 — Sellable-demand gate** (C09). *State:* none. *Info:* own `expected_sales_units`, `inventory_units`, planned workforce output, posted `price`, `pricing_operating_unit_cost`. *Rule:* `gap = expected_sales − min(inventory + feasible_output, expected_sales)` for storable goods; for Services `gap = last_tick_lost_sales_used_units` (no inventory); invest only if `gap > 0` **and** the existing VMPK/cost test passes (IR02) **and** the existing cash test passes. *Flows:* unchanged (self-finance or phase-1.5 loan → recycle). *Gate:* loan denial → no capital, no recycle. *Check:* high-margin, high-inventory Food firm does not invest; sustained lost-sales firm does; firm debit equals household recycle credit. *Fallback:* positive-gap gate only. *Choice:* Services proxy (lost sales vs sell-through ≥ 0.85, the long-term route's own test at `backend/economy.py:5039–5042`).

**FIRM-P02 — Funded entry, owned residual** (C04, C03). *State:* `owners` (exists), founder contribution amount on the firm (float), exit event record. *Trigger:* phase 13. *Flows:* founder cash → entrant cash once; loan proceeds via existing funders. *Gate:* candidate list bounded (top-k by cash from a stable hashed subset of H); if none, defer entry and keep `unmet demand` visible. *Exit:* waterfall in C04. *Actors:* bank/treasury (claims), households (founder liquidity, dividends), workers. *Check:* debit = credit; unfunded entrant has no ID; bankrupt firm's cash pays claims once; treasury loan write-off recorded. *Fallback:* record a "founder" owner with zero contribution (preserves current cash creation but labels it an opening endowment) — only acceptable as a diagnostic step. *Choice:* zero-stake owner for loan-funded entrants (recommended) vs mandatory stake.

**FIRM-P03 — Benefit wage-floor switch** (C07). *State:* config scalar. *Rule:* `floor_wage = max(policy_min, multiplier × benefit)` at both sites; default 1.5 keeps baseline replay; arm value 0. *Check:* under NAIRU-exceeding unemployment, private Food offers equal the legal minimum when the multiplier is 0 and 1.5×benefit otherwise; Services unaffected (FP05). *Fallback:* label the 1.5 link as part of the scenario. *Choice:* whether a benefit comparison should hold the multiplier at 0, 1.5, or sweep it.

**GOV-P01 — Ex-ante revenue-use rule** (C06). *State:* `allocation_rule ∈ {treasury_reserve, cash_rebate, project_fund}`, `u ∈ [0,1]`, `rebate_due_next_tick` (currency stock), `project_fund_cash` (encumbrance). *Info:* realized receipts after phase 11. *Rule:* `eligible = max(0, receipts_this_tick − existing_committed_outlays)`; allocate `u·eligible` from `spendable_cash`; rebates paid at phase 10 of the next tick to a declared eligibility set (all households, or pre-policy cash-poor cohort), visible to that tick's plan only if paid before phase 2 — recommend paying at pre-plan of the next tick so HH-P01 can see it. *Gate:* zero spendable cash → zero rebate, logged. *Actors:* households (recipients), misc beneficiaries (bonds continue unless the arm disables them), providers (project fund). *Check:* receipts credited once; rebate ≤ spendable; earmark never counted as an asset. *Fallback:* `treasury_reserve` with all existing sinks left on and reported. *Choice:* whether bonds/social spending stay on in both arms (recommend: on, identical, reported) — a user decision because it defines "the government does nothing else".

**GOV-P02 — Funded, delayed public capacity** (C06, C08, C09). *Reduced version:* a generic Services slot project: authorize `cost`, `slots`, `delay_ticks`; installments paid from encumbrance to one named Services firm as revenue only when that firm has ≥1 employee that tick; on completion `production_capacity_units += slots` (same asset as the service-infrastructure loan route, `backend/economy.py:5362–5363`); no multiplier; maintenance = slots decay unless a per-tick fee is paid from `spendable_cash`. *Exit:* provider exit cancels the project, refunds unspent encumbrance (C04). *Check:* cash without a staffed provider stays encumbered and slots flat; installment debit equals provider revenue credit; slots never exceed named capacity. *Cannot answer:* crowding-out of labor/materials, healthcare rooms. *Fallback:* aggregate delayed capacity stock with an explicit supplier payment. *Choice:* generic Services (recommended) vs healthcare (needs C08 first).

**BANK-P01 — Contract convention v2** (C03). *State:* per C03. *Trigger:* new registered loans only; phase 9.5 collection; 6.6b for mortgages. *Flows:* unchanged payer/funder. *Default:* firms K consecutive misses → write-off; households 8 (existing). *Check:* 26-tick and 104-tick loans at the same rate differ in total interest; partial payment credits one funder once; zero-rate pays exactly P; legacy contracts untouched. *Fallback:* relabel current charge as a full-term fee in displays. *Choice:* K for firms; whether missed interest accrues (recommend no accrual first).

**BANK-P02 — Cash-flow underwriting** (C01, C03). *Rule:* approve if `scheduled_payment ≤ α × max(0, settled_income_proxy − essential_outflows)` where households use `last_wage_income + last_transfer_income` and `due_rent + subsistence`, firms use `trailing_revenue_12t` and wage bill + existing installments; α a scenario parameter; then existing score/liquidity gates. *Liquidity:* static first — `lendable_cash − withdrawal_buffer` where buffer = β × trailing 4-tick actual withdrawals (one bank scalar updated at phase 11.3). *Check:* four distinct decline reasons observable; disbursement debits funder exactly; partial withdrawal reduces both sides by the actual amount. *Fallback:* payment-to-income gate only, no buffer. *Choice:* α, β and whether treasury rescue lending stays automatic (C06).

**BANK-P03 — Exogenous funding-rate path.** *Only after C03.* Scenario array of annual rates by tick; `base_interest_rate` for new quotes = path × pass-through with lag; deposit rate follows existing `update_deposit_rate`. *Check:* only post-boundary contracts change; deposit interest paid ≤ earned budget. *Cannot answer:* inflation control (no price index, no monetary system). *Choice:* whether to represent an external policy rate at all — user decision.

**CARE-P01 — Charge-sensitive nonurgent requests** (C02, C08). *Rule:* split `should_request_healthcare_service` into `draw_due_episode` (no mutation of `next_healthcare_request_tick`) and `decide_request`: if `health ≤ critical` request; else request with probability `p = 1 − s·min(1, expected_patient_price / planning_liquidity)` with `s` a scenario slope (no new trait; optional `price_sensitivity` scaling as a sensitivity arm); deferred episodes retry for up to `retry_ticks` then lapse (not healed). *Check:* same state, charge 30 vs 0 changes some noncritical requests, never the drawn episode count; critical unchanged; no duplicate queue placement. *Fallback:* price-blind requests, payer-only comparison. *Choice:* `s` and `retry_ticks`.

**CARE-P02 — Explicit payer contract** (C06, C08, C03). *State:* government `care_appropriation_per_tick`, `care_reserved`, `care_paid`, `care_unused`; per-visit `payer`. *Rule:* arm A: patient pays full posted price via cash → deposits → one registered medical loan (FP06 fixed or bank required); arm B: at service time reserve price from remaining appropriation; if exhausted, household enters `funding_wait` (stays queued, distinct counter) rather than being dropped; provider receives posted price once. *Financing:* appropriation carved from `spendable_cash` at pre-plan; the closure (reallocate from social/bonds vs raise wage tax) is a manifest field. *Check:* gov + patient = provider revenue per visit; reservations + paid ≤ appropriation; zero slots → no visit either arm. *Fallback:* label the existing sector subsidy a capped cost-share. *Choice:* closure (user), whether `funding_wait` displaces later arrivals.

**CARE-P03 — Qualified capacity; priority; workforce.** *Slice 1 (accept):* `_healthcare_effective_capacity` counts only `medical_visit_capacity() > 0` workers; unknown/unqualified IDs contribute 0 and raise a diagnostic; `CONFIG.firms.healthcare_nominal_capacity_fallback` (default False; tests set True). *Slice 2 (choice):* bands by current health vs `critical`/`urgency` thresholds, then wait age, then ID; sick-doctor exception as a named policy. *Slice 3 (defer):* training seats with a named payer. *Check:* no visits from unqualified rosters; worse bands never wait longer under equal funding. *Choice:* keep doctor exception in both arms?

**HOUSE-P01 — Contract rent and renewal** (C05). *State:* `contract_rent_per_tick`, `renewal_tick` (int). *Rule:* sign at ask; renew every `term_ticks` (52 default) at `min(ask, contract × cap(level))`; ask owned by C05. *Check:* ask rises mid-term without changing the due amount; cap applies once per renewal; entrants see the ask; paid = debit = credit. *Fallback:* one-tick term (current). *Choice:* per-renewal cap values; regulate vacant asks or not (recommend not).

**HOUSE-P02 — Partial payment and arrears** (C01, C05, C06). *State:* `rent_arrears` (currency), `missed_rent_ticks` (int) per tenancy; provider receivable = sum (derived). *Rule:* pay `min(due, cash + accessible deposits)`; shortfall → arrears; retain while `missed_rent_ticks ≤ grace` and provider `cash_runway_ticks ≥ r`; else terminate; on termination or provider exit write off both sides (no cash). Payment allocation: current rent first, then arrears up to a declared share. *Relief arm (deferred):* treasury → provider against verified arrears from `spendable_cash`. *Check:* 65 paid → +65 provider, 35 arrears; never collected twice; exhausted treasury → no grant. *Fallback:* one-tick warning without arrears accounting. *Choice:* grace length, provider rule, relief eligibility.

**HOUSE-P03 — Delayed completion** (C09; blocked by FP03). *Prerequisite:* make one route live (constant per-unit cost or a bounded curve) and decide whether private housing entry is allowed. *Then:* `units_under_construction`, `completion_tick` per provider (one project); increment `max_rental_units` at completion before matching; debt service from origination per C03; cancellation at exit per C04; property-tax base counts completed units. *Check:* funded project moves cash now, capacity later; rejected loan changes nothing. *Fallback:* zero lag (current). *Choice:* lag length; interest-only construction period.
