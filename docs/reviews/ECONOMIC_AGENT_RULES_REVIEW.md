## Findings and decisions

| Finding | Disposition | Verified correction and limits |
|---|---|---|
| AR-01 — Tax-to-consumption path | Accepted with narrower wording | K03 now states that `_batch_plan_consumption` uses gross wages, benefit level and last dividends without an explicit wage-tax input. Later cash/liquidity can transmit wage-tax effects; explicit take-home-income planning remains W08. This describes that planner, not a measured claim that every economy-wide tax effect has exactly one route. |
| AR-02 — Existing wage bands | Accepted | M06 now records contemporaneous wage percentiles including zeros, a whole-wage rate rather than marginal slices, relative thresholds/notches, and the absence of a separate wage-tax collection shortfall. Band scalers are not canonical policy levers. This is disclosure, not a bracket redesign. Fable's phrase “three flat rates” refers to schema rate controls; it must not be read as proving wage taxation itself is flat. |
| AR-03 — Healthcare support and queue | Accepted with conditions | M03 records a positive healthcare-targeted sector subsidy taking precedence, otherwise the configured fallback share (default zero); actual payments remain cash/cap-limited. An unaffordable patient without credit is removed and can re-request later. Queue scan rotation is conditional on no priority doctor leading. Zero default direct visit support does not mean that deposits/medical credit are absent or every possible configured baseline is fully patient-paid. M06 includes subsidy cash limits. |
| AR-04 — Spending redistribution | Accepted | K06 details the 0–20% miscellaneous tax credited to treasury, the net pool paid equally to a selected group growing to at most 50 households, and later distribution of inflows received after phase 6.7. Capital-investment recycling separately pays all households equally. K07 identifies the payment-amount-dependent RNG key. These are source mechanisms, not an executed estimate of their inequality effect. |
| AR-05 — Performance uncertainty | Accepted issue; different decision rule | The rules now disclose the earlier 6.5% baseline repeat variation at 1,000 households. Future checks predeclare an uncertainty method, interleave matched repeats, and report within budget, over budget or inconclusive for each relevant cell. We did not adopt the suggested example of making only 10,000 households the gate: the user's speed constraint also applies at smaller sizes. Prior W05 results remain measured point estimates with their original qualifications. |
| AR-06 — Goods target name | Accepted | M01 now distinguishes firm-ID targets from legacy exact-good-name targets. Category awareness/choice happens during household planning; clearing does not perform category-wide competition. Checked `_clear_goods_market` and `_plan_category_purchases`. |
| AR-07 — Loan borrower and CEO timing | Accepted | Phase 10 now names firms as borrowers on the direct-government-loan path and records the separate firm debit for CEO salaries. The phase-7 tax snapshot already includes CEO cost; recognition is distinguished from cash settlement. |
| AR-08 — Reference collisions/stale source links | Accepted | Working-contract references are now C-S1–C-S4, leaving R01–R12 unambiguous. Historical commit-line references in that contract were replaced with current local file/symbol references and the source index. Original archived reports retain their historical citations. |
| AR-09 — Assignment/template completeness | Accepted | The proposal template now explicitly asks for financing/revenue closure, mode/cadence/intervention boundary, metric populations/observation phase, and existing/proposed/hypothetical links. The rules include the sampled-case reviewer in the assignment table. |
| AR-10 — Ownership randomness | Accepted | The factory description and K07 now state that initial owners use module-level Python randomness, whose state the caller must initialize or restore. A config seed alone is insufficient; a new local proposal must not independently reseed the whole simulation. |

## Additional clarifications

The setup row now says that eligible bailouts/public-works authorization execute there, rather than describing only budgets. R06 also warns about stateful predicates: `HouseholdAgent.should_request_healthcare_service` changes episode state. The finite-state rule now identifies possible negative household cash after earlier payments and an unchecked ordinary purchase debit, without imposing a new clamping rule. R04's “settled once” wording was clarified to distinguish one settlement owner from legitimate partial installments.

The source index now includes the suggested subsidy, miscellaneous-pool, withdrawal and runtime `update_config` paths, plus the relevant capital-recycling/beneficiary helpers, household request/purchase methods and benchmark source files. No new empirical claim is inferred from these entries.

The optional suggestion to add examples of orchestrator escalation is deferred: the existing rule already permits routine in-scope resolution and refers materially new modeling preferences to Ayman. The optional default-zero-benefit sentence is not needed to settle this contract; scenario assignments must supply their effective configuration, including benefits and transfer budgets. Neither deferral blocks the rules.
