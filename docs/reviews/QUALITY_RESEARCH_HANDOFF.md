# Food/Services research: what changed

Both alternatives are implemented locally on `codex/food-services-research`. They keep baseline Food/Services at quality 0.1 without research, give private firms bounded starting products, and replace instant gains with paid projects. Cash trouble pauses progress; most completed projects improve quality, some fail, and further gains become harder across the scale. Research can add at most one point in any 52 weeks. The existing `instant` model remains the default while the alternatives are compared.

| Option | How it works | Observed quality after 520 weeks |
|---|---|---|
| A: `project_outcome` | $1,200 over 40 funded weeks. A 15% failure chance; successful gains shrink as quality rises. | Mean private quality 1.79–2.17 across the six named-sequence runs. |
| B: `project_cost` | Cost and duration rise with quality. A 10% failure chance; gains also shrink. | Mean private quality 1.39–1.78 across those runs. |

No new-model firm reached 9.5; the highest observed quality was 4.43. In the last 100 weeks, **64%–83% of open projects were paused**, measured as paused project-weeks divided by open project-weeks. Economy-wide progress was slower than the fully funded probe; pauses, failures and firm composition can all contribute. These settings are experimental, not empirical calibration.

The economic tradeoff is substantial. In the reference `income_first` sequence, the mean paired change over weeks 421–520 was **GDP −17.8%, Services units −25.9% and 160 fewer jobs for A**, versus **GDP −15.7%, Services units −20.5% and 133 fewer jobs for B**, relative to old behavior in 1,000-household runs. Jobs and Services units fell in all 18 new-model cases. Happiness fell in all three A seeds and two of three B seeds in the reference sequence. Research generally improved nominal GDP and Services delivery over the new scale with no research; Food units fell against that control in all six `income_late` project cases. Neither alternative consistently improved both GDP and wellbeing.

**Housing rules were unchanged, but housing outcomes changed sharply.** In one of three seeds, households meeting their housing need fell from about 993 of 1,000 to **591–803**, averaged over the last 100 weeks, under every new model and both sequences. Health and Services access fell there too. The no-research control also suffered this loss, so funded projects are not necessary for it to occur; this does not establish that projects have no effect. The starting-quality scale and removal of instant research transfers remain combined, unisolated changes.

Balances also shifted toward firms: across the 18 new-model cases, **household deposits were 13%–60% lower, active-firm cash 4%–33% higher, and Treasury cash 13%–93% lower** than matched old behavior in that window. Household cash fell in 17 of 18 cases. These are stocks, not proof of particular direct transfers or government insolvency. Outstanding wage claims were 36%–46% lower while employment also fell; that is not yet evidence of better payroll reliability.

Quality, research costs and recipient transfers all affect customers and firms through the existing household logic. That logic was left unchanged. Research payments still use the agreed abstract service route: investment tax and the existing miscellaneous government/household distribution. The old system had many quality-10 firms and much larger research-payment transfers. Less spending can conserve firm cash while reducing those receipts, but the panel does not isolate that explanation. A higher quality score also has no direct wellbeing reward in the current model.

The full configured **Linux** suite passed **1,456 tests**, with two skips, eight deselections and one expected failure. The preserved Windows run had 71 failures: 68 Unix-signal failures and three encoding failures. Independent code review passed its 149 focused cases. All **24 economy cases and 60 runtime cases** completed. The parent reran the shared case validator for all 84 cases, verified 695 worker artifact hashes before these report corrections, and separately recomputed outcome and runtime arithmetic. B met the 5% rule in the measured windows. A has not passed: its `income_late`, 1,000-household p95 interval for weeks 41–80 reaches 1.0594. Further confirmation requires a new, predeclared measurement, not selective reruns. Timing uses five repetitions of one seed; the 1,000-household windows still have queued private firms.

No model has been promoted to default or published. The selector is Python configuration, `CONFIG.firms.quality_research_model`; the town UI is unchanged. Housing/Healthcare rules, wages, customer choice, capacity rules and baseline exit rules were not edited; their outcomes can still change through other agents. GitHub Actions remain disabled. OpenWiki refresh is due because the approved CLI is unavailable. The pstack decision trail and blast-radius evidence are retained; the full parent protocol is not certified, including its missing formal architecture bakeoff.
